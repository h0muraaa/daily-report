Streaming Speech Recognition on a Microcontroller: a 65.7M-Parameter Transducer in 2 and 4 Bits on the ESP32-P4

Billal Boumedine

We run Kroko, a 65.7M-parameter streaming Zipformer2 transducer for English, on the ESP32-P4, a microcontroller with two 400 MHz RISC-V cores, a 128-bit SIMD extension (PIE) and 32 MB of PSRAM, but no neural accelerator. Without any training, the encoder's 290 matrices are stored with 2 or 4 bits per weight in Hadamard-rotated groups: the width of each matrix comes from its measured sensitivity, and each matrix is first corrected toward the original model's output, then rounded with GPTQ and a search over the group norms. The released 71 MB of int8 weights become a 26.65 MB file. Activations are int16 between the matrix products and int8 into them. A C engine with hand-written PIE kernels processes each 1.28 s chunk of audio in 0.90 s on both cores, beam search included, and its PC build computes the same bits as the chip, so accuracy is measured on whole test sets. LibriSpeech WER is 4.34% on test-clean and 10.26% on test-other, against 3.94% and 9.17% for the released model; on FLEURS and Common Voice the gap grows to 2.3 and 1.1 points. A GPU simulator shows that the integer arithmetic costs nothing and that the whole gap comes from the weights, and an ablation on all of test-clean shows that GPTQ does most of the work. We also report the port's speed history, from 51.3 s to 0.62 s of encoder time per chunk, each design step measured again in one build, an attempt to reach 16 MB that stopped at 19.63 MB, and what did not work. We did not measure energy. The inference engine, the firmware and the 2/4-bit weights are available at https://github.com/BoumedineBillal/esp32-pie-asr.

Structured Pruning of Feedback Delay Networks

Facundo Franchino, Karolina Prawda

Feedback delay networks (FDNs) are recursive filter structures used to synthesise artificial reverberation. Their feedback matrix governs both energy exchange between delay lines and the computation required at every sample, making its design central to balancing an FDN's acoustic performance and implementation efficiency. In this work, we develop an FDN-specific structured pruning method that connects these acoustic and computational concerns, allowing high-order dense orthogonal designs to be fitted to efficient implementations. The feedback matrix is replaced by stages of fixed permutations and small orthogonal blocks. We characterise connectivity growth with the network depth and derive exact conditional block updates for matrix fitting, followed by refinement against the reference FDN magnitude response. The resulting factors admit direct vectorised evaluation, with arithmetic cost and coefficient storage scaling linearly with network size for fixed block size and depth. On an Apple M2, the selected staged kernels achieve feedback-multiplication speedups over dense BLAS ranging from approximately threefold to over twentyfold across the tested network sizes, while substantially reducing coefficient storage. Objective metrics and listening tests indicate that the proposed staged approximations closely preserve the spectral and temporal character of their dense references. An analysis of processor utilisation and register reuse explains why execution time depends on more than the operation count. Finally, block size and depth provide practical control over response fidelity, storage, and real-time cost, allowing acoustic design to precede the choice of an efficient implementation.

Efficient Agentic Audio-Visual Question Answering through Toolset Pruning and Modality Consolidation

Yi Wang, Tianzi Wang, Peter Bell

Fine-grained audio-visual question answering (AVQA) requires models to resolve complex cross-modal correspondences and temporally grounding from sparsely distributed evidence. Omni-modal agents, which autonomously orchestrate perception tools to actively collect evidence, achieve strong performance, but it remains unclear whether active perception from both audio and visual modalities depends on broad specialized toolsets, especially audio or visual modality-specific tools. Such systems may also introduce long execution trajectories and substantial inference cost. We investigate whether comparable performance can be retained with a compact workflow exposing only essential perceptual capabilities. Starting from the specialized-tool OmniAgent, we conduct staged controlled experiments that prune the toolset and progressively consolidate modality-specific tools into omni-modal tools, motivating a two-tool agent comprising only Omni-modal captioning and Omni-modal QA. Across three widely used AVQA benchmarks, our compact agent achieves comparable or better accuracy than the specialized-tool agent while reducing inference cost by around 70%.

How Architecture, Typology, and Adaptation Strategies Shape ASR for Low-Resource African Languages: A Controlled Empirical Study

Mercy Muchai, Kevin Chege, Nick Mumero Mwangi 等7人

Despite recent advancements in ASR, identifying effective architectures, fine-tuning approaches, and data adaptation strategies for low-resource languages remains challenging. We present a systematic comparison across five low-resource African languages and Swahili as a mid-resource contrast, fine-tuning three models from distinct architectures: Phi-4-multimodal-instruct, Whisper-large-v3-turbo, and MMS-1B-all. We analyze data sufficiency, monolingual versus multilingual adaptation, domain fine-tuning, and language-adapter transfer. Phi-4 achieves the best overall accuracy, MMS shows comparable CER, while Whisper exhibits high CER, consistent with known hallucinations and repetition loops. Performance saturates at different data scales, offering practical data-efficiency targets, while typological complexity consistently shapes error rates. Domain fine-tuning benefits Phi-4 and Whisper but degrades MMS, revealing limitations of adapter-based adaptation. Proxy language adapters outperform training MMS adapters from scratch.

Reading-Level Verification of Kanji Words in Japanese Financial Speech Synthesis

Cheng Zhang

Few-shot voice cloning makes it cheap to produce daily Japanese financial commentary with several synthetic speakers, but kanji words with more than one reading, such as the word for "market" (shijo vs. ichiba) or "overall trend" (taisei vs. oze), are still misread. A common quality gate transcribes each synthesized sentence with automatic speech recognition (ASR), converts both the input and the transcript to kana, and regenerates on mismatch. We show that this gate is unreliable in both directions: when the audio contains a misreading, the ASR language model frequently writes the expected kanji back, hiding the error, and when the audio is correct, ordinary recognition errors trigger needless regeneration. We propose a reading-level check that scores the expected reading and its confusable alternatives with a connectionist temporal classification (CTC) hiragana recognizer and flags a word when an alternative explains the audio better. On 540 utterances from three voice-cloned production voices covering 30 financial terms, with misreadings injected under controlled conditions, the transcript-based gate detected 78.9% of misreadings at a 44.4% false-alarm rate, whereas the proposed check detected 91.7% at 5.0% (AUC 0.990). On natural kanji input, it caught a front-end error that the transcript-based gate missed for one voice, and reduced regeneration requests from 38.9% to 6.1% of sentences.

Array-Agnostic High-Order Ambisonics Encoding via Diffusion Posterior Sampling

Amit Milstein, Nir Shlezinger, Boaz Rafaely

Ambisonics provides a device-independent representation of three-dimensional sound fields; however, extracting these coefficients from practical microphone array recordings remains strongly dependent on array geometry, acoustic transfer functions, and spatial sampling resolution. This pipeline is typically divided into two distinct tasks: encoding, which entails reconstructing the physically resolvable spatial components, and upscaling, which requires generating higher-order coefficients. Model-based approaches often fail on irregular arrays due to ill-conditioned transfer matrices, whereas conventional neural encoders remain strictly tied to the specific acquisition setup seen during training. Furthermore, existing upscaling methods usually assume ideal low-order inputs, ignoring device-specific array artifacts. We present ADEUPS, a unified framework that couples an array-independent Ambisonic diffusion prior with an explicit physical acquisition model during inference, enabling zero-shot encoding and upscaling across diverse array configurations. We train our prior using an order-masking strategy and perform inference via score blending, which we demonstrate to be crucial for solving the underlying inverse problem. Through extensive numerical evaluations across various array geometries, we show that ADEUPS consistently outperforms task- and array-specific baselines. Finally, we conduct subjective listening tests to confirm the perceptual effectiveness of our approach.

EmpDPO: Enabling Emphasis Control in TTS via Direct Preference Optimization

Haonan Chen, Yunyun Wang, Megan Wei 等4人

Word-level emphasis conveys meaning that text alone cannot express and is therefore essential for a practical text-to-speech (TTS) system. In this work, we study how to add emphasis control post hoc to a pretrained TTS model that has never been trained with emphasis annotations. We present EmpDPO, a simple, efficient, and effective two-stage recipe that adds emphasis control to a multilingual zero-shot diffusion TTS model through supervised fine-tuning followed by direct preference optimization (DPO). The training signals for both stages come from an off-the-shelf multilingual emphasis detector, so that no manual emphasis annotation or task-specific reward design is needed. We find that DPO can substantially strengthen emphasis control with only a few thousand preference pairs and training steps. Objective and subjective evaluations against strong public systems show that our model produces perceptible emphasis while preserving naturalness and overall quality.

The Importance of Speech Genre for End-of-Turn Detector Training

Rini Sharon, Kadri Hacioglu, Jeena Prakash 等4人

End-of-turn (EoT) detection has been studied mostly through features, models, and modalities, while the speech genre of the training data has drawn far less attention. We isolate this factor. Using one reproducible annotation pipeline, we build a matched pair of corpora, multi-turn human-human conversational and single-turn human-machine, and train the same streaming ConvLSTM EoT detector on each. Across four test sets the conversational model reaches up to +0.45 higher F1, driven by fewer false alarms on the mid-turn pauses and hesitations that are rare in single-turn corpora. This conversational-training effect is not specific to frame-level decoding. Under a matched complete-or-not test it also appears at the utterance level, across two encoders, but only on conversational test data. Testing only on single-turn speech, as most prior work does, therefore hides the benefit of conversational training data. Against open-source baselines, our 1.8M-parameter model attains the highest F1 on every test set.

Toward Complete Speaker Anonymization: a Streaming Cascaded System for Voice Privacy and PII Removal

Stanisław Kacprzak, Thomas Thebaud, Najim Dehak

We present a cascade streaming voice privacy system, consisting of ASR, NLP, and TTS modules. By leveraging recent improvements in all three aspects of speech and language processing, we show that such a system is capable of achieving a result comparable to state-of-the-art offline voice privacy systems, while providing controllable capabilities of content protection beyond voice. With an estimated utterance-onset end-to-end latency of 717 ms, the presented system preserves speech content with 11% WER on Librispeech, while detecting PII with a 69% SLU F1 score and achieving up to 47.66% EER against a semi-informed attacker.

Towards Multilingual End-of-Turn Detection using Frozen Speech Encoders

Rini Sharon, Kadri Hacioglu, Andreas Stolcke

A spoken dialogue system must judge when the user has yielded the floor. This end-of-turn (EoT) detector is normally trained and tuned afresh for each language, which is costly to scale to new languages. We instead build one on a frozen multilingual encoder, training only a small fixed-size head. Using five CALLHOME languages, we investigate three questions. First, how well a head trained on one language transfers zero-shot to the others. Second, how much target-language adaptation is required to recover full task performance. Third, whether a single fixed decision threshold holds across languages without per-language tuning. We find that the head transfers well, even between tonal and pitch-accent languages, and that a few hours of audio recover nearly all the loss, matching in-language training. A frozen multilingual encoder with one lightweight head is a practical, language-agnostic baseline for EoT detection.

SpeakerTurn: Target-Speaker Semantic Turn Detection in Multi-Speaker Environments

Nan Li, Hetao Shan, Jintao Liang 等7人

Semantic completeness alone cannot determine when an assistant should respond in a multi-speaker environment: a completed utterance may belong to an interfering speaker, while the designated user is still speaking, acknowledging, or requesting time. We therefore study semantic turn inference conditioned on speaker identity. We propose SpeakerTurn, a framework that jointly predicts the target transcript and turn state from a speech mixture and an independent target-speaker reference. Built on CocktailASR-1, it links semantic decisions to the corresponding target content through joint supervision, distinguishing complete, incomplete, backchannel, wait, and target-absent states, with a separate interruption task. We also examine whether structured chain-of-thought (CoT) supervision improves decisions by representing speaker presence and semantic cues. On a synthetic multi-speaker test set, SpeakerTurn achieves five-state macro F1 of 0.811 and target-transcription character error rate (CER) of 4.20%, correctly resolving all ten reference-swapped interruption pairs. Joint-format transcription and native-interface recognition nevertheless behave differently, and CoT improves validation transcription without improving turn macro F1. These results show why target attribution, semantic turn discrimination, and recognition retention require separate assessment when adapting speech language models for speaker-conditioned interaction.

Ambisonics: Introducing an Accessible, Distributed Approach

Thomas A Rushton, Romain Michon, Tanguy Risset 等5人

This paper presents an accessible, distributed approach to higher order ambisonics using a network of low-cost microcontroller-based devices. Leveraging recent advances in embedded computing, networked audio, and precise time synchronization, the system offers an alternative to conventional centralized architectures that typically require expensive multichannel interfaces and high-performance digital signal processing hardware. The proposed architecture combines an audio plugin for soundfield design with a modular network of ambisonics processing units, which, exploiting the parallelisable nature of ambisonics decoding, are each responsible for a subset of loudspeaker outputs. Synchronization is achieved through the IEEE 1588 Precision Time Protocol, complemented by adaptive clock correction to maintain sub-microsecond alignment between devices. A third-order ambisonics implementation demonstrates the viability of the approach, with potential scalability to higher orders and larger arrays. Overall, this work contributes to the development of frugal, distributed solutions for immersive audio, expanding access to ambisonics-based systems for a wide range of applications.

Discovering Interpretable Acoustic Features in Autoregressive Voice-Cloning TTS Models

Anuj Diwan, David Harwath, Eunsol Choi

Voice-cloning TTS models synthesize an unseen speaker's voice from brief speech prompts, but it remains unclear which acoustic properties they represent and use. We introduce a feature-based interpretability framework for autoregressive voice-cloning TTS models, analyzing features from sparse autoencoders (SAEs), PCA, and random directions via sparse probing, steering, and quantitative auto-interpretability. We focus on Qwen3-TTS and extend probing and steering to $5$ additional models to test generalization across architectures. These models encode concepts like pitch, energy, noise, and accent, but sparse prediction and steering are effective only for some concepts. Strong probing does not always translate to steering or vice-versa. SAEs are useful for sparse probing of human-labelled concepts and novel-concept discovery, while PCA is competitive for dense probing and steering. Auto-interpretability finds $684$ automatically validated SAE features in Qwen3-TTS covering speaker clusters, speaking style, and recording conditions beyond known concepts. Overall, we perform a comprehensive interpretability study of autoregressive voice-cloning TTS models.
