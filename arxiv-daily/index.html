A Situational Speech Synthesizer for Yoruba: System Design, Phonological Rule Architecture, and Orthographic Extensions for Contour

Kola Tubosun, Adedayo Oluokun, Hafiz Adewuyi 等4人

We present TTSYoruba, a rule-based concatenative diphone speech synthesizer for Yoruba, deployed at online as part of the YorubaName.com open dictionary of Yoruba personal names. The system takes tone-marked Yoruba text as input and produces audio output by applying a hand-crafted phonological rule system to a recorded inventory of 651 diphone units spanning five tonal variants of every consonant-vowel combination in the language. We describe the phonological architecture of the system in detail, including our complete tonal file-selection logic, our treatment of the three-way nasal disambiguation problem (oral /n/, nasalized vowel, and syllabic nasal), and the derivation of contextual rising and falling tones from level-tone input. We also present, as an orthographic contribution, the adoption of the caron and circumflex, which are symbols with prior standing in Yoruba phonological transcription, as standard single-vowel contour tone markers, integrated into the TTS normalization pipeline and the WriteYoruba keyboard input tool. The system's performance was evaluated through a listener study (N=50), with detailed results on Mean Opinion Scores (MOS) presented in Section 6. Keywords: Yoruba, text-to-speech, low-resource languages, diphone synthesis, contour tones, African language NLP, rule-based synthesis

VietMed: A Dataset and Benchmark for Automatic Speech Recognition of Vietnamese in the Medical Domain

Khai Le-Duc

Due to privacy restrictions, there's a shortage of publicly available speech recognition datasets in the medical domain. In this work, we present VietMed - a Vietnamese speech recognition dataset in the medical domain comprising 16h of labeled medical speech, 1000h of unlabeled medical speech and 1200h of unlabeled general-domain speech. To our best knowledge, VietMed is by far the world's largest public medical speech recognition dataset in 7 aspects: total duration, number of speakers, diseases, recording conditions, speaker roles, unique medical terms and accents. VietMed is also by far the largest public Vietnamese speech dataset in terms of total duration. Additionally, we are the first to present a medical ASR dataset covering all ICD-10 disease groups and all accents within a country. Moreover, we release the first public large-scale pre-trained models for Vietnamese ASR, w2v2-Viet and XLSR-53-Viet, along with the first public large-scale fine-tuned models for medical ASR. Even without any medical data in unsupervised pre-training, our best pre-trained model XLSR-53-Viet generalizes very well to the medical domain by outperforming state-of-the-art XLSR-53, from 51.8% to 29.6% WER on test set (a relative reduction of more than 40%). All code, data and models are made publicly available: https://github.com/leduckhai/MultiMed/tree/master/VietMed.

SENSE: Semantic Neural Speech Synthesis from Brain Dynamics via Spatial Graph Encoding

Jisoo Park, Seonghak Lee, Hyojin Park 等4人

Reconstructing speech from non-invasive brain signals offers a promising pathway for restoring communication in individuals who are cognitively intact but unable to speak. Existing EEG-to-speech approaches formulate this task as acoustic reconstruction, optimizing waveform fidelity while ignoring whether the generated speech preserves high-level semantic content. In this work, we revisit this formulation and argue that EEG signals carry not only acoustic but also semantic information. We identify two key limitations of prior methods: (1) the neglect of spatial relationships between EEG electrodes, and (2) the failure to exploit the semantic structure of the N400 paradigm, where congruent and incongruent trials reflect distinct semantic processing. We propose SENSE(Semantic-EEG Neural Speech SynthEsis), which combines a graph-based EEG encoder over electrode geometry with EEG Semantic Conditioning (ESC), aligning EEG to a pretrained semantic space using only congruent trials. On the N400 dataset, SENSE consistently outperforms prior methods on both acoustic and semantic metrics, and model-internal channel attribution suggests distributed reliance on auditory, sensorimotor, and centro-parietal regions, consistent with known speech-perception neuroscience. In the unseen-subject setting, SENSE trained on only two subjects already surpasses the strongest baseline trained on all eighteen subjects in word error rate, and matches it on acoustic metrics with as few as eight subjects.

Towards Prosodically Informed Mizo TTS without Explicit Tone Markings

Abhijit Mohanta, Remruatpuii, Priyankoo Sarmah 等5人

This paper reports on the development of a text-to-speech (TTS) system for Mizo, a low-resource tonal language of the Tibeto-Burman family, spoken primarily in the Indian state of Mizoram. The TTS was built using only 5.18 hours of data; however, based on subjective and objective evaluations, the outputs were considered perceptually acceptable and intelligible. A baseline Tacotron2 model was built, and then, using the same data, another TTS model was built with VITS. VITS achieved significantly lower Mel-Cepstral Distortion (MCD) and significantly higher subjective ratings than Tacotron2, although the differences in DNSMOS, F0 RMSE, and F0 correlation were not statistically significant. In terms of tone synthesis, the VITS model showed numerically lower tone errors than the Tacotron2 model. The paper demonstrates that a non-autoregressive, end-to-end framework can achieve synthesis of acceptable perceptual quality and intelligibility.

ConvexLLM: Scaling Convex-Hull Speech Interfaces to General-Purpose Audio Intelligence

Ming-Hao Hsu, Shujie Liu, Yuxuan Hu 等7人

Speech and audio language models connect continuous acoustic representations to decoders pretrained primarily on text data. Most such models use MLP or Q-Former interfaces to map acoustic features into the decoder's input space, requiring the decoder to learn how to interpret the resulting representations. We investigate whether grounding these representations in the decoder's existing text embeddings can help it use acoustic information while better preserving its pretrained text capabilities. We present ConvexLLM, a general-purpose speech and audio language model whose C-Gate interface represents audio as continuous convex combinations of frozen text embeddings. ConvexLLM achieves an eleven-task VoiceBench aggregate of 75.36, the highest among the open-weight models we compare, and an MMAU Test-mini accuracy of 62.50%. Its largest margins over the baselines are on spoken knowledge and reasoning tasks, while the strongest baselines are more accurate on audio understanding and transcription. In a comparison with a 2B-parameter decoder, C-Gate achieves higher aggregate spoken-language performance than MLP and Q-Former interfaces, with text-task performance close to MLP and task-dependent tradeoffs in transcription and audio understanding. These results support convex combinations of text embeddings as a viable interface for general-purpose speech and audio language models.

PromptSep-Speaker: Generative Target Speaker Extraction via Multimodal Prompting and Semantic Alignment

Yutong Wen, Ke Chen, Prem Seetharaman 等8人

Target speaker extraction (TSE) isolates a desired speaker from a multi-speaker mixture using cues that identify the target. Prior work has explored acoustic and textual cues, such as enrollment audio and different types of speaker descriptors, but typically relies on a single cue family. Yet no single cue is both broadly available and reliably discriminative. Moreover, most TSE systems adopt mask-prediction models, which are prone to audible artifacts and generalize poorly across acoustic conditions. To address these limitations, we propose PromptSep-Speaker, a generative TSE model based on latent diffusion that unifies four interchangeable cues: speaker temporal order, transcript, enrollment audio, and speaker caption. The generative formulation delivers higher separation quality and multi-modal prompting improves applicability across scenarios. To fully exploit each cue, we further introduce a frozen WavLM representation with an auxiliary alignment loss that supervises query-mixture cross-attention without extra labeling. Across four evaluation sets and a listening study, PromptSep-Speaker achieves state-of-the-art performance over TSE and universal source separation baselines, improving separation accuracy and quality across cue modalities.

Semantic-Critical Regions for Video-to-Audio Generation

Yun Fang, Wei Guo, Zhuonan Liang 等5人

In real-world videos, sound events are often associated with localized and fine-grained visual cues, such as object motion, object-part dynamics, contact events, and interactions between objects. Existing video-to-audio (V2A) methods employ implicit conditioning based on global visual representations or explicit control derived from temporal and spatial cues. However, subtle sound-producing and sound-indicative interactions remain insufficiently captured, leaving room for more precise visual conditioning of sound-event semantics and temporal dynamics. Motivated by this observation, we refer to regions containing such sound-relevant visual evidence as Semantic-Critical Regions (SCRs) and introduce SCR-V2A, a V2A generation framework that explicitly localizes and encodes SCRs as fine-grained visual guidance for audio generation. Specifically, we first identify SCRs through sound-related visual localization. We then transform the localized regions into a control representation through adapter-based dual visual encoding, which jointly captures global visual context and fine-grained sound-relevant evidence. The representation is used to condition a downstream audio generation model. Experiments on both in-domain and out-of-domain benchmarks demonstrate that SCR-V2A achieves strong performance in distribution matching and audio quality while remaining competitive in cross-modal alignment and requiring few trainable parameters. These results highlight the value of prioritizing fine-grained, sound-relevant visual regions for V2A generation.

D-Composer: Language Modeling for Simultaneous Drum Transcription and Sound Event Decomposition

Patrick O'Reilly, Saumya Pailwan, Annie Chu 等5人

Drum beats are often conceptualized and produced as arrangements of repeated single-drum sound events or one-shots. Drum Analysis-by-Synthesis (AbS) models combine this structural assumption with reconstruction-based training to perform MIR tasks such as drum transcription, drum source separation, and one-shot extraction, offering a flexible alternative to more traditional task-specific supervision. However, leading AbS approaches struggle to generalize to a broad range of drum timbres (e.g., electronic drum kits), rely on high-quality paired audio-transcription training data, and do not model intra-class timbral variation within a single arrangement (e.g., multiple kick sounds). To overcome these limitations, we propose D-Composer, an autoregressive language modeling framework that decomposes drum recordings into labeled sound events and their onset times. In drum transcription and one-shot extraction tasks, D-Composer performs comparably to or better than a state-of-the-art AbS system and task-specific baselines while generalizing to a broader range of drum timbres. Our results demonstrate that when equipped with abundant synthetic data and compact acoustic token representations, language modeling provides a simple recipe for unifying transcription and sound event decomposition.

JAVIS: Joint Audio-Visual Interaction and Streaming for Real-Time Conversational Agents

Junseok Ahn, Jaehyun Park, Jeongsoo Choi 等5人

Human communication is inherently multimodal, conveying intent not only through linguistic and vocal cues but also through visual signals. However, existing conversational systems have mainly focused on text or speech, leaving out the visual feedback that is essential for natural interaction. In this paper, we propose JAVIS, an end-to-end streaming framework for real-time audio-visual conversational agents. Our key idea is to let the voice and the face speak from a single representation. On top of a shared LLM, a Joint Refined Head produces a unified audio-visual representation, which is fed into dedicated streaming branches for speech and face generation. This end-to-end design avoids intermediate bottlenecks, enabling real-time generation while keeping speech and face tightly synchronized. In addition, we present JAVIS-Syn, a 334-hour synthetic corpus designed to address the lack of expressive audio-visual dialogue data, covering emotionally rich multi-turn conversations and assistant-style exchanges. Experiments show that JAVIS achieves approximately 200$\times$ lower initial latency than a strong baseline, while outperforming baselines in lip synchronization and facial expression appropriateness.

From Individual Songs to Music Corpora: Evaluating Explainable Harmonic Decision-Making on HookTheory

Josef Pavl\'i\v{c}ek, Martin Pelik\'an

Automatic melody harmonization is often evaluated through individual compositions or data-driven corpus models. This study examines whether an explainable harmonic decision model developed for individual songs can be applied to independently collected human annotations without changing its rules. We evaluate a frozen Explainable Melody Harmonizer (EMH) on selected HookTheory excerpts containing aligned melody and chord annotations. EMH generates chord sequences from melody alone using explicit criteria for melodic compatibility, harmonic function, persistence, cadence, meter-aware segmentation, and Viterbi-like optimization. Human chords are withheld during generation and used only for post-hoc comparison. Nineteen excerpts yielded 439 evaluable harmonic segments. Exact chord agreement reached 20.50%, chord-root agreement 31.66%, and harmonic-function agreement 47.62%, with functions assessed relative to the key inferred by EMH. Decision-level analysis identified 203 segments (46.24%) where the human chord lay outside the model's candidate vocabulary and 146 (33.26%) where it was available but not selected. These findings distinguish representational limitations from differences in chord selection without treating the human reference as the only musically valid solution. The study demonstrates a reproducible, interpretable approach to corpus-based evaluation of harmonic decision-making.

AcousticAgent: A Floorplan-Centric Simulation Platform for Distributed Multi-Room Speech Processing

Zhiqi Ai, Han Cheng, Shiyi Mu 等5人

Existing acoustic simulation tools either generate room impulse responses (RIRs) efficiently in parameterized rooms or rely on preconstructed three-dimensional scenes, making it difficult to build large numbers of editable residential multi-room environments. We present AcousticAgent, a floorplan-centric acoustic simulation platform that converts structured floorplans into editable, acoustically connected multi-room scenes. AcousticAgent jointly models room geometry and connectivity, furniture, materials, sound sources, and microphone configurations. A scene-oriented Acoustic Materials Database maps semantic elements to acoustic parameters, while a floorplan-aware hybrid geometrical-acoustics solver generates multichannel RIRs and reverberant audio. The platform also supports directional and moving sources, distributed microphones, and VLM-assisted scene construction from floorplan images and text. Using AcousticAgent, we construct FP-RIR, a dataset covering 15,376 residential floorplans with diverse room topologies, propagation conditions, and source--receiver configurations. We validate its acoustic fidelity against real-world measurements and evaluate whole-home source localization, speech enhancement, and far-field speech processing under single-device and distributed microphone settings. The results demonstrate the effectiveness of floorplan-aware modeling for large-scale residential acoustic simulation and the benefits of distributed spatial processing in multi-room environments. AcousticAgent and FP-RIR provide scalable and configurable resources for developing and evaluating residential speech systems.

Bridge, Distill, Refine: High-Fidelity One-Step Generation for Multi-Bandwidth Speech Extension

Zhiqi Ai, Xu Shen, Mou Wang 等5人

Speech bandwidth extension (BWE) aims to recover missing spectral content from band-limited speech. However, most generative BWE methods are designed for fixed input bandwidths and rely on iterative sampling, limiting their flexibility and efficiency. We propose BDR-BWE, a three-stage generative framework for multi-bandwidth speech extension. First, to enable conditional generation from the observed band-limited spectrum, we design a cutoff-aware conditional flow bridge in the compressed complex STFT domain, focusing generation on the missing high-frequency band while preserving observed spectral information. Second, progressive consistency distillation compresses the multi-step flow transport into a one-step generator. Third, adversarial refinement further improves high-frequency detail and harmonic coherence. BDR-BWE achieves state-of-the-art log-spectral distance (LSD) on VCTK across all evaluated bandwidths with one-step inference, while maintaining competitive perceptual quality and generalizing well to EARS. Audio samples are available at https://aizhiqi-work.github.io/bdr-bwe-demo.
