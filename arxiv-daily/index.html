Towards Privacy-Preserving ASR: Speaker-Level Machine Unlearning

Seaone Ok, Seungu Han, Eungbeom Kim 等4人

Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) systems are commonly designed to transcribe linguistic content independently of speaker identity. However, speaker-specific information can persist within the latent representations of ASR models, raising privacy concerns when training data includes sensitive individuals. While machine unlearning has been studied in several speech tasks, applying speaker unlearning to ASR remains challenging due to the strong entanglement of linguistic and speaker-related information in speech representations. In this work, we conduct a layer-wise analysis of Self-Supervised Learning (SSL) based ASR representations, revealing that speaker identity is primarily encoded in intermediate encoder layers. Based on this observation, we explore a feature-level unlearning strategy that targets these identity structures. Experiments on the VCTK corpus show that the proposed approach removes the influence of specific speakers while maintaining robust transcription performance.

Unsupervised Cross-device Speaker Diarization And Linking Through Spatiotemporal Structure

Suhas BN, Nikitha K

Linking speakers across independently recorded rooms is difficult because microphone, room, and codec mismatch distort speaker embeddings. We study visitor linking without visitor enrollment or identity labels in simulated outpatient workflows. The proposed method combines per-device embedding centering, concurrency constraints, room-type-specific visit constraints, and a clustering threshold calibrated from concurrent visitors and identified staff voices. On simulated days built from VOiCES recordings, with companions, return visits, walk-ins, silent visits, and acoustic leakage, it improves B$^3$-F1 over the best label-free baseline for all 11 extractors, by 5.3-14.6 points (all 95\% intervals exclude zero). ECAPA-TDNN reaches 86.6, compared with 76.4 for the best label-free baseline and 87.7 for the best oracle-informed baseline. Gains persist across the reported workflow perturbations, but depend on removing duplicated cross-room speech: without this step, the method wins for only 2 of 11 extractors. Single-visit patients also remain vulnerable to false merges. These results support structure-aware linking while identifying preprocessing and identity-safety limitations that must be addressed before clinical deployment.

Controllable Mono-to-Stereo Upmixing using Reward-tilted Flows

Sattwik Basu, Gautam Bhattacharya, Alan Seefeldt

We consider the problem of mono-to-stereo audio upmixing: generating a high-fidelity two-channel stereo signal from a single-channel recording. The task is fundamentally ill-posed i.e., many stereo signals are consistent with a given mono recording. An effective upmixer must therefore generate a realistic, musically coherent stereo image while preserving the underlying audio content. Previous approaches have relied on signal-processing driven decorrelation, ambience extraction and supervised learning, offering limited spatial control or requiring task-specific training. In response, we propose Aura (Audio Upmixing using Reward-tilted Flows), which reformulates upmixing as a generative inverse problem using a pretrained flow prior. To enable controllable spatial imaging, we design a reward-tilted posterior sampler whose reward penalizes divergences between generated and target distributions of inter-channel cues. Composing forward operators further enables joint restoration and upmixing without retraining or fine-tuning. Objective and subjective evaluations on real-world music recordings from MUSDB18-HQ demonstrate our method's effectiveness relative to several baselines.

High-Fidelity Song Generation with Joint Discrete and Continuous Modeling

Zheqi Dai, Zhiyue Wu, Ruibin Yuan 等8人

Generating a full song from lyrics and a style description requires long-range musical planning without sacrificing vocal and instrumental detail. Low-rate discrete semantic tokens make this tractable, but quantization constrains the information available to acoustic rendering, while separately optimized stages prevent downstream generation objectives from shaping the planner. We propose joint discrete and continuous modeling for high-fidelity song generation. A VQ tokenizer supplies tokens for autoregressive planning, and a separately trained SSL-derived variational encoder supplies continuous semantic targets. Conditioned on the completed token plan and differentiable readouts of the planner's predictive states, a full-sequence flow model generates semantic features for acoustic rendering. Its flow-matching gradients reach the language model through the readouts, without differentiating through token sampling; the standard pathway keeps continuous features out of autoregressive feedback. On 192 shared prompts, its 48D configuration improves SongEval from 4.152 to 4.269 and reduces micro-PER from 20.26% to 14.47% relative to a discrete baseline, while receiving substantially higher expert listening scores. We further analyze the system through downstream semantic probes and conditioned reconstruction.

HMU-Bench: Hierarchical Music Understanding Benchmark for Omni Models

Zheqi Dai, Boyang Wang, Zhiyue Wu 等9人

Music-understanding benchmarks should identify both the evidence available to a model and the operations a correct answer requires. We introduce HMU-Bench, a bilingual benchmark with notation and audio components. ABC Q&A contains 1,029 Chinese-English question pairs across six musical domains, labeled as extraction, single-step inference, or multi-step inference. Audio Q&A contains 460 question pairs on 92 recordings, spanning five scope-difficulty configurations. All music is Suno-generated, and questions and references are human-reviewed. Reference-based evaluation of seven omni models yields high notation scores for the strongest systems, but no model averages above 1.4 on the 0-4 audio scale. Notation and audio rankings show little correlation. A transposition case study also reveals octave errors missed by LLM grading. These findings motivate separate modality reporting and event-level checks for symbolic outputs. Data, annotations, evaluator prompts, and graded responses will be released at https://github.com/stepaudiollm/hmu-bench.

The NPU-ASLP System for Music Restoration in the CCF-AATC 2026 Challenge

Zeyu Zhu, Yike Zhu, Xingchen Li 等9人

This paper presents the NPU-ASLP music restoration system developed for Track 1 of the China Computer Federation (CCF) Advanced Audio Technology Competition (AATC) 2026 Challenge. The task aims to recover clean music from mobile-device-captured recordings affected by coupled degradations from playback devices, acoustic propagation, environmental noise and recording terminals. System development is further constrained by limited and imperfectly aligned real data pairs and a strict model-complexity budget. In our system, we adopt TF-MoE as the backbone for its dual-axis modeling of cross-band and temporal dependencies, whose Top-1 routing activates a single expert per token to bound computation under the strict budget. We extend it with two task-specific adaptations: a scale-aware encoder and a complex mapping decoder. The former encodes normalized complex spectra jointly with per-band log-RMS to preserve the band-energy cues discarded by band-wise normalization. The latter directly estimates the clean complex spectrum rather than masking the input, thereby repairing coupled degradations. To address the limited, imperfectly aligned real recordings, we further build a challenge-calibrated playback--propagation--recording simulator that generates 1,572.12 h of sample-aligned data across 202 acoustic conditions. The final system contains 2.951M parameters and requires 82.659 GMACs for a 10-s input. On the official blind test, it achieved -49.067 dB SI-SNR, 1.273 LSD, 2.540 FAD, and 3.495 ViSQOL. It ranked third in LSD, FAD, and ViSQOL and received the third prize in the challenge.

LiveAnon: Improving Privacy and Utility in Streaming Speaker Anonymization with Codec Language Models

Yejin Kim, Kyungsu Kim, Seungu Han 等4人

Speaker anonymization (SA) aims to conceal speaker identity while preserving linguistic content and emotion, but existing streaming systems often lose emotion information through discrete content bottlenecks or require emotion-specific modeling. We present LiveAnon, a voice-conversion-based streaming SA system that directly conditions an autoregressive codec language model on hidden features from a pretrained streaming ASR encoder, preserving emotion without emotion-specific supervision. To reduce codec-generation latency, we introduce capped RVQ decoding, which preserves dependencies among coarse codebooks while predicting the remaining codebooks in parallel. Under VPC24, LiveAnon improves intelligibility and emotion preservation over fixed-target Stream-Voice-Anon (SVA), a strong prior streaming SA system, matches emotion-prompted SVA in emotion preservation, and achieves the strongest semi-informed privacy among streaming systems. Under VPC26, it ranks second across all metrics among the evaluated anonymization systems, demonstrating a strong privacy-utility balance.

KOMOS: Automatic Mean Opinion Score Estimation for Korean Speech

Sangchun Ha, Junwon Moon, Kyuhong Shim

As speech generation and real-time spoken dialogue systems advance, neural mean opinion score (MOS) predictors are increasingly used to evaluate speech quality. However, many existing predictors are trained primarily on English read speech and generalize inconsistently across languages and speaking styles. In particular, their reliability on Korean speech has not been adequately validated. We propose KOMOS, a Korean MOS predictor for evaluating the overall quality of both read and spontaneous speech. To train and evaluate KOMOS, we collect 5,000 ratings from 29 native Korean listeners for 1,000 utterances spanning real recordings, synthesized speech, and degraded speech. KOMOS achieves an utterance-level Spearman rank correlation (SRCC) of 0.900, compared with 0.703 for UTMOS v1 and 0.767 for UTMOS v2. The inference code is publicly available at https://github.com/upskyy/komos and the trained model at https://huggingface.co/upskyy/komos.

HE-NAC: High-Efficiency Neural Audio Coding via Hierarchical and Residual Multi-Band Quantization

Darius P\'etermann, Wootaek Lim, Seungkwon Beack 等4人

High-fidelity audio coding requires faithful reconstruction of perceptually important high-frequency components, which are often lost due to the aggressive temporal decimation and quantization of neural codecs. State-of-the-art neural audio coding methods rely on adversarial learning to hallucinate these missing components, yielding perceptually plausible but imprecise reconstructions; a limitation for high-fidelity music applications. As a remedy, we propose high-efficiency neural audio coding (HE-NAC), which provides a multi-band extension of the popular residual vector quantization (RVQ) scheme. HE-NAC introduces an effective training mechanism that harmonizes a bitrate-free blind upsampling process, residual learning to compensate it with minimal bitrate, and a multi-head decoder architecture for band-specific reconstruction. As a result, HE-NAC achieves a balanced trade-off, reliably reconstructing the core-band ($\leq$ 3 kHz) component while preserving overall sound fidelity. Subjective listening tests and objective evaluations demonstrate that HE-NAC outperforms both the DAC baseline and MPEG HE-AAC at equal or lower bitrates.

Word-Aligned Prosody Reflects Speaker Differences More Than Depression or PTSD Severity

Hsiang-Chen Yeh, Aurosweta Mahapatra, Shreeram Suresh Chandra 等5人

Word-aligned prosody is increasingly used to detect depression and posttraumatic stress disorder (PTSD), yet the stability and clinical relevance of these measurements remain unclear. We examine stability at two levels. At the word level, we ask whether a speaker produces the same word in a similar speaker-specific way across different utterance contexts. At the speaker level, we ask whether averaging prosody across many words produces consistent speaker scores across separate recordings. Drawing on principles of psychological measurement, we compare these representations in four corpora spanning acted, spontaneous, and clinical speech. Word-specific deviations show little cross-context recurrence (0.01-0.16), whereas speaker summaries are highly consistent, reaching 0.997 in ESD, 0.73-0.90 in MSP-Podcast, and 0.84-0.996 in DAIC-WOZ and PTSD-STOP. Despite this consistency, associations with depression or PTSD severity do not exceed 0.15; equivalence tests exclude effects of 0.30 in all analyses and 0.20 in DAIC-WOZ, while within-person associations with daily PTSD symptoms are near zero ($|\rho|\leq0.04$). Thus, aggregation yields stable speaker-level prosodic measures, but these measures do not detectably track depression or PTSD severity in the studied corpora. Code is available at https://github.com/jen900704/word-aligned-prosody.

SemiCodec: Bridging Speech Tokenization and High-fidelity Reconstruction with Semi-discrete Representations

Kecan Mao, Nan Xu, Zhaoyang Meng 等7人

Speech representation learning requires a discrete modeling interface while preserving high waveform fidelity. Existing approaches rarely achieve both: discrete tokens ease modeling but lose fine-grained acoustic information, while continuous latents preserve fidelity but require specialized generative objectives. We introduce SemiCodec, a novel Semi-discrete neural speech Codec with synchronized discrete tokens and continuous latents. Direct joint optimization may create a reconstruction shortcut through the continuous pathway, leaving the discrete tokens insufficiently grounded. To address this issue, we propose a Preserve-then-Ground training paradigm that first establishes a high-fidelity synthesis space and then grounds SSL-guided discrete tokens within it. Experiments show that SemiCodec outperforms fully discrete codecs in reconstruction quality and intelligibility while remaining competitive with continuous representations in acoustic fidelity and speaker preservation. Audio samples are available at https://pennyw1-se.github.io/SemiCodec-Demo-Page/

Cross-Lingual TTS for Indian Languages: A Benchmark and Inference-Time Duration Estimation Strategies

Adarsh Arigala, Arjun Gangwar, Srinivasan Umesh

Recent alignment-free Text-to-Speech (TTS) models achieve high synthesis quality by removing explicit alignment modules and simplifying training. However, they often struggle in cross-lingual scenarios due to differences in character durations across languages, leading to duration mismatch and degraded speech quality. Existing approaches typically address this issue using explicit duration predictors, increasing model complexity. Systematic evaluation of TTS performance for Indian languages in cross-lingual settings remains under-explored due to the lack of standardized evaluation benchmarks. In this work, we construct a cross-lingual evaluation benchmark covering English and nine Indian languages and propose a language-aware inference-time duration estimation strategy for alignment-free TTS that incorporates language-specific duration-per character statistics without retraining or architectural modifications. We further develop heuristic formulations that refine duration estimation based on language-dependent duration variations. Experiments on bilingual and multilingual A-DMA models demonstrate improved speech naturalness, intelligibility, and speaker similarity while reducing duration mismatch.

FAM-gate: field-aware metadata attention with prior-constrained vector gating for audio classification

Xiangyu Jing, Hongqing Liu, Liming Shi

User-generated audio collections frequently contain textual metadata such as titles, tags, and descriptions. Effectively leveraging these complementary fields requires accounting for both field het erogeneity and varying semantic relevance relative to the acoustic content. We propose FAM-Gate, a two-stage framework for metadata-enriched audio classification. In the first stage, field-aware metadata-view attention dynamically aggregates multiple metadata views using audio-guided weighting, explicit field-identity embeddings, a shared identity-initialized projection, and mean-anchored regularized aggregation. In the second stage, a prior-constrained vector gate performs bounded, dimension-wise fusion of audio and metadata representations centered around an acoustic prior. Ex periments on BSD10K, BSD35K, and FSD50K show consistent improvements in both hierarchical single-label and multi-label classification. Compared with matched random-view scalar-fusion base lines, FAM-Gate improves the hierarchical F-score by 2.53 and 5.72 percentage points on BSD10K and BSD35K, respectively, while increasing macro mAP and macro F1 by 5.07 and 10.25 percentage points on FSD50K.

RUBATO: Decoupled Clocks Allow Full-Duplex Models to Think as Fast as They Can

Yi-Jen Shih, Puyuan Peng, Haohe Liu 等5人

Recent Speech Full-Duplex (FDX) models are capable of engaging in naturalistic conversation by listening and speaking at the same time. Building on this foundation, recent architectures introduce internal thinking streams to enhance their intelligence. To guarantee these streams process successfully in real time, existing methods enforce a fixed decoding rate, which assumes a hardware profile capable of a specific latency. We argue this static approach is severely suboptimal. Because internal thinking is intrinsically asynchronous, an ideal FDX model should sustain real-time conversational flow while allowing each stream to decode at its own hardware-adaptive rate. Thinking streams must process as fast as the hardware allows to deliver accurate answers faster. To solve this, we propose RUBATO, a novel multistream positional encoding that assigns independent clocks to entirely decouple the streams. This structural isolation empowers the model to zero-shot interpolate across any inference decoding speed. It drastically simplifies dataset construction by requiring training on only two extreme decoding speeds while smoothly adapting to any intermediate rate during deployment. RUBATO demonstrates consistent robustness across five spoken reasoning tasks. On Spoken GSM8K, a baseline model collapses to 1.7% accuracy at interpolated speeds. Conversely, RUBATO-Full sustains 48.7% to 51.1% accuracy across all rates, leveraging faster hardware to achieve a 6.5x shorter time-to-answer with a constant time-to-first-response. This advantage holds on BIG-Bench Audio and amplifies significantly during complex full-duplex interruptions. Specifically, when a user interjects while the model is actively speaking and reasoning, RUBATO-Full achieves 40.0% to 45.1% accuracy, whereas the baseline fails completely.
