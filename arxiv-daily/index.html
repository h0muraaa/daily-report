Multi-Phonation Graph Learning with Self-Supervised Speech Embeddings for ALS Detection and Progression Prediction

Behrad TaghiBeyglou, Fatemeh Bagheri, Ervin Sejdic

Amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS) progressively impairs speech motor control, making acoustic analysis a promising biomarker for severity and progression estimation. We propose a subject-level graph framework that aggregates multiple phonation recordings into a unique k-nearest-neighbor graph built from pretrained SSL embeddings of 2s segments. We compare four SSL front-ends (wav2vec 2.0, HuBERT, data2vec-audio, and UniSpeech-SAT) and five graph neural networks (GCN, residual GCN, GAT, GraphSAGE, and GIN) on the SAND dataset tasks (339 participants: 205 ALS, 134 control): 5-class dysarthria severity and 4-class ALSFRS-R progression prediction. On the official validation set, the best configuration (HuBERT+GIN) achieves macro-F$_1$ of 0.73 for Task 1 and 0.69 for Task 2, outperforming SAND validation baselines (0.61 and 0.58). These results highlight the potential of combining GNNs with pretrained cross-lingual speech representations for low-resource ALS detection and progression monitoring.

DM-ASR: Diarization-aware Multi-speaker ASR with Unified Offline and Online Decoding with Large Language Models

Li Li, Ming Cheng, Weixin Zhu 等6人

Multi-speaker automatic speech recognition (ASR) aims to recognize not only what was said, but also who said it and when. Recent Speech-LLMs have shown promising results in structured multi-speaker transcription, but jointly modeling speaker attribution, temporal information, and lexical content remains challenging and often requires substantial model capacity and training data. To address this challenge, we propose DM-ASR, a Diarization-aware Multi-speaker ASR framework that reformulates multi-speaker recognition as diarization-conditioned multi-turn dialogue generation. By leveraging diarization-derived speaker-time cues as explicit structural priors, DM-ASR decomposes transcription into a sequence of conditioned queries while preserving mixed acoustic information and conversational context. We further introduce optional word-level timestamp prediction for fine-grained temporal grounding and investigate the complementary roles of diarization cues and language-model predictions. The proposed formulation also enables unified offline and causal sentence-level online decoding through flexible acoustic context and dialogue history management. Experiments on Mandarin and English benchmarks demonstrate competitive structured transcription performance with relatively compact models and training data, while effectively supporting both offline and online recognition.

AQA-TTRL: Self-Adaptation in Audio Question Answering with Test-Time Reinforcement Learning

Haoyu Zhang, Jiaxian Guo, Dong Yang 等5人

Large Audio Language Models (LALMs) exhibit strong capabilities in general audio understanding but remain static after deployment, limiting their adaptability to real-world data. Since supervised fine-tuning is costly, we propose AQA-TTRL, a novel framework for audio understanding that enables on-the-fly evolution via test-time reinforcement learning using only unlabeled test data. It generates pseudo-labels via majority voting and optimizes the model through reinforcement learning. To address the noise in self-generated labels, we introduce confidence weighting to adjust training signals. Furthermore, multiple-attempt sampling mitigates advantage collapse and stabilizes training. Across MMAU, MMAR, and MMSU, AQA-TTRL achieves significant average improvements of 4.42% for Qwen2.5-Omni 7B and 11.04% for the 3B model. Notably, the adapted 3B model outperforms direct inference of the unadapted 7B model, highlighting the effectiveness of test-time adaptation in audio understanding.

ARPEGE: Dataset and Methods to Estimate Acoustic and Geometric Parameters of Rooms from Sound

Jean-Daniel Pascal Prieto (UMRAE, MACARON), Prerak Srivastava (UMRAE) 等5人

Can one hear the walls of a room? To foster audio signal processing research around this question we release ARPEGE, a dataset containing hundreds of ambisonic spherical-array room impulse responses recorded in 6 empty rooms, annotated by the relative positions of sources, microphones and all acoustic reflectors. The dataset includes multiple room sizes, surface materials, orientations and distances, as well as recordings of human speakers and handclaps. Two state-of-the-art methods on image source localization and blind room parameter estimation are demonstrated on the data. Initial code for re-simulating the measured room impulse responses is provided, a longer term goal being to digitally twin the dataset.

Scalable Audio-LLM System for Speech MOS Evaluation: A Diagnostic Case Study and Adaptation Roadmap

Dimitrios Kokkinis, Panagiotis Kakoulidis, Nikolaos Ellinas 等13人

The continuous deployment of enterprise voice systems creates a critical operational bottleneck: evaluating the acoustic quality of synthetic speech across high-volume production updates. While human Mean Opinion Score (MOS) remains the quality gold standard, it introduces severe operational costs and turn-around delays that make continuous integration infeasible. In this work, we present a system design case study for a scalable, low-cost and offline evaluation pipeline capable of scoring large batches of synthetic speech at marginal cost. First, we use Qwen2-Audio-7B-Instruct in a zero-shot setting for granular MOS estimation, noticing perceptual blind spots and inherent biases. Then, we analyze SOMOS dataset and construct a diagnostic benchmark that operationalizes two prosodic dimensions associated with human naturalness ratings. Finally, we adapt Qwen2-Audio-7B-Instruct through a parameter-efficient finetuning (LoRA) on the task of MOS evaluation, observing improvements over base model and widely used neural baselines like UTMOS and establishing a practical blueprint for enterprise-grade speech quality assurance.

Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning of Transformer Encoders for Audio-Image-Symbolic Music Retrieval

Eliseo Fuentes-Martinez, Antonio Hidalgo-Centeno, Jorge Calvo-Zaragoza 等4人

Score-based multimodal music retrieval requires representations that are both discriminative within each modality and compatible across audio recordings, sheet music images, and symbolic scores. This paper investigates the effectiveness of parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) for adapting pretrained music encoders and examines whether modality-specific improvements translate into better multimodal representations. We compare full fine-tuning, LoRA, and QLoRA across nine pretrained audio, image, and symbolic score encoders on TriScore and the Musical Theme Dataset. Encoders are first evaluated and selected through supervised unimodal classification, using frozen-encoder linear probing as a reference. We then introduce a controlled 2x2 design crossing encoder adaptation with task-specific shared projection, comparing frozen and adapted representations before and after projection. LoRA or QLoRA is selected in 14 of 18 modality-target combinations, frequently matching or outperforming full fine-tuning while optimizing substantially fewer parameters. However, these unimodal gains do not transfer uniformly to multimodal classification. Adaptation and projection are complementary for composer classification on TriScore, largely redundant for TriScore instrument and the Musical Theme Dataset instrument and ensemble tasks, and partially interfering for composer classification on the Musical Theme Dataset. The final adapted-and-projected title representation on TriScore supports piece-level retrieval, reaching average Top-5 and Top-10 accuracies of 55.32 and 64.68, respectively. Cross-modal exclusion and neighborhood analyses further reveal strong modality-specific local structure in the projected spaces. Overall, PEFT provides an effective and computationally efficient alternative to full fine-tuning, but improved unimodal discrimination alone does not guarantee better multimodal organization.

Hierarchical Dual-Graph Retrieval for Long-form Audio Reasoning

Eunsang Lee, Taein Ju, Jiyoung Lee

Large audio language models (LALMs) struggle with long-form audio reasoning, as processing entire recordings often limits to capture query-relevant content distributed across distant segments while incurring substantial computational cost. We introduce AudioHelix, a hierarchical DualGraph framework tailored for LALMs' retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems. Unlike existing text-only or non-graph RAG approaches, AudioHelix connects related textual and acoustic evidence across temporally distant segments for multi-hop reasoning. Extensive experiments on TraceAV-Bench, MMAU-Pro, and AudioMarathon demonstrate that AudioHelix consistently outperforms full-audio processing and existing RAG methods. The project site is https://github.com/ewha-mmai/audiohelix.

From prediction error to sparse events: Biologically inspired signed-dyadic coding for Mandarin keyword spotting

Zijian Zhou, Rui Liu, Rongxiang Nie 等6人

In continuous spiking keyword spotting, temporally correlated acoustic frames can repeatedly trigger memory access and neural computation at a dense input interface, even when hidden activity is sparse. Predictive processing and sensory adaptation suggest retaining predictable input locally and responding selectively to deviations. Inspired by this mechanism, we propose Predictive Signed-Dyadic Coding (PG). PG maintains a local prediction for each input channel and transmits an event only when the residual exceeds a learned threshold. Each event carries a sign and a finite power-of-two magnitude, and the receiver reconstructs it with fixed-point shifts and additions. In Mandarin keyword spotting, INPUT-PG reaches 90.00% accuracy while reducing mean SRAM reads by 33.87% and inference cycles by 32.77%. PG provides a hardware interface for research on low-power continuous speech processing and event-based sensory systems.

From Transcription to Reformulation: Defining and Benchmarking Grounded Generative Speech Recognition

Qing Wang, Qihan Hu, Hang Lv 等12人

Speech language models have expanded automatic speech recognition (ASR) from transcription to general speech-to-text generation. Yet existing benchmarks still assume a single ground-truth transcript per utterance, an assumption that struggles when models produce readable, formatted, or structured outputs. In this work, we introduce Grounded Generative Speech Recognition (GSR), reformulating speech recognition as generating semantically grounded text instead of a single transcript. We define a grounded reformulation space that characterizes all valid textual realizations supported by the input speech, and establish a four-level hierarchy of reformulation capabilities, including literal transcription, canonical reformulation, semantic reformulation and speech-grounded structuring. Based on this formulation, we present GSR-Bench, the first comprehensive benchmark designed to evaluate generative speech recognition systems beyond exact transcript matching. GSR-Bench covers diverse scenarios including refined meeting transcription, cross-dialect translation, context-aware speech understanding, and robustness under silent or noisy acoustic conditions. To address the limitations of word-level evaluation metrics, we develop a multi-dimensional evaluation framework combining speech grounding assessment and LLM-based semantic judgment, whose reliability is validated through human preference studies. Extensive experiments across representative architectures, ranging from conventional ASR systems to end-to-end Speech-LLMs, reveal fundamental trade-offs between transcription fidelity and generative reformulation ability. Our analysis further identifies critical failure modes, including acoustic hallucination, unsupported generation, and instruction drift, providing insights into the design and evaluation of future grounded generative speech systems.

Incongruence-Aware Evaluation of Multimodal Speech Emotion Recognition in Audio Large Language Models

Fengji Jing, Guy Laban

Voice-interactive large language models process both what a speaker says and how they say it, yet the two channels do not always agree. We ask whether that disagreement degrades speech emotion recognition. Mapping text and audio into a shared valence-arousal-dominance space, we convert the distance between them into a continuous, threshold-free incongruence score with a two-component Gaussian mixture, and validate the score against human judgement. We evaluate three audiocapable models on acted, conversational and spontaneous speech (eNTERFACE'05, MELD and CMU-MOSEI). With the model held constant across modalities, incongruence raises the odds of error whenever audio is present, in every corpus, after controlling for the ground-truth emotion and for distance in the feature space. Supervised classifiers trained on the same features behave in the opposite way, which suggests that the difficulty lies in how these models combine raw evidence rather than in the signal itself. Text-only effects are unreliable: swapping only the model that reads the transcript reverses their sign on one corpus. Rebuilding the score without the emotion lexicon reproduces the central result, and among six simpler alternatives it is the only label-free score that predicts error in all three corpora. Code, data tables and prompts are released.

TAPE: Learning General Audio Effect Representations

Phillip Long, Yongyi Zang, Qihui Yang 等8人

Musicians and mixing engineers often can decipher the signal processing chain they hear on a reference recording, then adapt it to new material. We present TAPE (Transformations of Audio Parameter Embeddings), which estimates this processing from the processed reference audio alone. An encoder trained to identify processors in eight major effect families maps the reference to an embedding. A differentiable digital signal processing (DSP) chain decodes the embedding into editable parameters in one forward pass and applies them to a different unprocessed recording. Its modules are equalization, compression, waveshaping, a small residual network, and reverberation. TAPE achieves lower spectral error than DeepAFx-ST, the previous state of the art for single-step regression, and lower spectral error than per-example search over a chain approximating ST-ITO's, while running 275$\times$ faster than that search. Because the decoder outputs DSP parameters, its chain can also run causally: a C++ implementation with the trained weights processed 128-sample buffers 1.8$\times$ faster than real time, with no missed deadlines.

Apriel Voice 27B Preview: An Open Model for Speech, Reasoning, and Tool Use

Aman Tiwari, Akshay Kalkunte, Akintunde Oladipo 等31人

A capable voice assistant must do more than recognize words; it must understand requests, reason over context, sustain conversations, and act through tools. We present Apriel Voice 27B Preview, an open speech-language model that unifies these capabilities within a shared reasoning backbone while retaining text and image input modalities. Built on the post-trained Gemma 4 26B-A4B checkpoint, Apriel Voice directly processes speech and generates both responses and tool calls without requiring a separate dialogue model. We introduce a two-stage training procedure to adapt the model to speech. The first stage establishes the speech interface, while the second jointly adapts the projector and backbone through supervised fine-tuning, text replay, and source-dependent cross-entropy and forward Kullback--Leibler divergence supervision. Automatically constructed speech-conditioned dialogues provide conversational training data, while local teacher serving, overlapping teacher and student computation, and fused full-vocabulary losses enable efficient online distillation. In thinking mode, Apriel Voice achieves a score of 98.90 on Big Bench Audio, 81.13 on Speech-BFCL, and a task success rate of 65.51% on $\tau$-Voice, with a mean latency of 1.91 seconds. It also achieves 51.27% English EVA accuracy and 74.13% experience pass@1. Finally, we deploy Apriel Voice 27B Preview on a custom serving platform to support interactive voice interactions.

Youness Dkhissi (LIUM), Valentin Vielzeuf (IRT b-com), Antoine Caubri\`ere (IRT b-com) 等4人

We present our submission to the Beyond Transcription Challenge (BeTraC) on medical note generation from doctor-patient audio conversations. We propose a section-aware approach based on Qwen2.5-Omni-3B, in which a lightweight Q-Former learns SOAP section-specific queries to extract compact clinical representations from speech. In the lightweight track, our system ranks fourth and substantially outperforms the challenge baseline, achieving a Concept F1 of 0.5005 on the official test set. The organizers' analysis and our heuristic evaluation suggest improved robustness to hallucinations, and better stability under difficult audio conditions. These results show that explicitly modeling SOAP structure through section-specific queries is an effective way to improve the faithfulness and reliability of audio-based medical note generation.

VoxLock: Target-Speaker Extraction for Voice Agents Without an Enrollment Step

Arbaz Siddiqui

A voice agent's speech recognizer transcribes everyone near the microphone, including people other than the caller. I fine-tuned a public causal target-speaker extraction model (REAL-TSE, 27M parameters) on one GPU to work from a voice sample cut from the call itself, and released it as VoxLock. I score it by what a recognizer writes on real recordings. On Krisp's 265-recording benchmark it reaches 4.95% WER against 23.21% unprocessed and lets 9 background words into the transcript over 85 minutes of background-only speech. Its WER is not significantly different from Krisp's best filters and is significantly lower than the other commercial and open systems I tested. On 400 AMI meeting windows built and frozen before scoring, it cuts WER from 42.95% to 10.63%. The 8-bit model runs a live call on 77% of one CPU thread with 24 ms of algorithmic latency. Model, code and per-file scores are open.
