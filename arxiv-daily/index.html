HRIR-Former: Grid-Free Time-Domain Reconstruction of Head-Related Impulse Responses with a Spatially Encoded Transformer

Shaoheng Xu, Chunyi Sun, Jihui Zhang 等7人

Individualized head-related impulse responses (HRIRs) enable binaural rendering, but dense per-listener measurements are costly. We address HRIR spatial up-sampling from sparse per-listener measurements: given a few measured HRIRs for a listener, predict HRIRs at unmeasured target directions. Prior learning methods often work in the frequency domain, rely on minimum-phase assumptions or separate timing models, and use a fixed direction grid, which can degrade temporal fidelity and spatial continuity. We propose HRIR-Former, a time-domain, grid-free binaural Transformer for reconstructing HRIRs at arbitrary directions from sparse inputs. It uses sinusoidal spatial features, a Conv1D refinement module, and auxiliary interaural time difference (ITD) and interaural level difference (ILD) heads. On SONICOM, it improves normalized mean squared error (NMSE), cosine distance, and ITD/ILD errors over prior methods; ablations validate modules and show minimum-phase preprocessing is unnecessary.

MusicRWKV: A Multimodal RWKV Framework for Music Representation Learning

Terry Tan

Recent music foundation models have achieved remarkable progress in music representation learning, yet most existing approaches rely on Transformer architectures whose quadratic attention complexity introduces substantial computational and memory overhead when modeling long-duration music recordings. In this work, we propose MusicRWKV, a multimodal music representation learning framework built upon the RWKV architecture. MusicRWKV jointly models waveform, spectrogram, and metadata information within a unified self-supervised learning framework. To improve efficiency for long musical sequences, we introduce an invertible compression module that reduces sequence length while preserving reconstruction capability. A FiLM-based conditioning strategy is employed to facilitate modality-aware representation learning without introducing expensive cross-attention operations. We further investigate the integration of Soft ROSA as a differentiable retrieval mechanism for neural-symbolic interaction. Experimental evaluation across a diverse collection of Music Information Retrieval (MIR) benchmarks demonstrates that the proposed RWKV-based architecture can be effectively adapted to heterogeneous music understanding tasks. A controlled pretraining ablation shows that Soft ROSA improves validation performance, while layer-wise telemetry analysis reveals a progressive transition from distributed retrieval to selective retrieval in deeper network layers. These results suggest that RWKV provides a promising architectural alternative to Transformer-based models for multimodal music understanding.

Human-Likeness and Conversational Preference Diverge for Synthesized Agent Speech in Customer-Service Calls

Jeffrey M. Girard, Antony D'Avirro, Jason Z. Zheng 等5人

Listeners can judge an agent's speech on how human it sounds (human-likeness) and on how much they would want to talk to it (conversational preference). Listening tests rarely ask about conversational preference, so the relation between the two judgments is unclear. We compared them in paired-choice tests on 120 turns that actors improvised as customer-service representatives. On each trial, two agents spoke the same turn. In Study 1, both agents came from six commercial text-to-speech systems, each with eight voices. In Study 2, one was the actor's original recording and the other a system. Some systems ranked higher on human-likeness, and others on conversational preference. Listeners chose systems over actors in 19% of human-likeness trials but 36% of conversational preference trials (and 49% for the most preferred system). On both judgments, variation among a system's voices was about three-quarters of that between systems. Of three automatic naturalness scores, two predicted lower human-likeness, and none predicted conversational preference. In this setting, listeners tended to favor faster speech and fewer pauses between clauses on both judgments, and more pitch variation for conversational preference. Thus, measuring only human-likeness or testing one voice per system can misjudge which agents listeners would rather talk to.

RSE-TTS: Compact Text-to-Speech through Shared Text-Aware Reference Encoding and Generation

Ming Ma, Mingjie Chen, Yuanzhong Zheng 等11人

Can a speech generator also serve as an effective voice encoder? We show that a pretrained autoregressive text-to-speech (TTS) backbone can be repurposed for reference compression, improving speaker similarity in subsequent synthesis. Jointly encoding reference speech and its transcript further strengthens this capability. Controlled ablations and activation interventions trace the text benefit through intermediate acoustic representations into the compressed vectors that condition generation. Building on these findings, we introduce RSE-TTS, whose Reconstructive Speaker Encoder (RSE) shares the generation backbone and compresses paired reference text and speech into eight continuous vectors. Sharing reduces encoder-generator parameters by 48% relative to independent pretrained encoding. Large-scale English training yields a 118M-parameter generator that improves both word error rate and speaker similarity over the corresponding official MOSS-TTS-Nano cloning modes on all three English benchmarks. Combined with acoustic continuation, RSE-TTS approaches the speaker similarity of substantially larger systems while achieving lower WER than the evaluated CosyVoice 3 and MOSS-TTS-Local models. Audio samples are available at https://voxnexusai.github.io/RSE-TTS/.

Hy ASR 3.0 Technical Report

Aoxiong Yin, Auden Li, Chao Huang 等63人

We present Hy ASR 3.0, a large language model (LLM)-based automatic speech recognition (ASR) system that delivers strong recognition accuracy, supports flexible contextual conditioning, and maintains robust recognition performance across diverse speech conditions. Hy ASR 3.0 supports Mandarin Chinese, English and 13 Chinese dialect groups, including Cantonese. Among the evaluated systems, it achieves the lowest aggregate error rates on public Mandarin Chinese, English, and Cantonese benchmarks and the lowest error rates on the corresponding internal test sets, alongside first-tier dialect recognition performance. Beyond audio-only transcription, Hy ASR 3.0 supports multiple forms of external context, including hotword lists, question--answer dialogues, and summaries, enabling the use of task-relevant information during recognition. The model also leverages intra-utterance context to disambiguate homophonic and phonetically similar keywords using information from other parts of the same utterance, without requiring a separate contextual input. Evaluations demonstrate strong contextual recognition in both external-context and intra-utterance settings, as well as robust recognition of long-form speech, children's and older adults' speech, low-volume speech, and speech in noisy environments. This report describes the model architecture and training methodology and presents evaluations across recognition accuracy, context utilization, and robustness.

Distributionally Robust BEST-RQ For Speech Representation Learning

Muhammad Khan, Xiaodong Cui, Brian Kingsbury 等4人

BEST-RQ, based on random projection and quantization, has been a well-established self-supervised learning approach to learn speech representations from large-scale unlabeled audio due to its high performance and efficiency. However, in BEST-RQ representations learned by random quantization can be sensitive to the choice of quantization targets. To address this issue, we propose a distributionally robust extension of BEST-RQ that seeks representations robust across multiple quantization configurations. Specifically, we define groups by codebooks and formulate the training as a group Distributionally Robust Optimization (DRO) problem. Rather than optimizing solely for the average loss across groups, our approach adaptively reweights groups to emphasize those performing poorly. Additionally, we regularize DRO with a Kullback-Leibler divergence to prevent excessive distribution deviation. We evaluate our approach on the Multilingual LibriSpeech dataset and show that the distributionally robust BEST-RQ achieves superior performance over the conventional uniform average across multiple codebooks.

An LLM Is All You Need: Data-Efficient Adaptation to Full-Duplex, Word-Level Spoken Dialogue

Tairan Chen, Richard Yucheng He, Yihang Liu 等5人

Full-duplex spoken dialogue, in which a system listens while it speaks and can backchannel, yield the floor, and be interrupted, is usually obtained by training on hundreds of thousands to millions of hours of speech. This is costly, rewrites the language model that supplies the system's knowledge, and still leaves turn-taking either implicit or decided coarsely rather than word by word. We show that a frozen, general-purpose multimodal LLM can instead be adapted into a word-level full-duplex system with little data. Our system is a thinker-talker cascade on an 80 ms frame clock. The thinker keeps the LLM frozen and trains only lightweight adapters and a few new token embeddings (97M parameters); at every frame it emits one token of agent text, with pauses, yields, and onsets represented as tokens of the same vocabulary and kept legal by a finite-state machine. A four-stage curriculum, each stage anchored to the model's own behavior under the previous format, performs the conversion in about 30 GPU-hours. The talker reads only the thinker's discrete tokens, so the two halves are trained independently. On Full-Duplex-Bench and InteractionBench, the system is competitive with open-source full-duplex systems built through large-scale speech training: among them, it is the least likely to take the floor when the user merely pauses, backchannels most often, and leads in spoken-task accuracy, groundedness, and coherence over long conversations.

Kezek: Splitting Mono Two-Person Conversations into Per-Speaker Tracks for Full-Duplex Dialogue Data

Bagzhan Karl, Alizhan Karl

Full-duplex spoken dialogue models learn turn-taking, overlaps and backchannels from conversations recorded with one channel per speaker, but most available conversational audio is a single mono mix. We present Kezek, a model that turns a mono recording of exactly two people into one track per person with the original length and timing; overlaps and short replies ("yeah", "mm-hm") stay on the track of the person who said them. Kezek decides who speaks when and who is who once for the whole recording (stage 1: a two-voice activity model guided by a speaker fingerprint trained to be insensitive to phase), and a conditioned time-domain separator only carries out that decision (stage 2), with outputs that always sum to the input. Standard separation metrics miss the failures that matter for dialogue data, so we report short-reply retention, leakage onto the other person's solo, channel swaps and coverage. Against the open pipeline pyannote -> DialogueSidon on the same recordings, Kezek keeps more short replies (0.83 vs. 0.69 on Open Yap test; 0.80 vs. 0.71 over the other's speech on unseen telephone speech from CallHome English), mostly because it returns the whole recording while the external pipeline drops part of it (on the audio both return, reply retention is about the same: 0.859 vs. 0.865 on Open Yap); it also leaks less, swaps channels less often, and runs 4-5 times faster (about 230 hours of audio per GPU-hour). We also report negative results and an ideal-mask ceiling that locate the remaining gap: quiet replies under a louder voice. Code and weights are released under permissive licences.

MESH: Multi-receiver Evaluation of Spatial Hearing across Egocentric and Allocentric Views

Wen Wen, Bohan Li, KwanHo Tam 等7人

Spatial hearing in multi-receiver environments requires identifying who speaks, where they are, and how they move. Existing benchmarks evaluate spatial prediction, transcription, tracking, and distributed speech processing, but offer limited evaluation of whether speech signal remains associated with the correct physical source across receivers and motion. We introduce MESH, which evaluates this association through egocentric and allocentric views of shared acoustic scenes. The egocentric view uses local recordings and peer metadata, while the allocentric view combines synchronized recordings from multiple receivers in a shared 3D world frame. MESH contains nearly 40,000 questions across 13 static and dynamic localization and spatial transcription tasks. We evaluate eleven audio-model configurations and a language-model agent using specialist tools. The best static distance-estimation and dynamic direction-tracking accuracies are only 21.90% and 41.54%, respectively, while the lowest word error rates across four transcription tasks range from 59.15% to 73.10%. Ablation studies and additional experiments reveal that existing models are not yet able to effectively leverage peer information and struggle to handle multi-speaker and dynamic scenarios. These findings highlight the gap between access to distributed acoustic information and its effective use for spatial speech understanding. We release the dataset and evaluation code to support further research on collaborative and distributed spatial hearing.

The Unseen Threat: How Complementary Combinations of Acoustic Features Compromise Speech Privacy

Jule Pohlhausen, J\"org Bitzer

The interaction of acoustic features in leaking private information remains underexplored. This work focuses on time-domain features, i.e., broadband RMS level and pitch, alone and combined with temporally smoothed and subsampled Mel spectrograms. Privacy is assessed using informed automatic speech recognition and speaker-verification attacks, while utility for conversational analysis is evaluated using voice activity detection and speaker diarisation. RMS level and pitch alone exposed limited information, but fusion with 500 ms-smoothed Mel spectrograms enhanced utility while substantially increasing speech-content leakage. Specifically, the combination with RMS level showed that fine-grained temporal dynamics can amplify leakage from coarsely sampled spectral features. Speaker-identity protection remained limited for all tested features. Overall, these findings indicate that privacy assessments performed on individual acoustic features may underestimate leakage after feature fusion.

Structured Speaker Conditioning for Diarization-Guided Target-Speaker ASR

Ruoyu Wang, Shutong Niu, Jun Du 等5人

We propose structured speaker conditioning (SSC) for diarization-guided target-speaker recognition. SSC combines local activity structure with session-level acoustic identity to construct identity-consistent target conditions that control the acoustic encoder. Target-centric relational encoding (TCRE) retains per-speaker activity histories and factorized second- and third-order relations, capturing overlap and turn changes with aggregation cost linear in the number of tracks. Reference-based speaker conditioning (RSC) supplies session-derived identities that disambiguate local activity patterns within TCRE. Conditional low-rank modulation (CLRM) converts the joint condition into channel-wise affine modulation and a low-rank cross-channel residual with a condition-dependent core. On Qwen3-ASR-0.6B with oracle diarization, SSC uses 1.46M conditioning parameters and achieves a 6.23% mean relative reduction in concatenated minimum-permutation error rate (cpER) over four-state diagonal conditioning across four meeting test sets. SSC also transfers across audio foundation models. With predicted diarization and target-error group sequence policy optimization, the FireRedAudio text system achieves mean relative error reductions of 17.9% and 17.3% over DiCoW and MOSS-TD on the same four sets, and also outperforms both on three unseen domains. Scenario analyses identify gains under challenging overlap conditions, while fixed-checkpoint interventions support the contributions of session identity and the condition-dependent core. A timestamp variant jointly produces sentence boundaries and text. With predicted diarization, macro-average cpER across the four sets is 15.60% for text and 15.96% with timestamps.

qVocalTract: Efficient encoding framework of vocal-tract spectra on a quantum computer

Sheng Li, Jiyi Li

The vocal-tract transfer function links the physical articulatory model to speech. Quantum encoding of vocal-tract transfer functions requires circuits that preserve acoustic structure under hardware noise. This paper proposes the qVocalTract framework, which uses optimized, ancilla-free staircase layers for spectral loading. Simulations and a superconducting processor compare this approach with sequential matrix-product-state preparation on vocal-tract data. Staircase preparation lowers spectral error and improves resonance recovery, while a modal probe demonstrates coherent processing. The comparison reveals a trade-off between approximation accuracy and accumulated circuit noise. These findings guide practical support for further quantum acoustic processing with physical vocal-tract models.

JEV-VAD: Efficient Semantic Endpointing via Task-First Conditioning and Decoder-Prefix Pruning

Jintao Liang, Nan Li, Longbiao Wang 等5人

Semantic endpointing must distinguish a completed turn from a temporary pause, yet its binary output does not require the full generation pathway of an audio-language model. We propose JEV-VAD, an ASR-free framework that combines task-first conditioning, a scalar endpoint readout, and decoder-prefix pruning. The question and completion criteria are placed before audio tokens, allowing causal decoder attention to construct audio states conditioned on the endpointing objective. A dedicated scalar head maps the assistant-prefix state directly to a stop probability, removing transcript generation, answer decoding, and vocabulary-sized output projection. To reduce representation cost, decoder-prefix pruning physically retains only the initial decoder blocks. Matched head refitting then identifies which retained depths provide useful endpoint information under a common adaptation budget. The framework is built on Qwen3-ASR-0.6B and adapted through supervised fine-tuning followed by reinforcement learning for calibrated decisions. Together, these components address input conditioning, output complexity, and decoder depth within one endpointing framework. Experiments on EOT-bench show cross-language transfer from English endpoint supervision and useful discrimination in shallow prefixes, supporting a selectable quality-resource trade-off.

Scaffolding Self-Transduction with Alignment Attention Mask for Aligner-Encoder ASR

Jaeyoung Lee, Masato Mimura

End-to-end ASR systems must jointly solve speech- text alignment and token prediction from sequence-level super- vision. We study this problem in Aligner-Encoder ASR, where alignment is performed inside encoder self-attention and can thus be directly intervened on with an attention mask. We use forced alignment to build an attention mask that confines selected encoder self-attention heads to a narrow window around the alignment path. The mask is faded out over training, leaving a deployable model with an unmodified inference architecture; alternatively, kept active at training and inference, it serves as an oracle that measures recognition performance once alignment is externally resolved. On LibriSpeech, the faded alignment mask improves test-clean/test-other WER from 3.9/6.4 to 3.5/5.9 over an Aligner baseline. The oracle masks are far stronger, reaching 1.2/2.9 WER, which can be thought of as an upper- bound accuracy in the 100M-class Conformer regime without an external language model. This indicates that alignment inference is a major bottleneck in Aligner-Encoder ASR, and suggests that improved alignment mechanisms might yield substantial accuracy gains for end-to-end ASR.

Speech Tokenizers are Vulnerable: Transferable Semantic Attack and Robust Tokenizer

Zhisheng Zhang, Yifan Mi, Yixuan Zhou 等9人

Speech tokenizers serve as speech front ends, encoding input speech signals into representations for downstream models, such as automatic speech recognition (ASR) systems and large audio-language models (LALMs). In this paper, we find that speech tokenizers are fragile at the semantic level: small perturbations can disrupt their semantic encoding process and consequently cause severe degradation in downstream ASR and LALM performance. To study this vulnerability, we propose T-SemAttack, a transferable semantic attack based on a semantic encoder ensemble. By jointly drifting the representation spaces of multiple semantic encoders, T-SemAttack effectively disrupts the semantic content of speech while preserving perceptual quality, thereby inducing error features. We further analyze layer-wise representation drift and cross-model gradient alignment. Our findings show that perturbations can induce substantial shifts in deep semantic representations and that the input gradients of different surrogate encoders exhibit directional alignment. These observations suggest that shared adversarially sensitive directions in the input space may be a key factor underlying cross-model attack transferability. Building on these, we introduce ROSETok, a robust speech tokenizer that combines noisy training with robust semantic distillation to improve reconstruction fidelity and downstream SpeechLLM robustness. Extensive experiments on multiple datasets, 9 open-source and black-box ASR systems, and 4 LALMs demonstrate that T-SemAttack achieves strong transferable attack performance, while the proposed Robust Speech Tokenizer exhibits robustness under high-fidelity reconstruction and downstream tasks. Our code and demo are available at https://wxzyd123.github.io/t-semattack.
