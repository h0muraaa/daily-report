Closed-Loop Dual-Reference Guidance for Accent Control in Cross-Lingual Voice Cloning

Yuanzhong Zheng, Wei Huang, Ming Ma 等5人

Zero-shot cross-lingual voice cloning often carries the reference speaker's accent into the target language. We propose closed-loop dual-reference guidance, which pairs the original reference with a reusable native anchor constructed by voice conversion (VC). The references initialize separate language-model (LM) states that guide a shared acoustic trajectory; each completed patch then updates both states, keeping their generated histories aligned with the emitted speech. Anchor construction runs once per speaker, and online synthesis uses a frozen TTS model without per-utterance VC. On 20 held-out French speakers, the fixed setting $\lambda^{\!}=0.33$ reduces residual French LID mass by 73% versus direct cloning, at an ECAPA similarity cost of only $0.042$; relative to anchor-only cloning, it recovers $0.155$. The method achieves the lowest mean WER ($0.009$) and highest mean GOP ($4.750$) among the held-out test arms. In blinded preference tests, native-English listeners favor it over direct cloning on pronunciation naturalness, and over both anchor-only cloning and a TTS$\to$VC cascade on speaker identity. Online RTF is approximately 26% lower than the cascade's ($0.59$ vs. $0.80$), and the same setting reduces Spanish LID mass by 56% without re-tuning.

How Far Can the Gemma 4 Speech Encoder Go Without Changing Its Weights? Saudi and Gulf Arabic Speech Recognition with a Frozen and an Adapter-Augmented Encoder

Amr Abdelkhalik

We study the 305M-parameter audio encoder shared by Gemma 4 and EmbeddingGemma 2 (bit-identical to that of Gemma 4 E2B except for the final audio-embedding projection) for Saudi and Gulf Arabic speech recognition, without changing any of its pretrained weights; the adapters of our adapted system are additive and kept separate. A frozen system trains only a 22.6M-parameter CTC reader over a learned mix of four intermediate encoder layers and the encoder output; an adapted system adds 3.1M low-rank adapter parameters, a 41.5M-parameter hybrid CTC/attention reader and joint decoding with a sub-word language model. Against Cohere Transcribe Arabic (2.07B parameters), re-run on identical audio, the frozen system trails in word error rate (WER) but is not significantly different in character error rate (CER) on five of six human-labelled conditions, while running 3.4 times faster on CPU with greedy decoding. The adapted system is not significantly different in WER on a public Saudi customer-service call (19.20% against 19.27%) and on clean Emirati speech, is significantly better on telephone-coded Emirati speech, has a significantly lower CER on all six conditions of these sets, and beats a 0.6B recogniser fine-tuned on SADA in WER on all six; it still trails Cohere in WER on long-form Saudi speech. On a blind, human-corrected public call-centre sample it ranks 4 of 15 systems, behind three commercial APIs (significantly only one) and ahead of the other self-hostable systems by point estimate. An optimised joint decoder runs it at 139 times real time on one GPU. We also find, on our data, that references made by correcting machine drafts favour the drafting system. Code and public-set results are released.

Combining Masked Language Modeling and Cross-Modal Contrastive Learning for Prosody-Aware TTS

Kirill Borodin, Vasiliy Kudryavtsev, Maxim Maslov 等6人

Text encoders in neural text-to-speech (TTS) see only linguistic content, so prosody must be inferred from paired data and synthesised intonation is often flat. Contrastive text-audio pretraining (CLAPSpeech) injects prosodic knowledge by contrasting acoustic realisations of the same phoneme, but it never trains the encoder to tell different phonemes apart, and its interaction with masked language modelling (MLM) is unexplored. We study a curriculum for a speaker-conditioned phoneme-and-subword encoder: MLM, contrastive alignment over batches of different phonemes, and CLAPSpeech-style same-phoneme refinement. On Russian conversational speech, with each variant conditioning an identical Grad-TTS model, MLM followed by mixed-phoneme alignment gives the lowest word error rate (17.6% versus 32.8% for same-phoneme training alone) and the best quality and intonation ratings. Adding same-phoneme refinement improves same-phoneme retrieval yet yields the worst synthesis: embedding-space retrieval does not predict usefulness for generation.

ShlokaDhara: A Multi-Speaker Sanskrit Speech Corpus for Learning Meter-Aware Prosody in Text-to-Recitation

Harshal Kausadikar, Onkar Kishor Susladkar, Prathmesh Santosh Choudhari 等6人

Sanskrit verse recitation is governed by $\textit{Chandas}$, a strict prosodic framework defined by rhythmic $\textit{Laghu}$ (light) and $\textit{Guru}$ (heavy) syllables. Because existing Sanskrit speech datasets prioritize ASR or conversational TTS, they lack the metrical fidelity required for authentic verse synthesis. We introduce $\textbf{$\textit{ShlokaDhara}$}$, a 43-hour, multi-speaker Sanskrit speech corpus capturing diverse meters, recitation styles, and melodic variations. We specifically focus on $\textit{Text-to-Recite}$, a synthesis task explicitly requiring both prosodic accuracy and metrical correctness. Benchmarking state-of-the-art TTS architectures via an automated UTMOS (neural MOS) predictor demonstrates that XTTS-v2 achieves the highest perceptual quality. We further validate the XTTS-v2 generated samples using two paradigms: subjective assessments by 16 Sanskrit experts and novel empirical metrics, specifically, syllable-level $\textit{Laghu-Guru}$ duration analysis to objectively quantify structural metrical awareness. Ultimately, this work establishes a robust dataset and evaluation benchmark for prosody-aware computational Sanskrit recitation. Generated Samples: https://shlokadhara.vercel.app/

FiPA-SR -- FiLM-Conditioned Perceptually Informed Audio Super-Resolution

Wallace Abreu, Luiz W. P. Biscainho

Audio bandwidth extension aims to reconstruct missing high-frequency content from bandlimited signals. This paper proposes FiPA-SR, a GAN-based perceptual architecture capable of handling different input bandwidths within a single model. Building upon the previous $\textrm{AEROMamba}_\textrm{P}$ framework, the proposed model incorporates FiLM layers to adapt the reconstruction process according to the respective bandwidth. Experiments on the MUSDB dataset show that FiPA-SR outperforms the state-of-the-art AudioSR model across 8, 20, and 32 kHz input sampling rates. Moreover, the proposed architecture uses approximately 3$\times$ less GPU memory and performs inference more than 60$\times$ faster than the diffusion-based baseline.

Muting Tokens: Exploring Token Sparsification for Efficient Audio Transformers

Shreya Sajid, Fabrizio Julian Piva, Hiram Rayo Torres Rodriguez 等6人

Transformer-based models have shown strong performance across diverse tasks, but their reliance on self-attention mechanisms often leads to high computational costs. Token sparsification, which reduces the number of tokens processed in transformer layers, offers a promising solution to maintain performance while improving efficiency. However, although widely studied in the vision and language domains, token sparsification remains underexplored in audio, with existing work often limited to a single method, model size, or dataset. This work presents a comprehensive evaluation of token sparsification techniques for audio transformers. We assess a range of token pruning and merging strategies across multiple datasets and model sizes, under both post-training and fine-tuning settings. Furthermore, we introduce a novel sparsification approach that combines attention scores with spectral cues derived from the frequency content of token embeddings. Our experiments show that frequency-aware approaches, particularly those based on spectral flatness and low-frequency energy, consistently outperform attention-only methods. We also find that sparsification based on spectrogram patch intensity is especially effective for longer audio inputs. These results highlight the value of incorporating frequency-domain and input-specific information into sparsification strategies to enhance performance in audio tasks.

MQAScore: Fine-Grained Text-Music Alignment Evaluation via Large Audio Language Model

Wei-Jaw Lee, Chun-Ping Wang, Xuanjun Chen 等4人

Text-to-music alignment is commonly evaluated by the global similarity between text and audio embeddings, which collapses all described attributes into a single score and obscures which ones are missing. We instead introduce MQAScore, which verifies each atomic musical concept along five clip-level dimensions (genre, instrument, mood/theme, vocal, and usage) by the probability that a large audio language model (LALM) answers affirmatively to a binary question. To assess evaluator reliability, we construct verification labels from presence/absence annotations and LLM-guided concept substitutions, and propose the Sensitivity-Invariance Ratio, which measures how selectively an evaluator responds to meaning-changing substitutions. Experiments show that concept-level scoring improves both question-answering and similarity-based evaluators, and MQAScore can be a more reliable and interpretable fine-grained complement to global similarity for evaluating text-to-music alignment.

Signed Wasserstein distance for room impulse response comparison

Linda Fabiani, Sebastian J. Schlecht, Filip Elvander

The nature of room impulse response signals is often adverse to a clear evaluation of similarity: they are sparse, rapidly varying, signed signals resulting from the complex interaction of room reflections and phase relationships. Therefore, conventional point-wise metrics may be sensitive to small temporal shifts and provide poor loss surfaces for optimization. In this paper, we propose an optimal transport-based distance for RIR similarity that explicitly accounts for the structure of impulse responses, inspired by the signed Wasserstein distance formulation. Optimal transport provides a natural framework for this problem, by comparing signals based on how their underlying distributions are displaced rather than requiring a one-to-one correspondence. We evaluate the proposed distance against commonly used audio baseline metrics through a series of room-parameter perturbation studies. The results demonstrate the advantage of using the signed Wasserstein metric for geometric evaluation tasks and show a promising performance for room parameter optimization within a differentiable Image-Source model.

PerMix: Anomalous sound detection by predicting the perturbation ratio of mixed samples

Jihoon Choi, Jung-Woo Choi

In anomalous sound detection based on self-supervised learning, the performance of the detection model largely depends on the selection of the pretext task through which the characteristics of normal data are learned. A common pretext task is to mix samples from different classes and classify the mixed sample as its target class with an angular margin loss. However, this supervision becomes mismatched when the target sample is the minority component of the mixture and the margin remains fixed irrespective of the mixing ratio. To address these issues, we propose a pretext task that directly predicts the perturbation ratio of mixed samples by reformulating the logit so that the angular margin is scaled by the perturbation ratio. In addition, we adopt a supervised contrastive loss modified for mixed samples, in which positive pairs are formed according to the dominant class. Experiments show that our approach achieves state-of-the-art results on the DCASE 2020 Task 2 benchmark. Our code is available at https://github.com/kaistss/PerMix.

Spark-Audio 1.0 Technical Report

Chenyue Zhang, Cong Liu, Dingshu Tian 等66人

This paper introduces Spark-Audio 1.0, a speech foundation model trained on domestically produced computing infrastructure. The model accepts text and speech inputs and generates text outputs. It supports multilingual (99 languages) and multi-dialect (202 dialects) speech recognition, speech translation, audio perception and understanding, and both text- and speech-based dialogue, marking a shift from merely ``hearing clearly'' to genuinely ``understanding'' speech. Architecturally, Spark-Audio 1.0 couples a 0.65B-parameter dense audio encoder with a 30B-A3B Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) large language model (LLM). The audio encoder is pretrained with representation-enhanced BEST-RQ and then refined through multi-task supervised training. The audio--LLM is trained through staged pretraining, followed by post-training that combines supervised fine-tuning with on-policy distillation. In total, the model is trained on 13 million hours of audio data together with large-scale text data. We evaluate Spark-Audio 1.0 on mainstream audio and text benchmarks, as well as on in-house test sets drawn from real-world application scenarios. Despite using substantially less audio training data than Qwen3.5-Omni-Flash, Spark-Audio 1.0 achieves comparable performance on most audio and text tasks, outperforms it in multilingual speech recognition, spoken dialogue, and real-world application scenarios, and approaches the performance of larger closed-source models. Nevertheless, there remains room for improvement in instruction following and complex audio reasoning. Finally, the successful training of Spark-Audio 1.0 on domestic hardware, including Huawei Ascend 910B and Hygon BW1000 chips, demonstrates the feasibility of a domestic technical pathway for building speech foundation models.

A corpus-only AUC diagnostic for pooled speech-based depression detection

Cezary Polak, Milosz Dudek, Daria Hemmerling

When clinical speech corpora are pooled for evaluation, corpus membership can itself become predictive if class prevalences differ across sources. We derive the AUC of an oracle predictor that uses only corpus identity and empirical prevalence, a diagnostic computable directly from corpus composition. Across five depression corpora in three languages (590 speakers), the corpus-only AUC is 0.697, and corpus identity is predicted from six acoustic and self-supervised representations with 97.5-99.8% accuracy. Permuting depression labels within each corpus removes label structure while preserving prevalences; the residual pooled-minus-LOCO discrepancy then increases across three nested configurations in the same order as the diagnostic. Pooled 5-fold cross-validation also outscores corpus-held-out evaluation across all six representations. Prevalence equalisation substantially reduces the discrepancy for eGeMAPS and WavLM-Base, whereas a sample-size-matched control does not. Corpus-specific base rates therefore create a measurable shortcut opportunity in pooled clinical-speech evaluation, motivating corpus-aware diagnostics alongside pooled performance.

Hear Every Sound Object: Rescuing Overlooked Sources in Auditory Scene Analysis

Younghoo Kwon, Yongjoon Lee, Jung-Woo Choi

Auditory scene analysis (ASA) models extract information about individual sound sources from a complex auditory mixture. Recent object-based ASA models process the mixture through a sequence of modules and split it into source-wise features (i.e., object features) only at the last layer. However, substantial disparities in performance across sources limit overall performance. Our analysis using centered kernel alignment (CKA) reveals that CKA scores correlate strongly with per-source performance, with their disparities widening from intermediate to deeper layers. These findings motivate the early split and exchange of object features to address source-wise performance disparities before they widen in deeper layers. To this end, we propose PARASITE, a plug-in refinement architecture that splits object features at earlier layers and progressively refines them through sequential layers. Each layer integrates object features separated at different depths using Object-wise Gated Fusion (OGF) and then refines them using Windowed Cross-Object Attention (WCOA). We introduce the DeepASA++ model, which combines PARASITE with an ASA backbone and delivers state-of-the-art results on both ASA2 and STARSS23. DeepASA++ achieves a SELD score of 0.240 on STARSS23 and surpasses prior multi-stage inference architectures, including chain-of-inference.

Lip-Focused Audio-Visual Consistency Detects Speech Content Manipulation beyond Coarse Synchronization

Jian Zong, Chenghan Lin, Meng Liu 等9人

Speech-content manipulation changes what a visible person appears to say by replacing the speech track and, in stronger attacks, regenerating mouth motion to match the forged utterance. Such attacks are difficult to expose with texture artifacts or coarse lip-sync scores because modern retalking models explicitly optimize audio-visual synchrony. We test the narrower hypothesis that speech replacement still perturbs the local relationship between acoustic dynamics and mouth motion after speaker identity or coarse synchrony is controlled. To isolate this signal, we introduce SyncSpeech, an LRS3-derived benchmark containing real videos, audio-splicing cheapfakes and Wav2Lip retalking attacks under same-speaker and different-speaker replacement. We then introduce C-MIND, a lip-focused detector that aligns audio and mouth-motion representations at frame level and models their agreement and disagreement without transcripts, automatic speech recognition features or an unmanipulated reference. On SyncSpeech, pretrained SyncLip-C achieves 95.813% scenario-averaged accuracy and 0.9943 AUC, outperforming sentence-level fusion, full-face inputs and a reproduced SyncNet baseline under the reported protocol. When trained on the corresponding FakeAVCeleb training split, C-MIND correctly classifies 278 of 280 samples in the selected unseen-speaker test subset, whereas accuracy falls to 82.775% on unseen Video-Retalking attacks. These results identify lip-focused speech-articulation consistency as a useful but generator-sensitive forensic signal within the evaluated settings.

HC-AINet: An Auditory-Inspired Network with Harmonic Compensation for Low-Complexity Speech Enhancement

Nan Li, Xiaoxiong Wu, Huawei Liu 等4人

Speech enhancement on resource-constrained devices requires noise suppression with limited computation and minimal loss of spectral detail. Auditory-inspired subband processing reduces computation, but its smooth gains have limited ability to distinguish harmonic peaks from neighboring noise. We propose a harmonic-compensated auditory-inspired network (HC-AINet) for low-complexity speech enhancement. A triangular equivalent rectangular bandwidth (ERB) filterbank supplies compact features to a lightweight network comprising inverted-residual units, temporal Conformer layers, and parallel frequency-gated convolutions. The network predicts global and harmonic gains together with fusion weights. A harmonic-location map derived from the noisy spectrum confines the harmonic contribution to selected bins on the original frequency grid, while the global branch controls the broader spectral envelope. The compensated magnitude, noisy phase, and a magnitude-derived minimum-phase cue jointly condition a compact phase estimator for waveform reconstruction. On VoiceBank-DEMAND, the 64-band model achieves a PESQ of 3.20 and a STOI of 0.95 with approximately 0.27 million parameters.
