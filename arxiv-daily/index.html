Spatial DJ: Spatial Audio Control for Arbitrary Loudspeaker Layouts

Anastasia Geleta, Margarita Geleta

Object-based spatial audio promises render-anywhere playback, yet deploying it in a new venue still requires two tasks that are rarely addressed together: acquiring the physical loudspeaker geometry and enabling creators manipulate spatial audio while listening through the target loudspeaker system rather than a headphone proxy. We present Spatial DJ, a system for on-site loudspeaker layout acquisition and real-time spatial audio rendering and control, using head-worn AR in a coordinate frame aligned to the physical loudspeakers. In an 18-loudspeaker room, we evaluate calibration accuracy, repeatability, and control latency, and conduct an 18-participant listener study comparing diffuse and orbiting point-source rendering configurations applied to vocal and instrumental layers. Orbiting point-source conditions received higher ratings of perceived immersion, energy, and novelty than the diffuse baseline. Beyond this controlled setting, four representative deployments illustrate reuse of the same system across heterogeneous loudspeaker venues.

SPHERE: A Multi-Attribute No-Reference metric for Binaural Speech Quality And Spatial Fidelity

Bala Manikya Prasad Puram, Chandan K A Reddy, Artem Dementyev

In this paper, we introduce SPHERE (Spatial and Perceptual Human Evaluation and Rating Estimator), a multidimensional no-reference perceptual evaluation framework for binaural speech. SPHERE defines nine distinct perceptual and spatial attributes that characterize the immersive listening experience. We detail the crowdsourcing protocol and evaluation template used to collect ground-truth human ratings. To demonstrate the tractability of automating this framework, we evaluate six baseline configurations across single-branch and Siamese architectures utilizing Mel-spectrograms, complex STFTs, and pretrained spatial embeddings (PhaseCoder). Experimental results demonstrate that these baseline estimators achieve strong correlation with human subjective ratings, proving the feasibility of automated multidimensional spatial audio quality assessment.

C3: Speech-Guided KV Retrieval for Long-Context SpeechLLM Inference

Dancheng Liu, Markus M\"uller, Rupak Vignesh Swaminathan 等6人

Speech language models encode audio at a much higher rate than text, causing the KV cache to dominate the memory, bandwidth, and decoding cost of long-form generation. KV retrieval can reduce per-step access by attending to only a query-dependent subset of the cache, but existing methods are designed primarily for text and do not account for the structure of audio attention. We first show that retrieval errors may compound autoregressively into generation drift. We then analyze full audio attention during long-form generation and identify three properties relevant to retrieval: unequal attention concentration across query heads, non-contiguous audio support, and temporal continuity across decoding steps. Guided by these observations, we propose $C^3$(Cluster-Compete-Compete), which organizes audio KVs in a hierarchical key-space index, lets query heads sharing a KV head compete for a common retrieval budget, and adaptively reallocates retrieval resolution through paired split-merge updates. Across long-form speech recognition, translation, and summarization on four SpeechLLMs, $C^3$ achieves the strongest overall performance among retrieval methods under comparable final-KV access budgets, with particularly large gains in the tight-budget regime, while improving long-context decoding efficiency and accelerator-memory scaling.

One Waveform, Two Stories: Benchmarking Speech-Acoustic Conflict in Audio-Language Models

Wenbin Wang, Yuheng Li, Koichiro Yoshino

Audio-language models (ALMs) are evaluated on their understanding of both speech and environmental sounds. What remains underexplored is how they respond when spoken claims contradict acoustic evidence within the same recording. We introduce SAC-Bench, a benchmark of intra-audio speech--acoustic conflict comprising 616,031 unique clips from 188 sound-event classes across seven semantic, temporal, and spatial tasks. It pairs environmental recordings with compatible or contradictory synthesized spoken claims and includes audio-only controls where applicable. Across five open-weight ALMs evaluated on semantic and temporal tasks, high audio-only accuracy often coexists with large losses under conflicting claims; Event Identity accuracy is 24.8--46.5 percentage points lower in conflict than compatible conditions. Temporal Order failures persist even when speech follows the complete event sequence, so simultaneous speech--event masking cannot fully explain them. A spatial case study with Spatial-Omni finds lower motion accuracy associated with both spoken claims and the speech source's physical trajectory. Prompt interventions sometimes restore acoustic accuracy, but can also reduce claim-aligned answers without improving grounding or worsen performance. SAC-Bench thus separates acoustic competence, conflict robustness, and intervention effects.

SPATEX: Array-Flexible Multichannel-to-Multichannel Target Speech Extraction using Direction Clue

Wen Wen, Changda Chen, Yu Xi 等10人

Multichannel-to-multichannel target speech extraction (MIMO TSE) aims to recover the target speech signal at each microphone while preserving interchannel spatial information. However, existing MIMO TSE systems generally assume a fixed microphone configuration, limiting their applicability to recording devices with varying microphone configurations. In this work, we propose SPATEX, the first array-flexible MIMO TSE framework that reconstructs microphone-wise target speech signals across microphone counts and array geometries. Given microphone coordinates and a target direction-of-arrival (DOA), SPATEX combines geometry-aware channel exchange and shared temporal attention to jointly reconstruct microphone-wise target signals while maintaining permutation equivariance. Experiments show that SPATEX outperforms array-flexible single-output baselines under most test conditions and consistently improves extraction quality and spatial fidelity over fixed-array MIMO baselines. Ablation studies further validate the benefits of temporal coordination and spatial supervision, while additional evaluations demonstrate robustness to target DOA errors. Our code and dataset are available at https://github.com/Wenanzhi/SPATEX.

Zero-Shot Voice Cloning for Synthetic Data Augmentation in Children's ASR

Mohammad Mohsen Goodarzi, Stefan Wabnik, Matthias Stennes 等5人

Children's automatic speech recognition (ASR) lags behind adult ASR due to theacoustic mismatch between adult-trained acoustic models and children's speech, as well as the scarcity of labelled children's speech corpora. We investigate whether zero-shot text-to-speech (TTS) synthesis with voice cloning, requiring only a short reference clip per speaker, can serve as a practical substitute for real children's speech when fine-tuning large pre-trained ASR models. Using the OmniVoice multilingual TTS system we synthesise up to 16,000 child-like utterances from two English corpora (ENNI, clinical narrative samples, ages 7-12, and MyST, conversational science-tutor speech, grades 3-5) and fine-tune Whisper-small under different experimental conditions covering real data, synthetic data, cross-corpus data, and all-data combination. A systematic sensitivity analysis over speaker count, text-pool size, and utterances per speaker reveals that synthetic data alone reduces word error rate (WER) from a 19.8 % zero-shot baseline to 16.4 % on ENNI, recovering approximately 30 % of the gap to real-data fine-tuning (8.8 %). Combining synthetic with real data is essentially indistinguishable from real-data-only fine-tuning (9.2 % vs. 8.8 %). Speaker count plateaus at ~40 speakers for cross-domain transfer, while multi-speaker acoustic diversity per transcript, rather than raw text coverage, emerges as the primary determinant of cross-domain generalisation; out-of-vocabulary (OOV) rate proves to be a poor proxy for WER given Whisper's pre-trained language modelling capacity.

A Compact Statistical View of Musical-Instrument Directivity

Gioele Greco, Mirco Pezzoli, Raffaele Malvermi 等5人

Directivity is a defining feature of musical-instrument radiation and is known to vary strongly with frequency. With the growing availability of measured directivity datasets and their use in sound-field simulation and spatial audio, there is a practical need for representations that are both compact and comparable across sources. This work addresses that need by proposing a statistically grounded description of frequency-dependent directivity that reduces complex radiation patterns to a small set of meaningful descriptors. The approach enables directivity patterns to be summarized in an interpretable way and compared consistently across frequency bands, even when the radiation is not well described by a single dominant direction. The resulting representation supports tasks such as analysis, categorization, and model building, providing a bridge between detailed measurements and higher-level descriptors useful for musical acoustics and audio rendering. Experiments on a corpus of 41 orchestral instruments show that the proposed method preserves the distance geometry well while reducing the full order-4, 30-band corpus from about 492~kB to about 100~kB in double-precision storage.

Sequence-to-Sequence Refinement for EEND-VC Diarization with a Shared Encoder

Ruoyu Wang, Jun Du, Weixin Zhu 等4人

End-to-end neural diarization with vector clustering (EEND-VC) separates local segmentation from global speaker assignment, leaving speaker confusion (SC) despite strong segmentation. We propose shared-encoder sequence-to-sequence (S2S) refinement, which reuses DiariZen's self-supervised learning (SSL) representations for speaker-conditioned decoding and prior-constrained assignment. We study frozen-feature refinement with WavLM and structurally pruned w2v-BERT 2.0, and extend the latter to joint training of local segmentation and refinement heads. The heads learn separate layer mixtures while their losses update one acoustic encoder. Across eight domains, frozen-feature refinement reduces macro diarization error rate (DER) from 12.95% to 12.40% with WavLM and from 12.35% to 12.08% with w2v-BERT 2.0, with relative SC reductions of 17.4% and 14.6%. It adds approximately 15% to system real-time factor. The joint system further lowers macro DER to 11.90%, improving seven of eight domains over non-joint refinement. Error decomposition separates SC-driven refinement gains from detection-driven joint-system gains.

How Much Prosody Does Your ASR Encoder Already Know

Rini Sharon, Kadri Hacioglu, Andreas Stolcke

End-of-turn (EoT) detection decides in real-time whether a speaker has finished. Traditional detectors rely on hand-built prosodic features (pitch, speaking rate, pauses), while modern detectors run on a neural ASR encoder that has processed the same audio. Because the encoder reads the signal those features are computed from, it may already hold much of the information they were meant to supply. We test this three ways, reading a streaming Zipformer encoder at four depths from raw input to the final layer. First, we measure how much prosody helps over the encoder alone. Second, we test whether prosody can be recovered from the encoder. Third, we ask whether any single feature still helps given the encoder. The three tests agree; prosody adds a large gain on raw audio, but that gain collapses once the signal passes through even the first encoder layer. The same pattern holds for two open-source encoders, wav2vec2 and Whisper, both of which are offline.

Count-Density Networks for Modal Plate Parameter Estimation

Rodrigo Diaz, Pablo Tablas De Paula, David Marttila 等5人

We describe two submissions to Task B of the 1st DAFx Parameter Estimation Challenge, which estimates an unknown number of modal frequency, decay, and gain triples from a synthetic plate-reverb impulse response. The first method combines pooled spectral features with time-domain and absolute-scale conditioning in a real-valued convolutional count-density network, while the second uses a complex-valued Transformer count-density network. Both methods jointly infer the modal count and per-mode attributes directly from the IR. On an independently generated 100-IR comparison set, the two neural estimators achieve lower overall challenge error than the evaluated classical baselines, with frequency and decay estimation substantially more accurate than gain estimation.

Understanding the relationship between retrospective soundscape attributes and continuous pleasantness and eventfulness ratings

Yuanbo Hou, Kening Guo, Francesco Aletta 等6人

Soundscape studies ask listeners to rate perceived affective quality (PAQ) attributes, such as pleasant and vibrant, after listening to recordings or experiencing acoustic environments. These retrospective ratings summarise an experience during which impressions may change. International Organization for Standardization (ISO) procedures convert these PAQ ratings into Pleasantness and Eventfulness scores. Pleasantness and Eventfulness (P/E) can also be rated directly and continuously during listening, capturing changes in reported perception over time. Retrospective PAQ ratings and continuous direct P/E describe the same soundscape experience from different perspectives. We examine how PAQs relate to direct P/E and how ISO-derived P/E corresponds to direct judgements, using 1,782 paired 30-s trials involving audiovisual recordings from 18 streets. The results show that PAQs retain a predominantly two-dimensional structure. Vibrant and Monotonous lie closer to the Eventfulness-Uneventfulness axis in the direct P/E plane than in the ISO 2025 reference geometry. The full 30-s direct P/E mean has lower calibrated error against ISO-derived P/E than three 10-s means. An empirical mapping using the same 8 PAQs reduces held-out joint root-mean-square error by 4.9% relative to affine-calibrated ISO 2025 scoring. The findings show that retrospective and continuous assessments offer related but distinct soundscape descriptions.

Continual Residual Memory for Gradient-Free Test-Time Adaptation in Speech Enhancement

Yijia Shan, Tianrui Wang, Zixiang Wang 等7人

Offline-trained speech enhancement models can degrade under deployment mismatch. Test-time adaptation (TTA) addresses this problem using unlabeled test observations, but existing approaches mainly rely on online parameter optimization and do not explicitly exploit the residual discarded by the enhancement backbone. We propose Continual Residual Memory (CRM), a gradient-free continual TTA framework for selective residual recovery. CRM first estimates bounded time-frequency residual recovery from the current utterance alone and then uses causal prototype memory from preceding utterances to refine this estimate. During deployment, adaptation is driven solely by the evolving memory state while the backbone and CRM modules remain frozen. Across DNS, EARS-D, and Libri-MUSAN, CRM improves all seven reported metrics over frozen CMGAN and achieves the best PESQ, CSIG, and COVL on all three test sets among the compared methods. CRM remains lightweight and shows cross-backbone applicability on five additional backbones. Code is available at https://github.com/Ada312/continual-residual-memory.

Multilingual TASU: Extending Text-Only Alignment to Multilingual Speech Recognition via Simulated CTC Posteriors

Chenghao Wang, Yi Yang, Jing Peng 等6人

Speech Large Language Models (Speech LLMs) achieve strong multilingual ASR when large-scale paired speech-text data is available, yet such resources remain scarce and highly imbalanced across languages. Text-Only Alignment for Speech Understanding (TASU) alleviates this bottleneck by training cross-modal alignment from simulated CTC posteriors without paired audio, but prior work has been limited to English, leaving open whether text-driven alignment can scale to the multilingual setting where data sparsity and cross-lingual phonetic interference amplify the difficulty of learning a shared alignment interface. In this work, we investigate TASU-style posterior alignment as a means of mitigating paired-data scarcity in multilingual ASR. We adopt the native 50,002-way posterior space of a frozen OWSM-CTC frontend as a shared multilingual interface to Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, and systematically compare three supervision sources on eight Multilingual LibriSpeech languages: real audio-derived posteriors, heuristic CTC Posterior Simulation (CPS), and posteriors generated by a learned multilingual simulator. With real posteriors, LoRA adaptation reduces pooled WER from 10.08% (standalone OWSM-CTC) to 8.21%, improving all eight languages. For text-only alignment, the learned simulator substantially outperforms heuristic CPS and attains 10.51% WER with LoRA. Notably, a CPS-initialized model reaches 9.19% WER after only one real-posterior adaptation epoch using merely one-ninth of the complete MLS paired-speech training set, demonstrating that text-first initialization can drastically reduce the amount of paired audio required for competitive multilingual ASR. These findings establish TASU as a viable multilingual alignment framework and highlight text-only simulation as an effective remedy for paired-data scarcity and imbalance across languages.

Learning Timeline-Preserving Speech-to-Speech Translation

Zhisheng Zheng, David Harwath

Speech-to-speech translation (S2ST) increasingly preserves what is said and how it sounds, but not necessarily when speech and silence occur. For dubbing and other temporally grounded applications, matching total duration is not enough: speech and pauses must also occur at corresponding moments. We formulate timeline-preserving S2ST, which aims to preserve the source endpoint and salient internal pauses on a shared absolute timeline. We present TransDubber, an end-to-end autoregressive system that learns this temporal structure directly from aligned target waveforms, without inference-time frame-level timing control or post-hoc temporal correction. To provide this supervision, we construct 642K exactly duration-matched English-Chinese speech pairs by translating and synthesizing target speech on source-defined temporal scaffolds. Training combines a progressive supervised curriculum with verifier-guided reinforcement learning to balance translation quality and temporal alignment. On a 4,353-utterance test set spanning read and spontaneous speech, TransDubber increases the proportion of translations within 2% of the source duration from 25.6% to 61.5% and pause-alignment F1 from 0.118 to 0.550 compared with UniSS, while maintaining competitive translation quality. Our analysis shows that aligned supervision establishes timeline following, while reinforcement learning refines temporal precision and partially offsets the translation-quality cost of strict synchronization. Together, these results demonstrate the effectiveness of our data and training framework for timeline-preserving S2ST. Audio samples, code, datasets, and pretrained models are available at https://zhishengzheng.com/transdubber/.
