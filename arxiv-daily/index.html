Depth through recurrence: Looped transformers for flow-matching TTS

Jiabao Ai, Peng Han, Yuchen Song 等4人

We study how to organize Transformer depth through recurrence in flow-matching text-to-speech, varying the amount, order, and placement of weight reuse. Seven layouts perform 18 block calls per network evaluation under a common training objective and sampler. On Seed-TTS and LibriSpeech-PC, SEQUENCE applies each of nine blocks twice consecutively, retaining competitive intelligibility, speaker similarity, and predicted speech quality at 32 sampling steps with 47.1% fewer parameters than the unshared baseline. Cycling six blocks three times further reduces model size but raises 32-step word error rates relative to cycling nine blocks twice. At matched parameter counts and executed depth, reuse order and sharing position produce different quality trade-offs. These comparisons depend on sampling budget: Prefix and Suffix have similar 32-step word error rates, but Suffix is worse by 3.44 and 5.97 percentage points at four steps on the two datasets, respectively. Only Middle ranks first or second in mean word error rate at 32 and four steps on both datasets. These results show that the organization of recurrent computation affects synthesis quality, and that reuse layouts should be selected for both the sampling budget and the quality dimensions of interest.

A Semi-spontaneous Dutch Speech Dataset for Speech Enhancement and Speech Recognition

Dimme de Groot, Yuanyuan Zhang, Jorge Martinez 等4人

We present DRES: a 1.5-hour Dutch realistic elicited (semi-spontaneous) speech dataset from 80 speakers recorded in noisy, public indoor environments. DRES was designed as a test set for the evaluation of state-of-the-art (SotA) automatic speech recognition (ASR) and speech enhancement (SE) models in a real-world scenario: a person speaking in a public indoor space with background talkers and noise. The speech was recorded with a four-channel linear microphone array. In this work we evaluate the speech quality of five well-known single-channel SE algorithms and the recognition performance of eight SotA off-the-shelf ASR models before and after applying SE on the speech of DRES. We found that five out of the eight ASR models have WERs lower than 22\% on DRES, despite the challenging conditions. In contrast to recent work, we did not find a positive effect of modern single-channel SE on ASR performance, emphasizing the importance of evaluating in realistic conditions.

A Learned Musical-Time Coordinate for Piano Audio-to-Score Transcription

Min-Hsing Wang, Tsung-Ren Huang

End-to-end piano audio-to-score (A2S) transcription is the task of reconstructing a notated score from an audio recording in a single system. Existing A2S approaches usually rely on pre-segmented excerpts, and few handle whole pieces without external segmentation. This raises the question of whether a musical-time coordinate can guide score transcription while also helping the system segment whole pieces. Previous work on jazz saxophone A2S uses an external beat tracker to obtain such a coordinate for score prediction. We introduce downbeat phase, a musical-time coordinate learned jointly with score transcription that represents each audio frame's relative position within a notated bar. With this coordinate, our system can not only condition its score decoder on downbeat phase but also self-segment at bar boundaries and stitch the segments into a whole piece. Evaluation suggests that the coordinate improves metrical structure and supports whole-piece transcription with comparable notation quality.

Bio-Inspired Auditory Processing: Coupling Parallel Resonate-and-Fire Dynamics with Ternary Recurrent Networks for Speech Recognition

Pavlos Poulos, Katelyn Sadorf, Themos Stafylakis 等4人

Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) promise energy-efficient inference through sparse, event-driven computation, yet remain constrained on continuous, context-dependent tasks such as automatic speech recognition (ASR). A key bottleneck is the Leaky Integrate-and-Fire (LIF) neuron, whose membrane decay erases information across pauses and phonetic boundaries while it must both extract features and retain long-range context. Inspired by cortical separation of nonlinear filtering and somatic integration, we introduce the Spiking Ternary GRU (stGRU): a ternary-weight gated recurrent unit performs temporal filtering, while downstream Resonate-and-Fire (RF) neurons generate the binary spike stream driving the next block's event pathway. This makes binary event transmission a native training constraint rather than a separate deployment-time activation quantization step; together with ternary weights, learned projections avoid dense floating-point matrix MACs. Trained end-to-end on LibriSpeech-960, Small, Medium, and Large stGRUs reach 8.09%, 7.04%, and $6.44 \pm 0.17\%$ WER on test-clean without an external language model. To our knowledge, these are the first matrix-MAC-free spiking networks without a learned ANN sequence decoder to achieve single-digit WER on LibriSpeech. The architecture also achieves 10.88% test CER on Aishell-1 (9.28% on dev) when trained from scratch. Internally, trained networks develop a depth-graded hierarchy: shallow layers act as fast acoustic filters, while deep layers form slower semantic integrators whose latent representations segregate into part-of-speech manifolds and word-level spike events. Deep-layer firing rates follow lognormal distributions with a sparsity gradient, mirroring hierarchical processing observed in human cortex in vivo. Our biologically grounded architecture combines competitive ASR with favorable modeled compute and memory cost.

RubatoCodec: Budgeted Adaptive Speech Tokenization with Streaming Decoding

Chihiro Arata, Shohei Taniguchi, Gouki Minegishi 等4人

Neural speech codecs provide discrete tokens for speech generation. Lower frame rates shorten token sequences, but each token must then carry more linguistic and acoustic information. Variable-frame-rate codecs address this trade-off by adapting token allocation to speech content, but threshold-based allocation lets content determine the token count, so the sequence length a speech generator must produce varies across utterances and datasets at a fixed setting. We present RubatoCodec, a variable-frame-rate speech codec built on X-Codec-2 with an exact utterance-level event budget and streaming decoding. Its Adaptive Firing mechanism uses changes in codec features and waveform spectra to divide speech into variable-duration events without a learned boundary predictor. Discrete codes and an explicit duration represent each event, allowing a speech generator to predict and decode one event at a time. Controlled experiments show that adaptive allocation outperforms fixed-rate coding in reconstruction fidelity and intelligibility at low event rates. A model trained on large-scale multilingual speech achieves competitive reconstruction quality across English, Japanese, and Mandarin, with the best intelligibility preservation among the compared codecs on the evaluated English and Japanese benchmarks at comparable bitrates. In a matched text-to-speech comparison, the adaptive representation yields lower WER and higher UTMOS and UTMOSv2 with comparable speaker similarity. The speech generator also learns the codec's utterance-level event budget without an explicit generation-time rate constraint. These results demonstrate that budgeted adaptive speech representations support both high-quality reconstruction and streaming text-to-speech generation. Code and weights are available at https://github.com/third-intelligence/RubatoCodec and https://huggingface.co/third-intelligence/RubatoCodec.

Closed-Loop Dual-Reference Guidance for Accent Control in Cross-Lingual Voice Cloning

Yuanzhong Zheng, Wei Huang, Ming Ma 等5人

Zero-shot cross-lingual voice cloning often carries the reference speaker's accent into the target language. We propose closed-loop dual-reference guidance, which pairs the original reference with a reusable native anchor constructed by voice conversion (VC). The references initialize separate language-model (LM) states that guide a shared acoustic trajectory; each completed patch then updates both states, keeping their generated histories aligned with the emitted speech. Anchor construction runs once per speaker, and online synthesis uses a frozen TTS model without per-utterance VC. On 20 held-out French speakers, the fixed setting $\lambda^{\!}=0.33$ reduces residual French LID mass by 73\% versus direct cloning, at an ECAPA similarity cost of only $0.042$; relative to anchor-only cloning, it recovers $0.155$. The method achieves the lowest mean WER ($0.009$) and highest mean GOP ($4.750$) among the held-out test arms. In blinded preference tests, native-English listeners favor it over direct cloning on pronunciation naturalness, and over both anchor-only cloning and a TTS$\to$VC cascade on speaker identity. Online RTF is approximately 26\% lower than the cascade's ($0.59$ vs.\ $0.80$), and the same setting reduces Spanish LID mass by 56\% without re-tuning.

Decodability Is Not Controllability: Local and Anisotropic Speaker Control in Continuous-Latent TTS

Yuanzhong Zheng, Wei Huang, Ming Ma 等5人

High linear decodability does not entail causal controllability in continuous-latent text-to-speech (TTS). In VoxCPM2, position-matched probes and equal-norm interventions across 30 speakers and 60 directed dyads expose this separation: reference-audio states at the final residual acoustic language model layer (RALM L7) retain 0.970 probe accuracy, yet aligned edits yield 0/60 target-dominant dyads. Edits at early reference-audio positions produce the opposite pattern. At L0, pair-aligned updates increase target similarity by 0.326 and achieve target dominance in all 60 dyads, against at most 1/60 under any equal-norm direction or position control. Independent speaker encoders, retrieval against held-out speaker prototypes, and double-blind listening confirm that the shifted voice is heard as the intended target. Cross-architecture interventions in dots.tts distinguish backbone leverage from direct acoustic control: backbone edits displace the source without target attraction, while x-vector ablation reduces source similarity from 0.849 to 0.006. These findings identify two requirements for training-free speaker control: geometric specificity and a computational route that carries the intended speaker condition into acoustic synthesis.

KunSet: A Corpus and Benchmark for Lyrics Alignment and Rhyme Retrieval in Kunqu Opera

Lejun Min, Siqi Chen, Delong Wang 等13人

Kunqu is one of the oldest extant forms of Chinese opera, yet its digitization remains scarce and difficult to annotate, with two challenges standing out: first, close analysis of Kunqu performance demands syllable-level lyrics alignment; second, analyzing Kunqu pronunciation requires its phonological attributes (e.g., rhyme) to be linked with each syllable. To address these challenges, we present KunSet, the first Kunqu performance corpus comprising 482,164 fine-grained annotations of syllable-level lyrics and phonological attributes in total media duration of 155.2 h with 106.3 h annotated. To ensure annotation quality, KunSet was preannotated by an iterative ML-assisted pipeline and corrected by over 200 trained annotators via a purpose-built online annotation system under a two-round review process. To assess KunSet as a music information retrieval (MIR) benchmark, we evaluate two tasks: lyrics alignment, by finetuning a forced-alignment model with an acoustic anchor decoder; and same-rhyme retrieval, using frozen Chinese HuBERT features. Models built with KunSet outperform their baselines. We release KunSet and model checkpoints to support MIR research on traditional-music corpora.

How Far Can the Gemma 4 Speech Encoder Go Without Changing Its Weights? Saudi and Gulf Arabic Speech Recognition with a Frozen and an Adapter-Augmented Encoder

Amr Abdelkhalik

We study the 305M-parameter audio encoder shared by Gemma 4 and EmbeddingGemma 2 (identical except for its final projection) for Saudi and Gulf Arabic speech recognition, without changing any of its weights. A frozen system trains only a 22.6M-parameter CTC reader over a mix of encoder layers; an adapted system adds 3.1M low-rank adapter parameters, a 41.5M-parameter hybrid CTC/attention reader and joint decoding with a sub-word language model. Against Cohere Transcribe Arabic (2.07B parameters), re-run on identical audio, the frozen system trails in word error rate (WER) but is statistically indistinguishable in character error rate (CER) on five of six human-labelled conditions, while running 3.4 times faster on CPU with greedy decoding. The adapted system is statistically indistinguishable in WER on a public Saudi customer-service call (19.20% against 19.27%) and on clean Emirati speech, is significantly better on telephone-coded Emirati speech, has a significantly lower CER on all six conditions of these sets, and beats a 0.6B recogniser fine-tuned on SADA in WER on all six; it still trails Cohere in WER on long-form Saudi speech. On a blind, human-corrected public call-centre sample it ranks 4 of 15 systems, behind three commercial APIs (significantly only one) and ahead of the other self-hostable systems by point estimate. An optimised joint decoder runs it at 139 times real time on one GPU. We also show that references made by correcting machine drafts favour the drafting system. Code and public-set results are released.

NeuroCodec: EEG-Guided Latent-Domain Generative Model for High-Fidelity Target Speaker Extraction

Julian Silva, Jaliya Kodithuwakku, Navindu Sirimanna 等6人

Driven by recent advancements in hearing assistive devices, target speaker extraction (TSE) in multi-talker environments has drawn significant attention. To address this, hearing technologies identify attention using neural signals recorded via electroencephalography (EEG) to guide TSE, enabling neuro-steered auditory attention decoding. However, most existing techniques rely on time-frequency masking, which suffers from low fidelity in spectro-temporally overlapping regions and is often restricted to narrowband audio. In contrast, we introduce NeuroCodec, which, to the best of our knowledge, is the first generative EEG-guided TSE framework operating in the latent space of a neural audio codec. Specifically, NeuroCodec models spatial EEG dependencies via graph convolutions and fuses cross-modal EEG and speech representations through attention mechanisms and Mamba-based temporal modeling. We further introduce a six-phase training curriculum that progressively incorporates latent reconstruction, multi-scale spectral, and adversarial objectives to ensure stable, high-fidelity speech generation. Experiments on the public Cocktail Party and KUL datasets show NeuroCodec outperforms baselines, across several perceptual metrics. A listening experiment with 20 participants validated that NeuroCodec offers greater perceptual accuracy, achieving a mean score of 82.24 compared with 49.63 for M3ANet. Additionally, the proposed method is seen to enable 44.1 kHz reconstruction with spectral content extending up to 20 kHz, further supporting the perceptual results. Finally, we extend NeuroCodec with a causal streaming formulation suited for real-time processing, providing a basis for hearing assistive devices.

Geometry-Conditioned Target Speaker Extraction based on spatial features incorporating acoustic propagation principles

Jiatong Li, Simon Doclo

Recently, spatially selective non-linear filter (SSF) was proposed for target speaker extraction, utilizing the direction-of-arrival (DOA) of the target speaker as a spatial cue. To overcome its dependency on a fixed microphone array geometry, geometry-conditioned SSF (GCSSF) was proposed, which maps the target DOA and the microphone positions into abstract high-dimensional embeddings using positional encoding. As an alternative to these high-dimensional embeddings, in this paper we propose utilizing two spatial features incorporating acoustic propagation principles for geometry conditioning: 1) the steering vector of the target speaker, which captures inter-microphone phase differences, and 2) a spatial mask based on GCC-PHAT, which quantifies presence of the target speaker per time-frequency bin. These features are used as input to a geometry-conditioning encoder and integrated via a FiLM layer into the GCSSF system. Experimental results on circular and random arrays demonstrate that conditioning with spatial features improves target extraction performance compared to abstract high-dimensional embeddings, maintaining a clear performance advantage across a wide range of target DOA errors.

Optimized Compressor Parameterization for Produced Music in Hearing Aids

Robin Holland, Christoph Hoog Antink, Henning Puder

Hearing aid compression is typically designed for speech, yet music demands different trade-offs due to its narrower dynamic range (DR) and impulsive transients. This paper investigates whether music fitting benefits from being signal-aware, both in mapping compression demand across signal-audiogram conditions and in tuning its parameters to these combinations rather than inheriting them from speech-derived prescriptions. Compression demand is characterized across eight audiometric profiles, three residual DR severities, and three produced music signals under linear gain. Signal dynamics emerge as a first-order factor: production-compressed music fits the residual DR of the mild-to-moderate profiles evaluated, while dynamically rich recordings challenge even moderate hearing loss. Per-signal optimization of single- and multi-band wide dynamic range compression is then applied to two profiles with the highest compression demand. This optimization improves the Hearing Aid Audio Quality Index over gain-optimized NAL-NL2 baselines while also improving audibility, indicating substantial headroom for music-specific fitting.

An Absolute Acoustic Readout of Plate Wave Speed for Bullion Authentication, Using a Measured Fill-Factor Correction for Struck Coins

Sylvia Dong (Lynbrook High School), Takyiu Liu (West Valley College)

Acoustic "ping" testing of bullion is empirical: it compares a coin's ringing frequency with frequencies recorded for the same coin type, so it cannot assess a specimen absent from the reference library. This work replaces the comparison with an absolute measurement, computed from the measured resonance and geometry through a physical model rather than retrieved from a library. Free-plate flexural resonance is calibrated on five machined aluminium discs ($a/t=2.58$--$10.75$), recovering an empirical finite-thickness coefficient $\beta=0.895\pm0.047$ that reduces the worst-case departure from thin-plate theory from 8.9% to 0.75%. A fill factor, the ratio of a coin's displaced volume to its nominal cylinder, supplies the effective thickness that relief hides from calipers, extending the calibration to struck coins. The readout is the plate wave speed $c_{p}=\sqrt{E/\rho(1-\nu^{2})}$, a material constant, conditioned on an assumed Poisson's ratio. On six struck 0.999 copper rounds not used in the calibration it recovers a Young's modulus of $119.1\pm6.7$ GPa, inside the accepted 110 to 128 GPa. The recovered wave speeds form distinct observed groups across the measured specimens, with the strongest independent validation provided by these copper rounds. The $(3,0)/(2,0)$ mode ratio falls below its thin-plate value in thirteen of sixteen specimens (one-sided sign test, $p=0.011$), as finite-thickness theory predicts. Weighing displaced mass to 0.1 mg rather than 0.1 g cut per-specimen volume uncertainty from 1.6% to 30% to 0.5% to 2.6% and shrank an apparent 11% design-dependent bias to a 4.7% residual on one deeply relieved silver design. The readout is now limited by tabulated elastic constants and the assumed Poisson's ratio, not by the acoustics, which are reproducible to 0.15%.

XingChen-UniFE: A Unified Generative Speech Front-End via Complementary Acoustic-Semantic Modeling

Kelan Kuang, Qihan Hu, Jian Kang 等6人

This paper presents XingChen-UniFE, a unified large audio language model (LALM) for general speech restoration (GSR) and target speaker extraction (TSE). The proposed model employs complementary semantic and acoustic representations through a dual-stream encoder, where a self-supervised learning model provides hierarchical semantic features and a learnable acoustic branch captures fine-grained structures from complex spectra. A bidirectional cross-attention module further facilitates information exchange between the two representations. The fused representation conditions the LALM for discrete speech token generation with task and speaker information. Experiments demonstrate strong performance on both GSR and TSE and robust generalization to unseen degradation conditions.
