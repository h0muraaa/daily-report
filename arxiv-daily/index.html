Correspondence Is Not Transfer: A Pairing-Controlled Study of Clinical Audio-Metadata Alignment

Liu He, Xi Wang, Jiahong Yuan 等4人

Clinical audio models are often trained by aligning recordings with participant metadata or text derived from it. This metadata is rarely a clean disease signal, since a single profile also contains symptoms, age, sex, missing responses, and recruitment information. We examine what alignment adds to frozen audio features by varying only the audio-meta data pairing. We keep the audio backbones fixed and train only a projector under correct pairing and three shuffled conditions that progressively preserve the disease label and recorded sex. We then evaluate each projector on profile retrieval, attribute probing, and disease classification on covariate-matched test sets. On UKCOVID, correct pairing clearly improves retrieval and makes sex easier to decode, confirming that the pairing manipulation works. Most of the sex-probe gap disappears when the control also preserves recorded sex, while a small retrieval gap remains. Matched disease AUROC differences are small, and every interval includes zero, so modest benefits or harms are not ruled out. The same pattern appears when we rerun the frozen pipeline on CODA TB, Cambridge COVID-19 Sounds, and Coswara, where retrieval and sex-probe contrasts are consistently positive but disease contrasts are not. We find no consistent evidence that better profile correspondence translates into better disease prediction and suggest reporting these outcomes separately.

A Benchmark for Controllable Speaking-Style Captioning in Audio-Language Models

Shreeram Suresh Chandra, Ashwyn Sharma, Liang Lu 等7人

Speaking-style captioning (SSC) aims to generate natural language descriptions of how speech is delivered, capturing paralinguistic attributes such as vocal timbre, prosody, and expressivity. Many downstream applications, including conversational AI agents, controllable speech generation, and large-scale audio annotation, require dimension-specific style captions that describe targeted aspects of speech (e.g., speaker traits, emotion, or delivery style), rather than a single undifferentiated description. However, existing work lacks a unified formulation for controllable speaking-style captioning. We address this gap by formulating SSC as an instruction-following audio-language task, where explicit instructions specify the speaking-style dimensions to be described. We introduce StyleInstructCaps, the first standardized benchmark for controllable speaking-style captioning in audio-language models. StyleInstructCaps provides a dataset and evaluation framework that measures metadata groundedness, hallucination, instruction-following ability, generalization to unseen instructions and audio datasets, and speaker-style consistency. Across nine state-of-the-art audio-language models, we find that strong instruction-following ability does not necessarily imply grounded speaking-style understanding, and that SSC-specific instruction-diverse training substantially improves groundedness, controllability, and speaker consistency.

AzeroS: Extending LLM to Speech with Self-Generated Instruction-Free Tuning

Yiwen Shao, Wei Liu, Jiahong Li 等7人

Extending large language models (LLMs) to the speech domain has recently gained significant attention. A typical approach connects a pretrained LLM with an audio encoder through a projection module and trains the resulting model on large-scale, task-specific instruction-tuning datasets. However, curating such instruction-tuning data for specific requirements is time-consuming, and models trained in this manner often generalize poorly to unseen tasks. In this work, we first formulate that the strongest generalization of a speech-LLM is achieved when it is trained with Self-Generated Instruction-Free Tuning (SIFT), in which supervision signals are generated by a frozen LLM using textual representations of speech as input. Our proposed SIFT paradigm eliminates the need for collecting task-specific question-answer pairs and yields the theoretically best generalization to unseen tasks. Building upon this paradigm, we introduce AZeroS (Auden Zero-instruction-tuned Speech-LLM), which is trained on speech-text pairs derived from publicly available corpora, including approximately 25,000 hours of speech with ASR transcripts and 3,000 hours of speech with paralinguistic labels. Built upon Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, the model updates only two lightweight projection modules (23.8 million parameters each), while keeping both the LLM and audio encoders frozen. Despite the minimal training cost and modest data scale, AZeroS achieves state-of-the-art performance on both semantic and paralinguistic benchmarks, including VoiceBench, AIR-Bench Foundation (Speech), and AIR-Bench Chat (Speech).

Sonifying I2S Transport Signals to Detect Transmission Faults

Stephen Roddy

This paper outlines a sonification design to support fault detection in the transmission of I2S transport signals. I2S is a protocol for communicating real-time digital audio between integrated circuits that, while in wide and general use, does not include built-in error detection. Moreover, given the nature of the protocol transmission faults affecting timing, framing and alignment can be difficult to identify using conventional visual methods. The proposed design addresses this with an approach informed by Audification, wherein oversampling controls temporal rescaling to render protocol structure (SCK and WS) and payload data (SD) across separate stereo channels. A preliminary computational feasibility study was carried out to measure feature-space separability of I2S faults in the generated auditory representations as opposed to listener performance. It evaluates the design across several payload types and error conditions including jitter, bit-slip, and word-length errors. Class separability was assessed through clustering analyses of extracted features. The evaluation results show that while oversampling produces systematic changes in feature values, it does not meaningfully improve separability between error classes. However, a modest but consistent improvement in separability is observed as a function of the joint representation of structural and payload information across channels. The findings suggest that feature-space separability in sonified communication protocol data may be dependent on the integration of complementary information streams, rather than on signal scaling alone.

Integrated Spoofing-Robust Automatic Speaker Verification via a Three-Class Formulation and LLR

Kai Tan, Lin Zhang, Ruiteng Zhang 等9人

Spoofing-robust automatic speaker verification (SASV) aims to integrate automatic speaker verification (ASV) and countermeasure (CM). A popular solution is fusion of independent ASV and CM scores. To better modeling SASV, some frameworks integrate ASV and CM within a single network. However, these solutions are typically bi-encoder based, offer limited interpretability, and cannot be readily adapted to new evaluation parameters without retraining. Based on this, we propose a unified end-to-end framework via a three-class formulation that enables log-likelihood ratio (LLR) inference from class logits for a more interpretable decision pipeline. Experiments show comparable performance to existing methods on ASVSpoof5 and better results on SpoofCeleb. The visualization and analysis also prove that the three-class reformulation provides more interpretability.

Partially Edited Audio: Generation, Defense, and Open Problems

Lin Zhang, You Zhang, Nicholas Evans 等10人

Recent advances in generative audio models facilitate increasingly flexible and selective editing of linguistic content and paralinguistic, extralinguistic, and other attributes. This paradigm, named partially edited audio, differs from full audio generation as only portions of the real recording are edited. This technique can serve both beneficial and harmful applications. We therefore present a concise survey from both generation and defense perspectives, focusing on audio, as a contribution to the ICASSP 2027 special session entitled "Partially Edited Media: Perspectives from Synthesis and Defense." We first define partially edited audio and argue that what counts as ``fake'' or harmful is an application-dependent judgment, not a fixed property of the edit. We then survey audio editing by editing target, approaches, and evaluation, and then defense methods by detection, localization, diarization, and interpretability, summarizing representative benchmarks and toolkits. Finally, we discuss future directions and open problems.

Developing a Speech Language Model for Basque

Andoni Sudupe, Inma Hernaez, Ander Arriandiaga 等6人

Although the recent development of LLMs has paved the way for multi-modal interaction, minority languages face difficulties keeping pace. This work presents the first open Speech Language Model for Basque, designed for low-latency, end-to-end spoken interaction. Based on the LLaMA-Omni architecture, the system integrates a Latxa model as its backbone, transferring its Basque capabilities to the spoken modality by fine-tuning several modules. To address data scarcity, the English VoiceAssistant-400K corpus was translated into Basque, and the audio was synthesized using a Basque TTS. Evaluation results confirm the system's viability, achieving 3.86/5 in content quality, a 9.94% WER for spoken intelligibility, and a 3.07 UTMOS for audio quality. This work demonstrates that advanced spoken interaction systems can be developed using modular architectures and synthetic data even in low-resource languages.

SMEBENCH: Benchmarking Neural Music Editing with Synthetized MIDI Edits

Matthieu Cervera, Francesco Paissan, Micael Antunes 等6人

Neural music editing requires a system to perform a requested change while preserving the musical content that should remain unchanged. Existing evaluations often lack a paired target edit, making these requirements difficult to measure separately. We introduce SMEBench, a large-scale benchmark of controlled music edits constructed by modifying symbolic MIDI performances and rendering matched source and target audio. Each example contains a source, an instruction, a paired target, edit metadata, and complementary descriptions. The benchmark covers twelve instrumentation, articulation, dynamics, and pitch operations and includes a balanced 3,600-example test set together with a smaller real-audio complement derived from multitrack recordings. The paired targets allow source preservation, target agreement, and textual compatibility to be evaluated independently. We use these references to diagnose common audio-editing metrics and benchmark six representative editing systems, showing that the metric used to rank a system can materially change the conclusion. The dataset is available at our repository.

From Voiceprints to User Entities: Online Speaker-Memory Construction for Multi-User Spoken Agents

Yucong Zhang, Cancan Li, Bo Xiao 等5人

Personalized spoken agents must identify speakers before accessing user-specific memory, yet memory benchmarks typically assume correctly attributed histories. We argue that voiceprints are observations of identity, not identities themselves, and formulate online speaker-memory construction to maintain persistent user entities as acoustic evidence evolves. We introduce EvoSpeakerMem, a training-free layer for causal speaker routing, selective acoustic-state updates, and identity-partitioned memory writes and retrieval through a replaceable text-memory backend. We also construct LoCoMo-ID, a spoken-memory benchmark with hidden speaker identities, speaker-sensitive queries, simulated recording-condition shifts, and four-speaker identity-confusion tests. On longitudinal SMIIP-TV, genuine-probe rejection under a frozen near-enrollment threshold rises from 29.8% to 55.6% over approximately one year. On LoCoMo-ID speaker-sensitive queries, gated updating improves identity-gated QA over fixed representations by 3.86 and 11.62 percentage points under clean and shifted conditions, respectively, with no wrong-user authorizations. The advantage persists in the four-speaker test. Yet reducing fragmentation and improving QA can coexist with incorrect cross-user links, revealing a fragmentation--contamination trade-off that QA alone cannot capture.

StreamSE: Neural Audio Codec-based Streaming Speech Enhancement for On-device Inference

Hyunjin Choi, Zinu Kim, Junwon Moon 等6人

Recent generative speech enhancement (SE) models achieve high perceptual quality, but their non-causal processing and expensive computation make them difficult to use for streaming, on-device inference. We propose StreamSE, a 20M-parameter streaming SE model that operates in the latent space of a frozen pretrained neural audio codec. At each frame, StreamSE directly regresses a clean continuous latent from the noisy continuous latent while conditioning on its own predictions from previous frames. Enhancement therefore requires a single forward pass per frame and no iterative sampling or look-ahead. We further truncate the codec's residual vector quantizer (RVQ) to its coarse levels, halving the number of tokens per frame while improving robustness to reverberation. StreamSE is the only streaming generative model in our comparison, yet it matches or exceeds the perceptual quality of the offline generative baselines. Furthermore, the complete StreamSE pipeline runs in real time on a single smartphone CPU core. Our results show that continuous latent regression provides a practical route to generative SE under fully causal, on-device streaming constraints.

Perceptual Differences and Preferences in Speech Enhancement

Simon Dahl Jepsen, Jesper Rindom Jensen

Speech enhancement (SE) systems are commonly evaluated with objective metrics, with and without references, and with categorical subjective listening tests, all measuring speech quality and intelligibility. So far, it is unclear from such evaluations whether the processed speech is perceptually different or is preferred by everyday listeners. We investigate discrimination and preference as complementary dimensions of SE evaluation through crowdsourced triangle and pairwise preference tests across four SE systems, with comparisons to clean references. Results show that both listeners' ability to distinguish and whether they exhibit systematic preference vary across systems and substantially across acoustic conditions. Pairwise evaluations of objective metrics showed variable correlation with both discriminability and preference, motivating further studies into these novel aspects in speech enhancement evaluation.

A Lightweight Dual-Branch Visual Front-End for Efficient Audio-Visual Target Speaker Extraction

Zhan Jin, Peijun Yang, Juan Liu 等4人

Audio-Visual Target Speaker Extraction (AVTSE) uses face-related visual cues to extract a target speaker from a speech mixture. In many AVTSE systems, visual features are obtained from convolutional image encoders, and their computation may become a non-negligible part of the overall system when lightweight separation backbones are used. This work proposes a dual-branch visual front-end that reorganizes visual information according to its spatial characteristics. A compact lip region of interest is used to model local articulation cues through convolutional processing, while facial landmark coordinates are used to represent global geometric motion with a lightweight temporal convolutional network. The two representations are integrated into IIANet and SpatialNet with limited fusion overhead. Experiments on LRS3-2mix under a unified reduced training protocol show that the proposed method achieves a performance-computation trade-off close to the lightweight image-based baseline. Compared with the ResNet18-based visual encoder, it reduces the module-level visual encoding computation by more than 90% across both evaluated separation backbones, with a moderate performance gap. This comparison excludes face detection and landmark estimation and therefore applies to systems where landmark coordinates are already available from preprocessing. Demos are available at https://anonymous.4open.science/r/Lightweight-Visual-Frontend-E46E.
