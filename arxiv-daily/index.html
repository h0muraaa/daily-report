CLRM: Cross-Layer Relation Modeling for Audio Deepfake Detection

Yangyang Qu, Siqing Qin, Kong Aik Lee 等4人

Audio deepfake detection (ADD) aims to distinguish bonafide speech from synthetic or manipulated speech. Representations from different layers of self-supervised learning (SSL) speech encoders vary in their ability to distinguish bonafide from spoofed speech. Comparing representations of the same utterance across encoder depths may reveal useful spoofing cues, yet common layer-selection and aggregation methods do not provide these comparisons as detection inputs. We propose Cross-Layer Relation Modeling (\ours{}), which explicitly encodes cross-layer relations alongside layer-specific features, making both directly available for ADD. CLRM reuses the existing detector's SSL encoder and identifies informative layer pairs by combining speaker-relative representation geometry with source-domain detection performance. The resulting relation score is fused with the detector's original score, requiring only the training of compact relation heads and fusion parameters while keeping the existing detector frozen. Across five external datasets, CLRM reduces the mean equal error rate (EER) for all three evaluated detectors, achieving relative reductions of 7.8--16.7%. Controlled comparisons with identical layer summaries and approximately matched auxiliary-head parameter counts also show a lower mean EER for explicit relations than direct concatenation, both before and after score fusion. These findings support explicit cross-layer comparisons as useful detection inputs for SSL-based audio deepfake detectors.

SonoDyno: A Sonic Dynamometer for Engine Speed and Load Estimation from Audio

Robin Doerfler, Beat Rossmy

Controllable engine sound synthesis, Active Sound Design and engine order analysis all build on the relationship between the sound of an engine and its momentary operating state: its speed and its load. Recordings of engine sound are abundant in archives, sample packs and online video, yet they rarely carry time-aligned annotations of that state, which limits their applicability to controllable engine sound generation. We present SonoDyno, which estimates the engine speed, the cylinder count and an engine-normalized load intensity from a microphone signal alone, with no tachometer, no bus access and no prior knowledge of the engine configuration. A convolutional scorer of 124,302 parameters reads the harmonic order energy of each frame at three time-frequency resolutions to jointly score cylinder count and speed, and a decoder imposes the rotational dynamics of the engine on the speed trajectory. A second head estimates the load intensity, the position of the torque between the engine's drag floor at 0 and its full-load ceiling at 1, from the order levels at the decoded speed. On recordings of engines it has never heard, with microphone position, engine type and cylinder count unknown, SonoDyno places 93 to 97% of the frames within 2% of the true speed, identifies the cylinder count with 80% accuracy and confuses the octave on fewer than 2% of the frames. On an unseen engine of a known type, the load intensity has a mean absolute error of 0.12, and the sign of the torque, driving or braking, is right on 91% of the frames. General pitch trackers fall behind even when the harmonic order of each engine is supplied: on our validation recordings and at a tolerance of 5%, the best of them places 62% of the frames within the tolerance, against 96% for SonoDyno.

Virtual Microphone Array for Direction-of-Arrival Estimation Based on Fiber-Optic Position Sensing

Sebastian Schwarze, Jiekai Wang, Thomas Kissinger 等4人

This paper presents a virtual microphone array approach for estimating the direction of arrival of a sound source using a moving microphone and a stationary reference microphone. The method exploits the microphone motion to generate a time-varying array geometry, using fiber-optic position sensing to reconstruct the trajectory and determine the resulting virtual microphone configurations. For each configuration, the estimated time difference of arrival between the two microphone signals and the corresponding microphone geometry define a hyperboloid representing all possible sound source positions consistent with the time difference. By combining multiple hyperboloid constraints, the direction of arrival can be estimated in azimuth and elevation relative to the microphone coordinate system. In contrast to conventional arrays with fixed geometries and approaches based on predefined microphone trajectories, the proposed method derives the virtual array geometry directly from the measured microphone motion, enabling a spatially distributed virtual array to be formed using only two physical microphones and offering potential for size- and weight-constrained applications. The approach was experimentally evaluated under controlled low-reflection conditions for different source positions, source distances, and signal characteristics. The influence of microphone motion was further examined, and the method was tested with a moving sound source. The results demonstrate the feasibility of direction-of-arrival estimation using a virtual microphone array without requiring a predefined microphone trajectory.

Speaker-Consistent Grounding for Confusion-Resilient LLM-Based Target Speech Extraction

Hanyu Meng, Hao Shi, Zhanhong He 等8人

Deterministic target-speaker extraction (TSE) tends to preserve observed linguistic content but may extract the interfering speaker. In contrast, autoregressive generative TSE improves perceptual quality, but is prone to content hallucination and speaker identity drift, which can be mitigated by model grounding with the evidence from the deterministic TSE. However, grounding such a generative TSE model with speaker-confused evidence may therefore reinforce the error. To address this gap, we proposed a repair-before-grounding framework with a training-free evidence-repair stage. Conditioning-diverse candidate selection (CDCS) selects target speaker-consistent evidence from complementary frozen TSE outputs. The selected waveform can then be used by code-space grounding (CSG) to constrain LLM-based TSE decoding. On Libri2Mix, CDCS reduces wrong-speaker extraction errors from 6.87% to 0.75% on clean mixtures and from 18.95% to 10.46% under noise. With the LLM-based TSE checkpoint unchanged, CSG further reduces noisy content- and acoustic-switch rates from 12.79% and 1.68% to 9.10% and 0.51%, respectively. Despite this stronger grounding, generative TSE retains its predicted perceptual-quality advantage over direct deterministic extraction. These results show that repairing evidence identity before grounding improves the reliability of generative TSE. Audio demos and code are available at https://hanyu-meng.github.io/LLM-TSE-Grounding-Demo/.

The Hearpiece Database of Binaural Room Impulse Responses and Driver Responses on KEMAR

Tong Xiao, Reinhild Roden, Matthias Blau 等4人

This document describes the measurement setup, signal processing, data organization, and MATLAB file format of a database of acoustic responses measured on a Knowles Electronics Manikin for Acoustic Research (KEMAR) using the Hearpiece, a multi-microphone, multi-driver in-ear research device. The data include binaural room impulse responses (BRIRs) and Hearpiece driver responses, together with center-microphone room impulse responses and a loopback impulse response of the playback and recording system. The BRIRs cover two Hearpiece configurations, three room conditions, three independent insertions per configuration, and 24 horizontal source directions. The database supports reproducible research on binaural and hearing-device signal processing, with potential applications in speech enhancement, spatial filtering and source separation, dereverberation, active noise and occlusion control, and acoustic feedback cancellation. The database, MATLAB loading script, and technical documentation are publicly available at https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23667308.

SAUCE: Summary Analysis Using Conversation Entailment

Man-Ling Sung, Hemanth Kandula, Jeff Ma 等5人

With the growing need for evaluating Large Language Models (LLMs) and their applications to speech, challenges persist in summarizing and evaluating conversations that lack a clear end goal. We introduce SAUCE -- a reference-free, fact-based evaluation pipeline for cross-lingual conversational speech summarization. It measures the accuracy and the fact coverage of a summary through the entailment between conversation and text. We compare SAUCE against several popular summarization metrics and demonstrate the effectiveness of capturing information loss due to transcription and translation error and identifying broken summaries. Crucially, unlike black-box LLM evaluators or dense embedding metrics, SAUCE is inherently explainable: it maps summary scores to discrete, verifiable facts, allowing users to pinpoint exact hallucinations or omissions. We illustrate how this interpretability helps developers systematically profile LLM behaviors and gives end-users an actionable tool to verify summary accuracy in noisy, real-world conditions. Preliminary investigations show SAUCE strongly align with human judgment.

ProCraft: Instruction-Driven Music Production from Free-Form Text

Yuan-Chiao Cheng, Yi-Hsuan Yang, Vincent K. M. Cheung

Music production typically involves multiple editing operations that each require specialized tools and expertise. Existing text-guided music editing systems support few operations or instruments, operate on short clips, or handle only one operation per instruction, unlike real workflows where several changes are requested at once. Here, we present ProCraft, a lightweight 0.5B-parameter autoregressive codec language model that edits 30s music excerpts end-to-end from free-form instructions, including compound requests. ProCraft supports seven operations across eight instruments, from tasks with a unique target (e.g., track removal) to creative ones (e.g., accompaniment generation). We align intermediate representations with pretrained music embeddings and pitch and instrument targets to preserve content outside the edit, and gated cross-attention in every decoder layer to keep generation tied to the instruction. To train on compound requests, we introduce On-the-fly Editing-pair Synthesis, which generates paired instructions and audio combining track-level operations with effect changes. ProCraft performs competitively against task-specific systems on single-task editing, and outperforms four free-text editors on compound instructions for track removal, extraction and denoising on real (MoisesDB) and synthetic (Slakh) recordings. In a user study (16 listeners), ProCraft was rated highest on removal and denoising and on-par with a 16-times larger model on extraction and instrument transfer. Ablations further show that representation alignment and cross-attention reduce leakage from unwanted tracks and raise editing success rates, especially on out-of-domain data.

A Unified Multi-Encoder Framework for Music Lyric Intelligibility Assessment: Bridging Invasive and Non-Invasive Paradigms

Jing Yang, Shenghao Liao, Shuqing Zhang 等7人

Lyric intelligibility significantly impacts music engagement and comprehension, especially for hearing-impaired individuals. Existing speech intelligibility metrics perform poorly in musical contexts due to differences in prosody, timing, and spectral dynamics. We propose a unified multi-encoder framework that supports both invasive and non-invasive modeling for robust lyric intelligibility prediction. By capturing complementary local acoustic-semantic alignments, global musical semantics, and ASR-derived textual cues, our method achieves superior performance over baselines on the ICASSP 2026 Cadenza dataset, with the best model achieving 26.61 RMSE, a $9.2$\% improvement over the baseline, demonstrating its flexibility and robustness.

FUSEMOS: Perceptual Evaluation of Text-to-Music Generation with Dual-Encoder Fusion and Ranking-Aware Composite Loss

Jing Yang, Haoyu Wang, Ningning Pan 等6人

Perceptual evaluation of generated music is essential for developing text-to-music (TTM) generation systems. Existing automatic music perceptual evaluation methods rely solely on the contrastive language audio pretraining (CLAP) model to extract both audio and text features for predicting human-annotated mean opinion scores (MOS), particularly for overall musical impression (OMI) and textual alignment (TA). However, CLAP is limited in capturing fine-grained musical attributes such as timbre and expressiveness \emph{etc}. To address this, we propose FUSEMOS, a dual-encoder fusion framework that integrates CLAP with MERT, a self-supervised model pretrained on large scale music audio, to better capture both high level semantic alignment and detailed musical characteristics for more accurate OMI and TA estimation. Furthermore, to overcome the limitations of conventional regression losses in measuring human preferred relative ranking of music segments, we design a novel ranking-aware composite loss function. Experiments conducted on the MusicEval benchmark demonstrate that our approach outperforms the existing SOTA methods in both mean squared error and rank correlation metrics by a large margin.

Auditory and Visual Virtualization in 6DoF XR: Effects on Plausibility and Dynamic Auditory Distance Perception

Johannes M. Arend, Christian Scheer, Melissa Ramírez 等5人

Extended reality (XR) enables controlled yet naturalistic multisensory experiments, but its validity depends on whether virtualized cues are perceptually plausible and lead to behavior comparable to the real world. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to perceptually evaluate a six-degree-of-freedom (6DoF) XR system that combines real-time room acoustic simulation, binaural rendering, and an audio-visually matched virtual replica of a large laboratory room in terms of both plausibility and behavior. In two experiments with 45 participants, we investigated how auditory and visual virtualization affect plausibility and dynamic auditory distance perception (ADP). A plausibility experiment compared real and virtual sound sources in audio-augmented reality (AAR) and virtual reality (VR), while a locomotion-based ADP experiment assessed distance-judgment accuracy, signed error, and task effort across four virtualization conditions. The system produced plausible binaural rendering in both AAR and VR, with higher plausibility in VR, indicating that perceptual requirements for XR audio depend on the visual reference frame. In the ADP task, any form of virtualization reduced accuracy and increased effort, although performance remained high overall. Virtual audio produced a bias toward nearer perceived source distances, whereas visual-only virtualization reduced accuracy without a significant directional bias. These effects did not add up in full VR. Overall, the findings demonstrate the strong potential of current 6DoF XR systems for interactive perceptual research, while revealing that task-specific behavioral validation may be necessary.

Joint Higher-Order Low-Rank Tensor Decompositions for Multichannel Acoustic System Compression

Matthias Blochberger, Filip Elvander, Jan Østergaard 等4人

Multichannel room impulse responses (RIRs) in acoustics are often long and therefore the sample count becomes large if seen as finite impulse response (FIR) filters, making storage and real-time convolution costly. Pole-zero (PZ) models are an established way to represent RIRs compactly as infinite impulse response (IIR) transfer functions. We show that a PZ model implies a low-rank canonical polyadic decomposition (CPD) of tensorized RIRs: the early reflections form a sparse rank-1 sum, while the late reverberation admits a CPD with factors determined by the poles. This motivates higher-order-tensor-based compression of RIRs via the block term decomposition (BTD), a generalization of the CPD. We propose two multichannel compression schemes: (i) a stacked multichannel block term decomposition (SMCBTD) applied to a (D+1)-mode tensor of RIRs, and (ii) a joint multichannel block term decomposition (JMCBTD) that shares subsets of factors across channels discovered by spectral clustering on factor-subspace affinity graphs. Both are optimized by an alternating higher-order orthogonal iteration (HOOI) procedure. On simulated rooms as well as measured datasets, the proposed methods achieve consistently lower reconstruction error at equal compression ratios compared to separate single-channel decompositions and a generalized low-rank approximation of matrices (GLRAM) baseline. The largest gains occur with scalar cores (i.e., CPD), supporting the PZ-to-CPD modelling link. We also find D = 3 to be a practical sweet spot, offering most of the achievable gain at moderate compute/memory cost. The compressed models enable efficient downstream multichannel convolution.

SubRoformer: Subband Time-Domain Representation for Music Source Separation

Chunyat Wu, Chuang Ding, Paris Smaragdis 等4人

Music source separation extracts vocals and instruments from a mixed recording, enabling remixing, content production, and music analysis. Existing systems commonly operate on waveforms or full-band spectrograms. Waveform inputs contain many samples even in short segments, whereas a single spectrogram resolution imposes a shared time-frequency resolution trade-off across frequency ranges. We propose SubRoFormer, a Transformer-based system comprising a subband module and a residual RoFormer module. The subband module decomposes the mixture into frequency-localized, lower-rate signals for separation. It then reconstructs the target waveforms from the estimated subbands. The residual RoFormer module models temporal and cross-band relationships on temporally compressed features. It combines this context with fine-rate features through gated residual fusion to estimate source-specific complex masks. On the MUSDB18-HQ test set, training only on MUSDB18-HQ yields an average signal-to-distortion ratio (SDR) of 10.63 dB across four stems using target-specific models, achieving state-of-the-art performance among the compared systems without extra training data. With 500 additional training songs, a single model jointly separating all four stems achieves an average SDR of 11.59 dB, establishing state-of-the-art performance among the compared single-model multi-stem systems trained with extra data. Code and checkpoints are released at https://github.com/ArchiMickey/SubRoformer.

Improving the Clinical Generalizability of Machine Learning-Based Alzheimer's Disease Detection Across Domains and Modalities: A Comprehensive Review

Yingchao Huang, Yuhan Su, Xin Wang 等4人

Objective: This review aims to provide a comprehensive synthesis of domain adaptation (DA) and domain general- ization (DG) strategies for AD detection. It focuses on their potential to improve the robustness, generalizability, and clinical reliability of ML-based diagnostic systems. Methods: We conduct a comprehensive scoping review of existing DA and DG approaches across multiple data modalities. The reviewed studies are organized according to data modality, algorithmic framework, adaptation strat- egy, clinical relevance, and reported cross-domain performance. Key methodological trends, performance gains, limitations, and remaining challenges are analyzed to identify future research directions. Results: DA and DG approaches have demonstrated encouraging improvements in cross-domain performance and model robustness. Emerging modalities, such as speech-based approaches, EEG and eye-tracking data, show great po- tential for early, accessible, and non-invasive cognitive assessments. However, important limitations remain, including insufficient external validation, limited interpretability, computational complexity, privacy concerns, and challenges in scaling to routine clinical practice. Conclusion: DA and DG provide important methodological pathways for developing more generalizable ML-based systems for AD detection. Future research should prioritize explainable, computationally efficient, privacy-preserving, and clinically validated DA/DG techniques. Strengthening these aspects will be essential for translating robust AI- based AD detection tools into clinically deployable solutions.

VoxCanvas-41M: A Large-Scale Bilingual Dataset for Instruction-Based Speech Editing and Its Learning Dynamics

Zhikang Niu, Wenming Tu, Yalin Li 等11人

Recent advances in speech generation have spurred the development of speech editing techniques. However, achieving high-quality speech editing remains challenging due to two key limitations. First, the major hurdle is the lack of large-scale, high-quality speech editing datasets. Constructing such datasets is costly, while open-source, scalable data pipelines remain limited. Second, the learning dynamics of speech editing models remain poorly understood. In particular, how models acquire editing capabilities and the relationship between generation and editing remain unclear. To address these challenges, we introduce VoxCanvas-41M, a large-scale bilingual dataset comprising approximately 41 million speech editing pairs. VoxCanvas-41M covers two major categories: content editing, including single-span, multi-span, and location-reference editing, and paralinguistic editing, covering the insertion and deletion of 39 classes of nonverbal events such as laughter, sighs, and coughs. Building on VoxCanvas-41M, we train VoxCanvas-Edit, an instruction-based speech editing model, demonstrating the effectiveness of our dataset in improving editing performance. Furthermore, we systematically investigate the learning dynamics of speech editing, examining how models learn different editing operations and how data scale, task interactions, and training strategies affect editing performance. The dataset, pipeline, and demos are publicly available.
