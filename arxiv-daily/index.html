VoxCanvas-41M: A Large-Scale Bilingual Dataset for Instruction-Based Speech Editing and Its Learning Dynamics

Zhikang Niu, Wenming Tu, Yalin Li 等11人

Recent advances in speech generation have spurred the development of speech editing techniques. However, achieving high-quality speech editing remains challenging due to two key limitations. First, the major hurdle is the lack of large-scale, high-quality speech editing datasets. Constructing such datasets is costly, while open-source, scalable data pipelines remain limited. Second, the learning dynamics of speech editing models remain poorly understood. In particular, how models acquire editing capabilities and the relationship between generation and editing remain unclear. To address these challenges, we introduce VoxCanvas-41M, a large-scale bilingual dataset comprising approximately 41 million speech editing pairs. VoxCanvas-41M covers two major categories: content editing, including single-span, multi-span, and location-reference editing, and paralinguistic editing, covering the insertion and deletion of 39 classes of nonverbal events such as laughter, sighs, and coughs. Building on VoxCanvas-41M, we train VoxCanvas-Edit, an instruction-based speech editing model, demonstrating the effectiveness of our dataset in improving editing performance. Furthermore, we systematically investigate the learning dynamics of speech editing, examining how models learn different editing operations and how data scale, task interactions, and training strategies affect editing performance. The dataset, pipeline, and demos are publicly available.

TOMORI: A Tool-Orchestrated Mixing Operator for REAPER Interaction

Yining Wang, Tianjiao Li

Code-based agents can operate creative software, but mixing requests often describe an intended sound without specifying the edits needed to achieve it. We present TOMORI, a code-based mixing-agent harness for native digital audio workstation (DAW) control. Custom plugins provide REAPER project inspection, optional audio measurement, and execution of model-generated Lua code. All observations return as text; delivery includes an editable project and rendered audio. TOMORI-BENCH pairs 110 human-reviewed reference edits with three instruction forms and evaluates native DAW execution end to end through operation structure, parameter precision, and rendered-audio outcomes. Across 990 cases from three primary models, explicit instructions yield high reference agreement, while abstract requests reduce project agreement and audio improvement. Paired ablations show that the full workflow improves explicit execution over a single submission. Prompted audio measurement succeeds in all 28 selected abstract cases but yields no clear overall improvement on the reference-based metrics and increases tool calls. These results expose the gap between executable DAW control and precise realization of perceptual mixing goals.

Encoding of musical structures in hidden units of restricted Boltzmann machines

Mutsumi Kobayashi, Hiroshi Watanabe

Restricted Boltzmann machines (RBMs) are energy-based models originating from statistical physics. In this study, we investigate how musical structures are represented in the hidden layer of a Bernoulli-Bernoulli RBM trained on binary piano-roll representations of J.~S.~Bach's music. By examining the visible patterns induced by individual hidden units, we find that the hidden layer primarily captures local temporal and pitch-statistical features rather than explicit musical concepts such as melodies, chords, or keys. Free-energy comparisons show that the trained RBM assigns higher probabilities to the examined musical inputs than to digit or noise patterns. Analysis using t-SNE, cosine similarity, and PCA reveals that hidden representations are sensitive to absolute pitch and input overlap, with transposed versions of the same piece not necessarily mapped close together. Generated samples exhibit local pitch organization but limited long-range coherence. These findings illustrate how a simple statistical-physics model captures certain regularities in structured musical data while highlighting its limitations in representing higher-level musical relationships.

Integrate the Reward, Don't Sample It: Window Likelihood for Interruptions and Backchannels in Full-Duplex Speech Models

Guoqiang Hu, Lichao Zhang, Yizhou Peng 等8人

When a user talks over a full-duplex spoken dialogue model, the model must decide whether the user is interrupting (stop) or only backchannelling (carry on). Post-training can make the model better at telling the two apart, or merely change how readily it stops. The whole-rollout GRPO runs we examine never lower both error rates: any gain in discrimination is offset by a threshold shift, and two released checkpoints discriminate no better. We trace this partly to how the reward is used. The probability that the model stops inside a short window is a known function of a few stop probabilities that the policy itself emits, yet GRPO estimates it by sampling. A window on which all rollouts agree then gives that update no gradient, even when all fail, and whole-rollout credit reaches decisions the reward never judges. Window likelihood (WL) instead samples a continuation and integrates over its stop decisions: the probability of the correct outcome is computed in closed form and its logarithm maximised. On HumDial-FDBench, across four open models with different stop interfaces, WL gives the best F1 and lowers both error rates beyond their confidence intervals, which no GRPO run does, without adding talking into silence.

REAL-2MIX: Evaluating Identity Faithfulness in Audio-Visual Target Speaker Extraction

Peijun Yang, Zhan Jin, Xiaoyi Qin 等7人

Audio-visual target speaker extraction should recover the person indicated by the video, rather than repeatedly return the acoustically dominant voice. We introduce REAL-2MIX, a Mandarin benchmark of jointly recorded two-speaker mixtures with synchronized multi-view video and scene-matched solo references. Its 77 scenes contain 7,598 clips; filtering yields 6,038 evaluable mixtures, each queried for both speakers, giving 12,076 paired target queries. We also curate VOXBLINK2-AVSE, comprising 250,828 synchronized audio--lip pairs. A seven-system TF-GridNet study examines visual representations, training corpora, and conditioning strategies. We evaluate transcription accuracy, speaker identity, residual overlap, and perceptual quality. The AVSR early-fusion system achieves 22.99% CER, and 93.38% of its outputs are more similar to the target speaker than to the interferer. Yet only 42.49% satisfy both this identity criterion and the overlap detector's single-speaker criterion; both target queries satisfy these criteria for just 20.77% of mixtures. A separate Real-AVSE challenge study examines development-remix adaptation. The benchmark reveals that favorable content and identity scores need not coincide with low detected overlap, motivating paired-target evaluation.

Bridging Spiking and Continuous Dynamics in Spiking Neural Networks: A Muscle-Inspired Model

Maryam Soltani, Philip N. Garner

Transforming the spike trains produced by spiking neural networks (SNNs) into continuous signals through biologically inspired mechanisms remains an open challenge in generative and regression tasks. In biological systems, a similar transformation occurs when muscles integrate neural spikes to produce smooth, coordinated movements. Inspired by this mechanism, we introduce a muscle-response model, a biologically motivated dynamical layer that converts spike trains into continuous representations. Our approach uses second-order dynamics to model the temporal integration of spiking activity. We investigate how the number of spiking neurons and temporal resolution affect the quality of the generated signals in a text-to-speech task, showing that increasing representational capacity has a greater impact than increasing temporal resolution under the evaluated configurations. Furthermore, an analysis of the learned dynamics reveals that, without explicit constraints on the response type, most neurons converge toward non-oscillatory behavior. These results demonstrate the potential of biologically inspired dynamical decoders for bridging spiking neural computation and continuous signal generation.

SPDMX: Large-Scale Open Stem Audio From Symbolic Music

Phillip Long, Eduardo Escoto, Julian McAuley 等4人

Large public datasets have driven recent progress in machine learning, from text and vision to speech and general audio. Music remains an outlier, as copyright blocks redistribution of training audio, while many modern tasks need individual stems rather than mixtures. Existing multitrack resources trade off realism, scale, and licensing, while synthetic alternatives rely on proprietary VSTs or naive open rendering that yields weak stems. We introduce SPDMX, a listening-guided rendering of public-domain PDMX scores into an openly-licensed stem corpus with aligned scores and an open render pipeline: $253{,}982$ songs, $490{,}159$ mono stems, and $5{,}469$ song-hours at $44.1$ kHz under CC BY 4.0, with paired MIDI and identifiers linking to PDMX metadata. $88{,}403$ songs ($34.8\%$; $2{,}734$ song-hours) provide multitrack supervision. To our knowledge, this is the largest openly-licensed synthetic multitrack subset by song-hours, enabling source separation, stem-conditioned synthesis, MIDI-to-audio, transcription, and joint symbolic-audio modeling. We release the corpus, metadata, and open pipeline for reuse on other symbolic collections. Demos and experimental notes are on the project page.

AircraftWitness: How Often Aircraft Can Locate Recordings

Kris Atallah (New York University, New York, USA)

Aircraft overhead can reveal where an outdoor recording was made. Propellers and rotors leave Doppler curves in audio, and public ADS-B archives log equipped aircraft. We measure how often, blind and against chance. Our main test was registered and timestamped before any audio, and its two amendments came before any answer was read. It covers 24 new places, with the recorders' own clocks, a 300 s slack and a 20 km square. The best of three locators places clear-pass clips within 1 km at 4 places, where a uniform guess predicts 0.57 (p = 0.0065 after correcting for the pick). With the wrong time or swapped audio it places none. The other two hit 1 and 2. On 360 clips from 7 places of a GPS-timed network, two locators place 6% and 8% within 1 km. By place, a unit chosen after that run, they hit 4 and 5 of 7 where a uniform guess predicts 2.3. On 1,600 random public recordings, picked and cut without the truth in a time-bounded run, the three locators answer at most 20 times and place at most 2. We release AircraftWitness, a benchmark, and pass labels for 11.7 million recordings.

NAVE: A Normalized, Adaptive Conformer for Antarctic Whale Vocalization Detection

Matthias Nagl, Paul Primus, Gerhard Widmer

Antarctic whale vocalization detection requires temporally localizing call boundaries in long underwater recordings and classifying them into predefined call types. Training reliable machine learning models for this task is difficult due to the variability and sparsity of whale calls in passive acoustic monitoring datasets. These difficulties are further amplified by time-varying noise that can mask whale calls and by site-specific ambient sound characteristics to which a trained model may not generalize. In this work, we present NAVE, a Conformer-based detector that improves whale-call detection via three components. First, its input representation combines per-bin complex mean subtraction, which suppresses constant site-specific ambient sound characteristics, with an additional per-channel energy normalization (PCEN) channel that adapts to temporal variations in the noise floor. Second, a frequency-dynamic stem processes these noise-aware representations with convolutions that adapt across the characteristic frequency bands of the target call types. Third, we use a Conformer backbone with a convolution kernel matched to the duration of the target calls that adds an inductive bias and improves temporal localization. We evaluate NAVE on BioDCASE 2026 Task 2 using leave-one-site-out cross-validation across the validation sites, which approximates deployment on unseen recording sites. A single NAVE model with a tuned per-class threshold exceeds the stronger WhaleVAD-BPN baseline, using no boundary-proposal network and no search over post-processing parameters. A controlled ablation study shows that the normalized front-end and a duration-matched convolutional kernel each contribute substantially to recovering the D downsweep, the hardest class for previous detectors, with the wide kernel giving the largest aggregate gain.

AISHELL8-FISHEYE: A Fisheye Audio-Visual Dataset for Target Speaker Extraction with Distortion-Aware Baselines

Peijun Yang, Zhan Jin, Juan Liu 等8人

Fisheye cameras offer wide visual coverage but distort facial and lip geometry, challenging audio-visual target speaker extraction. We introduce AISHELL8-FISHEYE, a 47.03-hour corpus of 94 Mandarin speakers with synchronized audio and paired fisheye and normal-camera videos. Speaker-disjoint splits and synthetic two-speaker mixtures support reproducible evaluation under matched acoustic conditions. We also establish a baseline that combines a lip-reading encoder with a frozen distortion-aware branch transferred from fisheye head pose estimation. On fisheye video, adding this branch to an adapted lip encoder improves scale-invariant signal-to-distortion ratio improvement from 10.47 to 11.84 dB. The branch does not improve performance with a frozen lip encoder, highlighting the importance of visual adaptation. A substantial gap to paired normal-camera inputs remains. The corpus and baseline resources support further research on speech extraction from distorted visual cues.

GTCRN-SS: Lightweight Causal Speech Separation with Residual Allocation and Reconstruction Refinement

Shao-Chun Hu, Zi-Xiang Lin, Hsin-Min Wang 等5人

Low-complexity causal speech separation must preserve speaker information and allocate residual mixture energy without relying on future feature frames. We present GTCRN-SS, a two-stage time-frequency separator that adapts the compact GTCRN enhancement backbone to fixed-output speech separation. The model combines internal residual allocation, reconstruction-conditioned refinement, and frame-aligned recurrent-state transfer. Its complex-mask estimator assigns non-target content to an unreturned residual branch, while the complementary speech estimate conditions a second stage whose final masks remain anchored to the original mixture. A lightweight low-rank adapter transfers causal recurrent evidence between recursive executions without adding another recurrent memory network. The neural graph excludes future feature frames and incurs only the fixed analysis look-ahead of the centered short-time Fourier transform. On noisy Libri2Mix, GTCRN-SS achieves 10.1 dB SDRi and 9.5 dB SI-SNRi with fewer than one million parameters and a low computational footprint. Independently trained ablations confirm the benefits of recurrent-state transfer and reconstruction conditioning, and corpus-level diagnostics show that the internal branch primarily captures residual rather than target-speech content. These results establish GTCRN-SS as an effective quality-efficiency operating point for causal monaural speech separation.

AccentCanvas: Activity-Guided Bidirectional Accent Conversion

Jaehun Kim, Kang Zhang, Sung Woo Kim 等5人

Accent conversion for dubbing must change pronunciation while preserving content, voice, and source timing. Normalization alone cannot produce requested non-native accents, and matching total duration does not ensure local speech/pause alignment. We present AccentCanvas, a shared discrete diffusion model for normalization and L1-to-L2 conversion, trained on cross-paired shared-prompt recordings and synthetic pairs. Frame-level speech activity and repetition-preserving units guide local timing through synthesis. Across both tasks, AccentCanvas achieves the highest accent conversion performance (Accent Arrival) and mean listener-rated accent similarity among compared systems, with over 73% lower speech-boundary error than the best baseline for each task. These gains accompany higher L1-to-L2 WER than TokAN and Vevo-Style, highlighting a tradeoff between requested-accent resemblance and ASR-based content recognition. https://smokedindia.github.io/accentcanvas/

Conflict-Aware Meta-Learning for Generalizable Speech Deepfake Detection

Siqing Qin, Kong Aik Lee, Youzhi TU 等5人

Speech deepfake detectors built on large self-supervised learning front-ends achieve high in-domain accuracy, yet suffer degradation under domain shift caused by unseen synthesizers, transmission codecs, or unfamiliar acoustic conditions. Meta-learning, particularly model-agnostic meta-learning for domain generalization (MLDG), addresses this issue by simulating domain shifts. However, applying MLDG directly introduces a bi-level optimization challenge that remains unsolved. First, effective domain-shift emulation requires genuine distributional gaps between meta-splits, yet such gaps can frequently produce gradient conflicts, destabilizing the bi-level optimization. Second, the stochastic nature of mini-batch gradients causes the reference direction to fluctuate across iterations, resulting in an optimization trajectory that fails to achieve domain generalization. We propose a conflict-aware meta-learning (CAML) framework, which addresses both challenges through three complementary components. Firstly, base domain gradient surgery (base-DGS) introduces an asymmetric projection that treats the meta-train gradient as a primary anchor. Secondly, EMA-DGS replaces the unstable anchor with its exponential moving average (EMA), providing a stable optimization trajectory anchor that substantially reduces domain-shift-induced conflict detections. Thirdly, layer-wise DGS (LW-DGS) further extends the framework to individual parameter tensors, enabling fine-grained, layer-specific intervention that targets conflict-prone layers while leaving well-aligned parameters intact. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed CAML framework achieves lower EER than the compared baselines on most cross-dataset evaluations across multiple cross-dataset benchmarks and architectures, achieving a maximum relative improvement of 45.2% in terms of EER.
