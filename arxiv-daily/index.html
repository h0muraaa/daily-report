KR A1: Design and Preliminary Evaluation of a Humanoid-Robot Interaction Prototype Integrating Personalized Voice Synthesis, Contextual Dialogue, and Emotion-Aware Response Generation

Mahek Aanthu, Om Aanthu, Aaryan AK Aanthu

This paper presents the design and preliminary evaluation of KR A1, a humanoid-robot interaction prototype that integrates personalized voice synthesis, contextual dialogue processing, and emotion-aware response generation for potential mental-healthcare applications. The system architecture combines a locally hosted large language model (Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, served via Ollama) for natural-language understanding and response generation, OpenVoice v2 for zero-shot speaker-adaptive speech synthesis, and a two-tier emotion-processing pipeline comprising a keyword heuristic and LLM-based inference. Face recognition (InsightFace) and vector memory retrieval (FAISS with sentence-transformer embeddings) enable user-personalized interaction. The experimental evaluation reported here is limited to the voice-cloning component: cloned voices were evaluated against reference recordings using eight acoustic similarity measures - mel-frequency cepstral coefficients, pitch, spectral centroid, spectral contrast, spectral rolloff, zero-crossing rate, formants, and speaker-embedding cosine similarity - supplemented by a same-speaker versus cross-speaker baseline comparison. All tests were conducted on a small, informal development sample; no statistical significance testing against a held-out baseline was performed. The emotion-recognition accuracy, end-to-end response latency, and therapeutic or clinical impact of the integrated system have not yet been formally evaluated and are identified as directions for future work. All testing was informal and self-conducted by the development team; no patients or external human participants were involved, and the system has not been evaluated or deployed in a clinical setting.

Improving Large Audio-Language Model Embedding Baselines for Instruction-Aware Speech Retrieval

Chen-An Li, Cheng-Han Chiang, Hung-yi Lee

Speech retrieval finds relevant spoken documents given a spoken query. The spoken query alone may not reveal the retrieval goal, such as finding an answer or matching speaker identity. Instruction-aware speech retrieval expresses this goal through a natural-language text instruction. Large audio-language models (LALMs) effectively process speech and text, and our analysis shows that different retrieval requirements favor hidden states from different audio-encoder layers. We introduce IRIS-Embed, which adapts LALMs into embedding models through contrastive training and instruction-aware audio layer weighting. We construct about 900K training samples from real data and synthetic data, with instructions expressing requirements for one or more speech attributes. On INSPIRE, an instruction-aware speech retrieval benchmark, IRIS-Embed consistently outperforms the corresponding LALM baselines. The best model achieves 62.92% macro-average Recall@50, surpassing the strongest baseline at 45.15%. Controlled comparisons demonstrate instruction awareness and the benefits of audio layer weighting. On MAEB, a massive audio embedding benchmark, macro-average gains of 9.4-19.8 points over the corresponding baselines show generalization beyond speech retrieval.

Full-sphere emission and reception directivities of seven echolocating bat species

Jan Steckel

Echolocating bats shape their calls and the returning echoes with their noseleaf or mouth, head and pinnae. Understanding these spectrospatial directivity patterns is central to bat echolocation. We describe a dataset of simulated far-field emission and reception directivities of seven echolocating bat species from three families (Phyllostomidae, Rhinolophidae and Vespertilionidae). For each species, the head of one preserved specimen was scanned with micro-computed tomography and converted into a closed surface mesh, on which the responses of both ears and of the emitter were computed with a boundary element method or, for the two mouth-emitting species, with an analytical piston model. The complex-valued responses cover the full sphere at 2.5 deg resolution from 20 to 160 kHz in 0.5 kHz steps. They come with the meshes, the landmarks of ears, nostrils and mouth, alternative source positions and skeletons for posing the pinnae and noseleaves, all in one frame, and with Python and MATLAB code. The technical validation covers solver verification, mesh convergence, comparisons with earlier simulations and measurements, and sensitivity analyses.

REAL: Reward Modeling through Evidence-Linked Analysis of Lifelikeness in Generated Speech

Yaodong Song, Jie Lian, Xusheng Yang 等9人

As modern text-to-speech systems become increasingly intelligible and natural, evaluation is shifting from detecting gross synthesis failures to identifying subtle departures from lifelike human delivery. Because large-scale listening tests are costly and slow, speech reward models can serve as scalable automatic judges for comparing generated utterances, curating data, and supporting iterative TTS development. Existing speech reward models can predict scores or pairwise preferences and may generate natural-language rationales, but their supervision is typically defined at the utterance or pair level. The connection between local acoustic phenomena and the final preference therefore remains implicit. We introduce REAL, a framework for Reward modeling through Evidence-linked Analysis of Lifelikeness in generated speech. Its central idea is to supervise not only the final pairwise preference but also a structured account of its supporting observations, linking local audio spans to perceptual phenomena, affected dimensions, and the overall judgment. Specifically, REAL-Jury constructs these evidence-linked reports through timestamp alignment, multi-model analysis, and dispute-oriented deliberation. REAL-Judge learns to generate them from paired audio inputs. REAL-Eval assesses preference prediction in two tiers, from salient perceptual differences to subtle distinctions among strong TTS systems. Instantiated with MiMo-Audio, REAL-Judge improves pairwise preference accuracy over its backbone by 16.01 and 11.41 percentage points on the two tiers, respectively, and outperforms the open-source speech reward models SpeechJudge and UniSRM on both tiers.

Nemotron 3 Diarization: Advancing the Accuracy-Efficiency Frontier in Streaming Speaker Diarization

Ivan Medennikov, Taejin Park, Tatiana Timofeeva 等10人

Streaming Sortformer introduced an arrival-order speaker cache to maintain speaker identities across chunks in end-to-end streaming speaker diarization, with a single model supporting configurable latency profiles spanning offline operation to ultra-low-latency streaming. Nemotron 3 Diarization addresses the original model's four-speaker limitation by extending support to eight speakers while improving both accuracy and computational efficiency. The previous FastConformer-Transformer stack is streamlined into a pure Transformer encoder. A learned upsampling layer then maps the encoder's coarse 80-ms representations to 10-ms resolution, enabling fine-grained predictions. Training leverages large-scale simulated mixtures and adds activity and phantom-speaker losses to the standard Sortformer objectives. Despite doubling speaker capacity over its predecessor, the new architecture reduces training memory by a factor of 2.8, makes training $2.2\times$ faster, and delivers up to $6.4\times$ higher inference throughput. Nemotron 3 Diarization establishes a new state of the art in DER on DIHARD III and CALLHOME. When used to condition DiCoW, it also achieves state-of-the-art tcpWER on the single-microphone conditions of AMI and NOTSOFAR-1. Training code is open source, and model weights are released under the permissive OpenMDW-1.1 license.

SCLAP: Temporal structural alignment improves localization in long-form audio

Yacouba Kaloga, Ina Kodrasi

Audio-text models such as Contrastive Language-Audio Pretraining (CLAP) typically pool frame and token sequences uniformly into global embeddings. This discards temporal structure and impairs both retrieval and localization, especially for short events in long recordings. We introduce SCLAP, a sequence-level contrastive framework that aligns audio frames and text tokens using monotone one-dimensional optimal transport. Its learned marginals and transport plan provide fine-grained temporal supervision from clip-level captions alone. On short-form audio (i.e., Clotho and TACOS datasets with 15-30s recordings), SCLAP remains competitive for retrieval while improving zero-shot localization over CLAP and Text-aware Attention Pooling. On long-form audio (i.e., CASTELLA dataset with up to 300s recordings), SCLAP improves retrieval performance and its localization advantage grows as the event-to-context ratio decreases. At inference, SCLAP requires no optimal transport computation since retrieval can use pooled embeddings and localization can use inexpensive frame-text cosine scores.

Discriminator-free adversarial domain adaptation for robust speech classification

Yacouba Kaloga, Ina Kodrasi

The performance of speech classification systems such as speech emotion recognition or pathological speech detection can degrade substantially when there is a domain shift between training and test data. This shift can arise due to e.g., differences in recording setup, acoustic environment, speaker, language, or speaking style. Adversarial domain adaptation addresses this problem by encouraging representations that are less sensitive to domain-specific differences. However, many adversarial approaches rely on training a separate domain discriminator, which can be unstable and difficult to train. In this paper, we propose to use a discriminator-free adversarial learning network (DALN) for unsupervised domain adaptation in speech classification tasks. DALN reuses the task classifier as an implicit discriminator and aligns domains through the nuclear-norm Wasserstein discrepancy on classifier outputs. We extensively evaluate the performance of DALN on intra-lingual, cross-lingual, and noisy adaptations for speech emotion recognition and pathological speech detection. Extensive experimental results show that DALN generally achieves the best performance across the considered tasks and domain shifts, outperforming discriminator-based domain adaptation approaches.

CallCleaner: Lightweight Bioacoustic Denoising from an Audio Foundation Model

Michael Xie, Ga\v{s}per Begu\v{s}, Morris Alper

Many studies of animal vocalizations require clean recordings, but animals are usually recorded in the field, with background noise from weather, vehicles, and other animals. Animal vocalization denoising is challenged by the scarcity of clean training data and the extreme diversity of animal vocalizations. Classical spectral gates leave residual noise, especially without voice activity detection (VAD), while detector-free neural denoisers designed for bioacoustics remove less noise. We show that a general-purpose source separation foundation model, SAM-Audio, removes more noise than either of these types of methods, when used without training and prompted with time spans from a simple animal VAD. To turn this heavy method into a practical tool, we distill it into CallCleaner, a 19M-parameter denoiser that needs no detector and runs ~90x faster than real time on a laptop CPU. We find that SI-SDR has limited utility in our setting as it penalizes regenerated output and conflates noise removal with call fidelity, a trade-off our separate metrics reveal. CallCleaner leaves 11 dB less noise around the call than Biodenoising, the leading neural bioacoustic denoiser with public weights, and among fast, CPU-capable systems it pushes the Pareto frontier of noise removal vs. call fidelity, without requiring a detector. We release code and weights for CallCleaner, which enables fast, private, local denoising of single-caller recordings without specialized hardware.

Capability-Guided Iterative Online Optimization of Cascaded Spoken-Dialogue Modules

Yuhang Qiu, Bohan Li, Wenbin Huang 等8人

Cascaded spoken dialogue systems comprise perception-and-comprehension, decision-making, and response-generation modules. We study iterative optimization of the first and last modules. Their reciprocal speech-content interfaces enable one-sided data to provide cross-module supervision: acoustic observations, such as waveforms or feature sequences, can be pseudo-labeled to train generation, whereas content-only samples can be rendered into speech to train comprehension. Because the reliable teacher-student direction varies across domains, we propose a two-stage, capability-guided pipeline for iterative online optimization. Stage 1 compares both modules through a shared intelligibility probe and obtains a domain-wise capability prior. Stage 2 activates the compatible one-sided-data route, constructs and filters supervision with the current teacher, updates the selected student, and refreshes the relevant checkpoints across cycles. We instantiate comprehension with Whisper large-v3 and generation with CosyVoice2. After only 200 steps for Whisper and 600 for CosyVoice2 of our iterative online optimization, we observe that, on the Chinese MAGICDATA-RAMC dataset, Whisper reduces CER from 14.18% to 10.77%, while CosyVoice2 reduces it from 4.78% to 4.04%. On the Japanese Common Voice dataset, Whisper reduces CER from 15.23% to 15.10%, while CosyVoice2 reduces it from 14.85% to 11.18%. These results demonstrate the feasibility of capability-guided iterative optimization of complementary spoken-dialogue modules.

Task-adaptive anonymisation enables privacy-preserving sharing of clinical speech data

Ziyun Cui, Haoyu Niu, Wen Wu 等15人

Clinical speech is increasingly used for disease screening and clinical assessment, but directly distributing speech recordings poses privacy risks because they contain both speaker-identifying voiceprints and sensitive spoken content. Existing speech anonymisation approaches are primarily developed for general speech applications or task-specific clinical speech and overlook that different clinical tasks rely on distinct speech characteristics, necessitating task-adaptive anonymisation strategies to protect patient privacy while preserving clinical utility. Here we introduce CASA, a clinically adaptive speech anonymisation agent that transforms anonymisation from a fixed processing procedure into a task-guided optimisation process, dynamically tailoring voiceprint and content anonymisation to task-specific privacy and clinical utility requirements. Through recursive self-improvement (RSI), CASA iteratively evaluates and refines anonymisation strategies against task-specific privacy and clinical utility requirements, while learning from these outcomes to guide more efficient planning in subsequent clinical tasks. CASA is validated across 14 clinical tasks encompassing five languages, diverse elicitation paradigms, and mental health, cognitive and voice disorders. CASA preserved disease-related classification performance while meeting speaker-anonymisation requirements across all clinical tasks, versus only 8/14 for the strongest fixed method. Assessments of resistance to speaker re-identification by external attackers and identification and redaction of sensitive personal information in clinical speech further demonstrated CASA's dual protection of speaker identity and sensitive spoken content. Clinical expert assessments further supported the preservation of clinically relevant information and the acceptability of anonymised speech for controlled research sharing.

Virtual-Flow: Virtual Microphone-based Speech Enhancement via Semi-supervised Flow Matching

Dongheon Lee, Ashutosh Pandey, Sanjeel Parekh 等8人

Neural network-based virtual microphone estimation (neural-VME) synthesizes microphone signals at unobserved spatial locations from a limited set of real microphone (RM) physical recordings. This enables high-resolution arrays that improve spatial processing for downstream tasks such as speech enhancement. However, existing methods rely on supervised training with ground-truth signals captured at virtual locations, requiring datasets that scale with the number of virtual microphones (VM) and leaving open the question of how to design optimal large-array configurations. We propose Virtual-Flow, a flow-matching framework that implicitly generates VM signals conditioned on real recordings, without requiring ground-truth VM signals. We introduce a semi-supervised training strategy based on pseudo-VM targets constructed from time-delayed superpositions of real recordings. Virtual-Flow shows improved magnitude estimation in high-frequency bands, yielding richer spatial cues for beamforming. Experiments demonstrate that the proposed Virtual-Flow, combined with reflow distillation, outperforms existing neural-VME baselines in VM-based beamforming and achieves competitive end-to-end speech enhancement performance with low computational cost.

Pandemonium: Longform Target-Speaker Speech Recognition with Cross-Speaker Interactions

Alexander Polok, Dominik Klement, Ivan Medennikov 等12人

Diarization-conditioned target-speaker ASR (TS-ASR), in particular Diarization-conditioned Whisper (DiCoW), is an accurate and data-efficient approach to multi-talker transcription. It has, however, three structural limitations: the model transcribes each speaker independently, operates on short (30 s) windows, and optimizes its diarization and TS-ASR components separately. We address the first two. We integrate the diarization conditioning into a longform Parakeet backbone (DiCoP) and add cross-speaker attention, which lets the model attend across the speaker dimension so that speakers are no longer decoded in isolation, and an STNO mask refiner that re-estimates the diarization-derived conditioning. As conversational data with genuine multi-speaker interaction is scarce, we scale training with a pseudo-labeling pipeline that mines 5.4k hours of in-the-wild multi-speaker audio from YODAS2. Combining these with synthetic and real data yields state-of-the-art results across several meeting benchmarks. We open-source the resulting models, DiCoP and Pandemonium, and the pseudo-labeled data.

BAmbiDrop: Array-Agnostic Speech Enhancement with Zero-Shot binaural Reproduction using Multi-center Ambisonics Encoding

Michael Tatarjitzky, Vladimir Tourbabin, Boaz Rafaely

Array-agnostic enhancement networks generalize across microphone geometries but produce a single channel, while binaural enhancement methods preserve interaural cues only for one fixed device geometry. We propose BAmbiDrop, a framework that extends array-agnostic enhancement to binaural reproduction without retraining. Exploiting the fact that the Ambisonics origin is an arbitrary point in space, a frozen single-channel AmbiDrop network estimates a complex mask at multiple centers across the array; these masks are fused by a complex mean into a single shared mask, applied to the microphone signals and rendered by fixed BSM filters. Across 21 free-field and rigid-sphere arrays, the fused mask improves enhancement and interaural cue reproduction simultaneously, whereas masking each microphone independently degrades the latter without a consistent gain in the former.

Lightweight Neural Prediction for MDCT-Based Perceptual Audio Coding

Niccolo Abate, Gabriel Soule, Marina Bosi

Perceptual audio codecs often use predictive coding to further reduce signal redundancy. Rather than learning the entire codec pipeline end-to-end, we insert a small neural network as a drop-in, deterministic predictor in an otherwise conventional codec: the network estimates the next Modified Discrete Cosine Transform (MDCT) coefficient block from a short context of reconstructed blocks, and the encoder transmits the quantized residual. We compare Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) and Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) predictors against a non-predictive baseline and a per-line Normalized Least Mean Squares (NLMS) linear predictor (similar to the MPEG-2 AAC Main Profile predictor). The CNN consistently outperforms the MLP, the linear predictor, and the baseline in signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), noise-to-mask ratio (NMR), and MUSHRA evaluation scores. Using a 512-sample analysis window (w = 512), our neural predictors recover, and the CNN exceeds, the coded quality obtained by the non-predictive baseline after quadrupling the analysis-window length to w = 2048, at substantially lower algorithmic delay. The CNN uses only 0.13M parameters (roughly 20x fewer than the MLP) and has a decoder real-time factor (RTF) of ~0.10 on a consumer CPU.

M5-TTS: A Mini-Scale Multilingual and Multi-Dialect Text-to-Speech Language Model with Multi-Token Prediction

Wenhao Li, Jingbin Hu, Shengming Ling 等23人

We present M5-TTS, a compact text-to-speech system supporting multilingual and Chinese multidialect synthesis, zero-shot voice cloning, speaker-specific multilingual generation, and CPU streaming. Its 50M-parameter language model, excluding the speech codec, combines a temporal backbone with a local multi-token prediction module over 16 codebooks. GRPO post-training combines global speaker-similarity and pause feedback with a token-level CER process advantage. Character-level error comparisons provide local feedback, with negative credit propagated backward within a clause and projected onto audio-token positions. M5-TTS achieves 1.37% CER on Seed-ZH and 2.91% WER on Seed-EN, corresponding to relative error reductions of 62.2% and 35.3% over MOSS-TTS-Nano with half the language-model parameters. It also achieves lower content error rates in five of six evaluated MiniMax languages, while difficult Chinese inputs remain a limitation. On four Intel Xeon Gold 6530 cores, it achieves a real-time factor of 0.653 and a warm first-chunk latency of 225ms. A 10-listener evaluation gives M5-TTS higher speaker-similarity and intelligibility ratings than MOSS-TTS-Nano, with a lower naturalness rating. We will release training, post-training, and inference code, together with voice-cloning and speaker-specific checkpoints, to support reproducible research on compact speech generation for on-device voice interaction.
