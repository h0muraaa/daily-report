Score-Based Training for Energy-Based TTS Models

Wanli Sun, Anton Ragni

Noise contrastive estimation (NCE) is a popular method for training energy-based models (EBM) with intractable normalisation terms. The key idea of NCE is to learn by comparing unnormalised log-likelihoods of the reference and noisy samples, thus avoiding explicitly computing normalisation terms. However, NCE critically relies on the quality of noisy samples. Recently, sliced score matching (SSM) has been popularised by closely related diffusion models (DM). Unlike NCE, SSM learns a gradient of log-likelihood, or score, by learning distribution of its projections on randomly chosen directions. However, both NCE and SSM disregard the form of log-likelihood function, which is problematic given that EBMs and DMs make use of first-order optimisation during inference. This paper proposes a new criterion that learns scores more suitable for first-order schemes. Experiments contrasts these approaches for training EBMs.

Energy-Based Models For Speech Synthesis

Wanli Sun, Zehai Tu, Anton Ragni

Recently there has been a lot of interest in non-autoregressive (non-AR) models for speech synthesis, such as FastSpeech 2 and diffusion models. Unlike AR models, these models do not have autoregressive dependencies among outputs which makes inference efficient. This paper expands the range of available non-AR models with another member called energy-based models (EBMs). The paper describes how noise contrastive estimation, which relies on the comparison between positive and negative samples, can be used to train EBMs. It proposes a number of strategies for generating effective negative samples, including using high-performing AR models. It also describes how sampling from EBMs can be performed using Langevin Markov Chain Monte-Carlo (MCMC). The use of Langevin MCMC enables to draw connections between EBMs and currently popular diffusion models. Experiments on LJSpeech dataset show that the proposed approach offers improvements over Tacotron 2.

Quantizer Redundancy and Exact Index Recoding in a Frozen Neural Audio Codec

Youssef Hemimy

Residual scalar quantization can produce highly dependent indices when successive stages reuse identical, unscaled grids. This case study examines that behavior in a frozen neural audio codec and develops an exact recoding procedure that reduces storage while preserving its decoder inputs. Analysis of the repeated-grid recurrence identifies predictable stage sequences; finite-precision checks motivate preserving every original index through explicit exception records. The model weights and reconstruction graph remain fixed. On the studied 62- and 512-file cohorts, a predictor, adaptive coding and explicit file selection reduce complete stored bytes. The final 512-file rate is 129.591 kbps including headers, 25.943% below an original-index XZ control. Original recorded CPU runs establish bitwise latent and waveform preservation; independent readers verify exact index and metadata recovery in subsequent saved-stream experiments. Later coding choices use previously studied cohorts, and fresh encoding requires generating three alternatives. The engineering result is implementation-specific storage reduction with exact index recovery; the underlying lossy reconstruction quality remains unchanged.

vst-bench-ml: Auditable Dataset Generation for Audio Plugins

Youssef Hemimy

Generating datasets with audio plugins requires preserving how each audio observation was produced. vst-bench-ml is a Python package that samples plugin parameters, coordinates rendering through a separately installed host, and records software identities, rendering settings, and output hashes for later verification. A sealed manifest binds this evidence and supports offline integrity checks without loading the plugin. A companion repository provides a sample-free VST3 test plugin, a deterministic offline host, and additional checks linking rendering attempts to recorded outputs. On a pinned macOS arm64 and Python 3.12 stack, functional validation of the pre-release versions reproduced package and host tests. Two runs in fresh processes reproduced the same render-plan hash and all eight expected output hashes, covering four sampled parameter configurations and two fixed MIDI signals; 12 negative cases also passed. Later licensed 0.1.0 release candidates passed producer-run continuous integration and local validation. Version 0.1.1 adds public Git source repositories, package-index installation, and user documentation; a separate release check reproduced the expected plan and output hashes. These checks establish integrity and repeatability within the tested setup; they do not establish portability, scientific suitability, or perceptual quality.

Repairing Speech Access After Text Updates with Frozen Affine Fields

Ming-Hao Hsu, Yuxiang Wang, Yingda Shen 等4人

Speech-capable language models inherit most of their knowledge from text-rich language backbones, and continued model maintenance is likewise dominated by text updates. Knowledge acquired through a text update can nevertheless remain difficult to access when the same query is spoken. We formulate this mismatch as post-update speech access repair, where the knowledge-bearing checkpoint stays frozen and a detachable operator repairs speech-only access in one model pass. We introduce Affine Fields for Interface Repair across Modalities (AFFIRM), which first learns and freezes a base correction for speech hidden states, then adds query-specific corrections with zero mean over the operator-training queries. Paired speech and text states provide the coordinates that condition these corrections on each query. On balanced synthetic-speech PopQA, paired comparisons do not establish a clear advantage for AFFIRM over global SALSA steering or low-rank ReFT interventions under the same frozen single-state constraint. On recorded SLUE-SQA-5 questions with text passages, a full-scale PopQA-fitted AFFIRM operator transfers without refitting and improves ROUGE-L by 0.0941 over its paired No Repair baseline. Low-rank ReFT interventions also transfer positively, whereas the matched SALSA variant falls 0.0707 below its paired baseline. These results show that text acquisition and speech access can diverge after an update and should be verified and repaired separately.

Designing Textual Interfaces To Enhance Llm Reasoning On Audio-Visual Question Answering

Yi Wang, Tianzi Wang, Tong Lei 等5人

Automatic prompt optimization (APO) has shown strong effectiveness for text-based LLMs and LLM systems, but its application to audio-visual understanding systems requires suitable textual interfaces through which multimodal reasoning can be exposed to text-based optimizers. We investigate how the design of textual interfaces affects prompt optimization for multimodal reasoning, on the audio-visual question answering task. We compare three interface designs, including a direct inference system consisting of a single omni-modal large language model (OmniLLM), a captioner-reasoner system, and an agentic architecture in which a primary agent orchestrates subagent OmniLLMs. Experiments with Qwen3-Omni and Gemini show that direct prompt optimization provides model-dependent gains; the captioner-reasoner system and the agentic system expose more interpretable optimization interfaces but are harder to optimize, where error analysis of cases affected by optimization is conducted. Our study highlights textual interface design as an important factor in making prompt optimization effective for omni-modal reasoning systems. Our implementation details are specified in GitHub repo.

Few-Shot Adaptation for Anomalous Sound Detection at Low False-Positive Rate

Simone Lugani, Christof Pichler, Hannes Wegleiter 等6人

Reliable fault detection is crucial for industrial machinery in large-scale facilities, where unexpected failures can cause costly downtime and production interruptions. Although acoustic monitoring provides a scalable solution for machine condition monitoring, its deployment is often constrained by high false-positive rates. We propose a two-step approach to automatic anomalous sound detection: first, when only normal operating audio samples are available, a regularized unsupervised model is deployed; then, after the first machine fault, a few-shot adaptation strategy exploits the anomalous samples acquired during the fault to improve the system. This adaptation increases the area under the precision-recall curve (AUPR) from 23.0 to 64.0 and improves detection in the low false-positive-rate region, with the partial area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (pAUROC) increasing from 39.6 to 72.8. We also show that covariance regularization improves both prediction accuracy and training stability in the unsupervised model. These results provide a practical strategy for acoustic anomaly detection systems, enabling improved accuracy and reduced false alarms.

Learning Visual Speech Representations via Cross-Modal Distillation and Joint Face-Lip Modeling

Jing-Xuan Zhang, Genshun Wan, Jianqing Gao 等6人

Lipreading remains challenging due to the inherent ambiguity of visual speech cues and their sensitivity to head pose and video resolution. In this work, we propose FLARE, a framework for robust visual speech representation learning via cross-modal distillation and joint face-lip modeling. FLARE leverages semantically rich Whisper representations as the acoustic teacher signal, converting them into discrete distillation targets through a random-projection quantizer and pretraining the model with a masked prediction objective. To capture complementary visual information, FLARE employs a dual-stream architecture that jointly processes lip region-of-interest (ROI) and full-face inputs, where the lip stream captures finegrained articulatory motion and the face stream encodes broader facial dynamics. During pretraining, auxiliary audio input is incorporated to facilitate cross-modal alignment, and modality dropout is applied to encourage robust visual-only representations. For downstream lipreading, FLARE serves as a visual encoder coupled with either a lightweight Transformer decoder or an LLM-based decoder. Evaluated on LRS2, LRS3, and the out-of-domain WildVSR benchmark, FLARE achieves strong lipreading performance, with word error rates of 11.7%, 13.2%, and 34.3%, respectively. A progressive design study confirms the contribution of each major component. Further analyses demonstrate that joint face-lip modeling improves robustness under degraded video resolution and large head pose variation, while yielding more phoneme-discriminative visual representations. Code and pretrained models are available at https://github.com/jxzhanggg/FLARE.

Revise-TTS: A Non-Masking Refinement Framework for Masked Diffusion Models in Text-to-Speech

Wanli Sun, Dan Su, Anton Ragni

Text-to-speech (TTS) based on masked diffusion models (MDMs) achieves competitive zero-shot speech synthesis performance. Unlike autoregressive (AR) models, MDMs define a corruption process that maps clean speech-token sequences to masked maps and learn a reverse denoising process to recover the reference tokens. However, MDMs commit to intermediate predictions that cannot be revised in subsequent inference steps. Consequently, prediction errors accumulate and propagate, degrading synthesized speech quality. We present Revise-TTS, a single-stage framework that applies hierarchical binary quantization (HBQ) and generative refinement networks (GRN) to zero-shot speech generation. First, we replace finite scalar quantization (FSQ) with HBQ to introduce a scalable binary representation for speech tokens with an explicit coarse-to-fine structure. Second, GRN is trained to reconstruct the token sequence from a mixture of ground-truth and random tokens. During inference, iterative refinement enables self-correction by randomly discarding a small proportion of previously predicted tokens with new predictions. Experiments and ablation studies demonstrate that Revise-TTS achieves competitive zero-shot speech synthesis performance using HBQ. It can further be extended to any MDM-based TTS task as an alternative training and inference method.

What Does LALM Post-training Learn? Rethinking Audio Understanding and Reasoning Capabilities

Hua-Hsuan Chen, Xuanjun Chen, Wei-Chung Lu 等8人

Post-training often improves the performance of large audio-language models on audio understanding and reasoning benchmarks. However, these improvements may reflect adaptation to specific question formats, such as multiple-choice questions, rather than genuine advances in audio understanding and reasoning capabilities. To investigate what LALMs actually learn from post-training, we conduct controlled experiments that disentangle improvements in audio understanding from adaptation to specific question formats. Across different models and post-training methods, we find that much of the reported improvement persists even without informative audio, but largely disappears when the question format changes. These findings suggest that post-training often teaches models to exploit task-specific textual cues and answer patterns rather than developing generalizable audio understanding and reasoning capabilities.

Point-and-Play: Instrument Timbre Cloning from Music Mixtures

Baotong Tian, Zhiyao Duan

Neural instrument synthesizers can clone the timbre of a solo instrument, letting music producers make music with the sound they desire. However, an instrument of interest is often embedded in a fully produced, multi-instrument recording, beyond the reach of existing methods. Source separation before timbre cloning is an approach, but it may suffer from separation artifacts and is also likely a detour for the task. In this work, we propose Point-and-Play, a system that clones the timbre of a cued source directly from a polyphonic mixture and uses it to synthesize new pitches and velocities. To achieve this, we introduce a Cue-Gated Statistics Pooling (CGSP) module that extracts the timbre code of the cued instrument from adapted CLAP embeddings of the music mixture. This extracted timbre code is then used to condition a separately trained note synthesizer together with pitch and velocity inputs. Experiments show that our proposed system outperforms separate-then-clone baselines in terms of timbre fidelity and synthesis quality, achieving comparable performance with that cloned from a clean solo instrument.

Continued fractions and passive ladder networks for acoustic impedances: the baffled piston and the horn

Ronald M. Aarts

Lumped-element networks give transparent, passive models of acoustic impedances whose low- and high-frequency limits can be read from the circuit, but a systematic, arbitrarily accurate construction for non-rational impedances is lacking. Continued fractions, one- and two-point Pad\'e approximants and their identity with ladder networks and Cauer synthesis are introduced in tutorial form and applied to two examples: the radiation impedance of a baffled circular piston and the throat impedance of a horn with a piston mouth. The even contraction of the piston's continued fraction has positive coefficients, certified by interval arithmetic for the first 100 pairs, and yields a ladder of positive resistances, masses and compliances; with 14 elements the error is 4e-4 up to ka = 5. Beranek's four-element network is, to about 2%, the order-two two-point Pad\'e approximant. A horn modelled as a cascade of waveguide sections is a continued fraction whose tail is the mouth impedance; the infinite exponential horn is a periodic continued fraction, an exact high-pass ladder. The piston networks are passive at every certified order (up to 202 elements) and are extended without changing existing elements; for a finite horn the error of the mouth network passes one to one to the throat impedance.

CochlearMark: Audio Watermarking via Phase Locked Auditory Nerve Representations

Qin Liu, Strahinja Nikolic, Milos Cernak

Modern audio watermarks achieve strong robustness with learned encoder-decoder models, but their behavior under new processing is established empirically. CochlearMark is introduced as a training-free audio watermark that uses auditory-nerve phase-locking as its feature representation. A small waveform perturbation is optimized against a keyed random projection of this representation, and the same projection is used for detection, with no trained encoder or decoder. Because phase-locking represents temporal fine structure, the resulting robustness has a direct physiological interpretation: the mark is retained when this structure is preserved and lost when it is disrupted or rebuilt. Across speech, music, and environmental audio, CochlearMark achieves signal-edit robustness and imperceptibility comparable to trained neural baselines while requiring only a lightweight computational footprint. Among the evaluated training-free methods, CochlearMark is the only one to retain substantial detectability under neural-codec re-encoding.
