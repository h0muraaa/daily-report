Wave Physics as Representation: Tensor-Structured Neural Reconstruction for Ocean Acoustic Fields

Yifan Sun, Panqi Chen, Weifeng Shi 等5人

Reconstructing three-dimensional (3D) complex ocean acoustic fields from sparse measurements is challenging due to highly oscillatory wave structures and restricted sensing patterns/routes. Learning-based approaches fall into two categories. First, purely data-driven models can learn complex field patterns, but do not explicitly exploit wave physics that may enhance interpretability and reduce training costs. Second, physics-informed methods impose a specified physical equation as a reconstruction constraint, but often require environment-dependent parameters that are difficult to acquire. As a result, practical 3D acoustic field reconstruction that is both physically grounded and robust to uncertain environmental information remains largely unexplored. This work takes an alternative route: instead of solving a fully specified wave equation, it extracts a structured representation from wave physics and learns its components from data. From the Helmholtz equation and normal mode propagation, we derive a structured spatial-angular tensor representation, termed the S/c representation. Under a low-rank specialization, it takes the form of a rank-(L,L,1) block-term tensor decomposition, which connects structured sampling patterns/routes used in ocean sensing to sufficient conditions for field recovery. Building on this tensor representation, we propose TIDAL, a neural reconstructor that uses the S/c representation as an inductive bias for structured decoding and aligns information aggregation with the sampling geometry. The neural learner removes the need for known model parameters required by conventional physics-based recovery methods. Experiments across three structured sampling geometries show that TIDAL achieves the best reconstruction performance and fastest inference among baselines, with benefits from the physics-derived representation under limited data and model capacity.

MuLaCover: Flexible Cover Song Generation via Symbolic Tonal Control

Lynn Yi, Zihan Xiong, Junjie Cao 等20人

A cover song re-renders an existing piece, most commonly by preserving its tonal content -- melody and chord progression -- while reshaping other musical attributes. This suggests a natural formulation for generative cover synthesis with pretrained music language models: explicitly control tonal content, while specifying lyrics and style through text. Existing approaches condition on frame-level features, tightly coupling generation to the reference's timing and structure, and limiting flexibility such as tempo variation, structural rearrangement, or partial conditioning. To this end, we present MuLaCover, a cover generation model that conditions a pretrained text-to-song foundation model on an editable symbolic lead sheet. Our design enables alignment-free control: generated outputs preserve the tonal signature of the source without frame-level alignment, allowing flexible timing, structure, and segment-level generation. We further introduce a training curriculum with partially mismatched audio-symbolic pairs, improving diversity and robustness. We evaluate MuLaCover with objective metrics, standard subjective ratings, and in-depth expert interviews that provide fine-grained diagnostic insights. Experiments show that MuLaCover achieves state-of-the-art performance among open-source systems and is competitive with leading commercial models such as Suno-v5.5. Audio samples and code are available at our project page: https://mulalabs.ai/mulacover/

OLIVE: View-Augmented Latent Prediction with Waveform Reconstruction for Speech SSL

Karl El Hajal, Mathew Magimai. -Doss

We propose Online Latent prediction with Invariant Views and rEconstruction (OLIVE), a self-supervised speech representation learning framework that jointly optimizes analysis and synthesis objectives. OLIVE couples masked latent prediction and waveform reconstruction through a functional separation within a single encoder: reconstruction constrains early encoder features to retain signal-level information, while masked latent prediction shapes later contextual representations for robust downstream performance, with waveform view augmentations defining the invariances they learn. To the best of our knowledge, OLIVE is the first speech SSL model that directly reconstructs waveforms, retains its vocoder after pre-training, and remains competitive on discriminative tasks. Adding reconstruction largely preserves the speech recognition performance of masked latent prediction alone, while improving waveform reconstruction and speaker tasks. Adding waveform mixup and gain augmentations provides a controllable way to shift the representations further toward speaker, generation, and reconstruction tasks, while remaining competitive on recognition and semantic tasks.

DUET: Unified Dual-Space Emotion Control for Diffusion and Flow-Matching Driven Text-to-Speech

Xu Zhang, Longbing Cao, Zhangkai Wu

Diffusion and flow-matching based text-to-speech (TTS) models excel in naturalness but often lack explicit emotion control, as emotional signals remain entangled with speaker identity. We discover that emotion embedding emerges as a linearly decodable direction of frozen hidden states, nearly orthogonal to the direction embedding speaker identity. This inspires a plug-and-play framework DUET for emotion control over pretrained diffusion and flow-matching based TTS models. During generation, DUET unifies dual-space control to achieve fine-grained emotion intervention in a single per-step update: hidden space steering shifts generation along the target emotion direction, while mel-space guidance refines spectral details through gradients backpropagated from a differentiable vocoder. We validate DUET on five architecturally diverse pretrained TTS backbones across three datasets, where it outperforms 10 supervised state-of-the-art emotional TTS baselines across paradigms and achieves the highest human-rated emotion appropriateness. To further showcase its qualitative behavior, we deploy DUET on an Ameca humanoid robot, where it produces richly expressive emotional speech on the humanoid, demonstrating the strong potential for plug-and-play affective interaction for embodied agents.

ENACT-Heart -- ENsemble-based Assessment Using CNN and Transformer on Heart Sounds

Jiho Han, Adnan Shaout

ENACT-Heart is a two-branch ensemble for heart-sound classification. A Vision Transformer (ViT) processes spectrogram images, while a convolutional neural network (CNN) processes visualizations of waveforms and spectral centroids derived from the same recordings. The branch predictions are combined by a weighted average with fixed, input-independent weights. On the PASCAL heart-sound dataset, the ensemble achieved an accuracy of 97.52%, compared with 93.88% for the ViT and 95.45% for the CNN. The ensemble achieved a macro-F1 of 0.94 and recall of 0.74 for extra heart sounds. On this dataset, late fusion improved accuracy and macro-F1 relative to both individual branches.

PAID: Phoneme-Aligned Acoustic Units for Cross-Corpus L1-Background Classification

Yangyang Qu, Michele Panariello, Massimiliano Todisco 等4人

First-language (L1) background classification from second-language English speech aims to identify pronunciation-related acoustic cues that remain informative across speakers and corpora. Global pooling is commonly used to aggregate frame-level speech representations, but averaging features across an utterance may obscure local acoustic differences in how individual phonemes are pronounced. To model these pronunciation-related cues before utterance-level aggregation, we introduce Phoneme-Aligned Input Design (PAID), which constructs acoustic units from frozen Whisper representations using transcript-assisted forced alignment. PAID applies a shared nonlinear mapping to each acoustic unit before utterance-level aggregation and incorporates auxiliary phoneme-label embeddings. We construct a six-L1 dataset with speaker-disjoint partitions and evaluate cross-corpus performance on the Speech Accent Archive (SAA). PAID achieves 89.72% Macro-F1 on the source Test set and 80.14% on SAA. Matched comparisons show that phoneme-aligned acoustic-unit modeling improves SAA Macro-F1 by 13.44 percentage points over global pooling. To address uneven classification performance across L1 backgrounds, we additionally evaluate class-sensitive training, which improves observed weakest-class performance.

SAUCE: Summary Analysis Using Conversation Entailment

Man-Ling Sung, Hemanth Kandula, Jeff Ma 等5人

With the growing need for evaluating Large Language Models (LLMs) and their applications to speech, challenges persist in summarizing and evaluating conversations that lack a clear end goal. We introduce SAUCE -- a reference-free, fact-based evaluation pipeline for cross-lingual conversational speech summarization. It measures the accuracy and the fact coverage of a summary through the entailment between conversation and text. We compare SAUCE against several popular summarization metrics and demonstrate the effectiveness of capturing information loss due to transcription and translation error and identifying broken summaries. Crucially, unlike black-box LLM evaluators or dense embedding metrics, SAUCE is inherently explainable: it maps summary scores to discrete, verifiable facts, allowing users to pinpoint exact hallucinations or omissions. We illustrate how this interpretability helps developers systematically profile LLM behaviors and gives end-users an actionable tool to verify summary accuracy in noisy, real-world conditions. Preliminary investigations show SAUCE strongly align with human judgment.

SonoDyno: A Sonic Dynamometer for Engine Speed and Load Estimation from Audio

Robin Doerfler, Beat Rossmy

Controllable engine sound synthesis, Active Sound Design and engine order analysis all build on the relationship between the sound of an engine and its momentary operating state: its speed and its load. Recordings of engine sound are abundant in archives, sample packs and online video, yet they rarely carry time-aligned annotations of that state, which limits their applicability to controllable engine sound generation. We present SonoDyno, which estimates the engine speed, the cylinder count and an engine-normalized load intensity from a microphone signal alone, with no tachometer, no bus access and no prior knowledge of the engine configuration. A convolutional scorer of 124,302 parameters reads the harmonic order energy of each frame at three time-frequency resolutions to jointly score cylinder count and speed, and a decoder imposes the rotational dynamics of the engine on the speed trajectory. A second head estimates the load intensity, the position of the torque between the engine's drag floor at 0 and its full-load ceiling at 1, from the order levels at the decoded speed. On recordings of engines it has never heard, with microphone position, engine type and cylinder count unknown, SonoDyno places 93 to 97% of the frames within 2% of the true speed, identifies the cylinder count with 80% accuracy and confuses the octave on fewer than 2% of the frames. On an unseen engine of a known type, the load intensity has a mean absolute error of 0.12, and the sign of the torque, driving or braking, is right on 91% of the frames. General pitch trackers fall behind even when the harmonic order of each engine is supplied: on our validation recordings and at a tolerance of 5%, the best of them places 62% of the frames within the tolerance, against 96% for SonoDyno.

Human-like cross-language generalisation in deep neural speaker embeddings and its acoustic foundations

Tianze Xu, Xiyang Li, Xiaoming Jiang 等4人

Generalisability is a central question for deep neural networks, including the cross-language generalisation of speaker embeddings. This study examined whether deep neural speaker embeddings generalise across languages and how such generalisation relates to human perception and acoustic structure. Using Cantonese--English bilingual speech presented in within- and cross-language conditions, we derived similarity from 18 state-of-the-art models and compared it with human ratings and acoustic features using linear mixed models and representational similarity analysis. Embeddings exhibited cross-language generalisation that broadly paralleled human behaviour, reflecting accurate identity discrimination and sensitivity to language switching. This generalisation relied on largely shared acoustic cues with language-sensitive weighting, mirroring human patterns. Together, these findings provide converging evidence for human--machine alignment in cross-language voice similarity processing.

The Hearpiece Database of Binaural Room Impulse Responses and Driver Responses on KEMAR

Tong Xiao, Reinhild Roden, Matthias Blau 等4人

This document describes the measurement setup, signal processing, data organization, and MATLAB file format of a database of acoustic responses measured on a Knowles Electronics Manikin for Acoustic Research (KEMAR) using the Hearpiece, a multi-microphone, multi-driver in-ear research device. The data include binaural room impulse responses (BRIRs) and Hearpiece driver responses, together with center-microphone room impulse responses and a loopback impulse response of the playback and recording system. The BRIRs cover two Hearpiece configurations, three room conditions, three independent insertions per configuration, and 24 horizontal source directions. The database supports reproducible research on binaural and hearing-device signal processing, with potential applications in speech enhancement, spatial filtering and source separation, dereverberation, active noise and occlusion control, and acoustic feedback cancellation. The database, MATLAB loading script, and technical documentation are publicly available at https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23667308.

Speaker-Consistent Grounding for Confusion-Resilient LLM-Based Target Speech Extraction

Hanyu Meng, Hao Shi, Zhanhong He 等8人

Deterministic target-speaker extraction (TSE) tends to preserve observed linguistic content but may extract the interfering speaker. In contrast, autoregressive generative TSE improves perceptual quality, but is prone to content hallucination and speaker identity drift, which can be mitigated by model grounding with the evidence from the deterministic TSE. However, grounding such a generative TSE model with speaker-confused evidence may therefore reinforce the error. To address this gap, we proposed a repair-before-grounding framework with a training-free evidence-repair stage. Conditioning-diverse candidate selection (CDCS) selects target speaker-consistent evidence from complementary frozen TSE outputs. The selected waveform can then be used by code-space grounding (CSG) to constrain LLM-based TSE decoding. On Libri2Mix, CDCS reduces wrong-speaker extraction errors from 6.87% to 0.75% on clean mixtures and from 18.95% to 10.46% under noise. With the LLM-based TSE checkpoint unchanged, CSG further reduces noisy content- and acoustic-switch rates from 12.79% and 1.68% to 9.10% and 0.51%, respectively. Despite this stronger grounding, generative TSE retains its predicted perceptual-quality advantage over direct deterministic extraction. These results show that repairing evidence identity before grounding improves the reliability of generative TSE. Audio demos and code are available at https://hanyu-meng.github.io/LLM-TSE-Grounding-Demo/.

Virtual Microphone Array for Direction-of-Arrival Estimation Based on Fiber-Optic Position Sensing

Sebastian Schwarze (Electronic Media Technology Group, Technische Universit\"at Ilmenau, Germany) 等12人

This paper presents a virtual microphone array approach for estimating the direction of arrival of a sound source using a moving microphone and a stationary reference microphone. The method exploits the microphone motion to generate a time-varying array geometry, using fiber-optic position sensing to reconstruct the trajectory and determine the resulting virtual microphone configurations. For each configuration, the estimated time difference of arrival between the two microphone signals and the corresponding microphone geometry define a hyperboloid representing all possible sound source positions consistent with the time difference. By combining multiple hyperboloid constraints, the direction of arrival can be estimated in azimuth and elevation relative to the microphone coordinate system. In contrast to conventional arrays with fixed geometries and approaches based on predefined microphone trajectories, the proposed method derives the virtual array geometry directly from the measured microphone motion, enabling a spatially distributed virtual array to be formed using only two physical microphones and offering potential for size- and weight-constrained applications. The approach was experimentally evaluated under controlled low-reflection conditions for different source positions, source distances, and signal characteristics. The influence of microphone motion was further examined, and the method was tested with a moving sound source. The results demonstrate the feasibility of direction-of-arrival estimation using a virtual microphone array without requiring a predefined microphone trajectory.

CLRM: Cross-Layer Relation Modeling for Audio Deepfake Detection

Yangyang Qu, Siqing Qin, Kong Aik Lee 等4人

Audio deepfake detection (ADD) aims to distinguish bonafide speech from synthetic or manipulated speech. Representations from different layers of self-supervised learning (SSL) speech encoders vary in their ability to distinguish bonafide from spoofed speech. Comparing representations of the same utterance across encoder depths may reveal useful spoofing cues, yet common layer-selection and aggregation methods do not provide these comparisons as detection inputs. We propose Cross-Layer Relation Modeling (\ours{}), which explicitly encodes cross-layer relations alongside layer-specific features, making both directly available for ADD. CLRM reuses the existing detector's SSL encoder and identifies informative layer pairs by combining speaker-relative representation geometry with source-domain detection performance. The resulting relation score is fused with the detector's original score, requiring only the training of compact relation heads and fusion parameters while keeping the existing detector frozen. Across five external datasets, CLRM reduces the mean equal error rate (EER) for all three evaluated detectors, achieving relative reductions of 7.8--16.7%. Controlled comparisons with identical layer summaries and approximately matched auxiliary-head parameter counts also show a lower mean EER for explicit relations than direct concatenation, both before and after score fusion. These findings support explicit cross-layer comparisons as useful detection inputs for SSL-based audio deepfake detectors.
