The Importance of Speech Genre for End-of-Turn Detector Training

Rini Sharon, Kadri Hacioglu, Jeena Prakash 等4人

End-of-turn (EoT) detection has been studied mostly through features, models, and modalities, while the speech genre of the training data has drawn far less attention. We isolate this factor. Using one reproducible annotation pipeline, we build a matched pair of corpora, multi-turn human-human conversational and single-turn human-machine, and train the same streaming ConvLSTM EoT detector on each. Across four test sets the conversational model reaches up to +0.45 higher F1, driven by fewer false alarms on the mid-turn pauses and hesitations that are rare in single-turn corpora. This conversational-training effect is not specific to frame-level decoding. Under a matched complete-or-not test it also appears at the utterance level, across two encoders, but only on conversational test data. Testing only on single-turn speech, as most prior work does, therefore hides the benefit of conversational training data. Against open-source baselines, our 1.8M-parameter model attains the highest F1 on every test set.

Array-Agnostic High-Order Ambisonics Encoding via Diffusion Posterior Sampling

Amit Milstein, Nir Shlezinger, Boaz Rafaely

Ambisonics provides a device-independent representation of three-dimensional sound fields; however, extracting these coefficients from practical microphone array recordings remains strongly dependent on array geometry, acoustic transfer functions, and spatial sampling resolution. This pipeline is typically divided into two distinct tasks: encoding, which entails reconstructing the physically resolvable spatial components, and upscaling, which requires generating higher-order coefficients. Model-based approaches often fail on irregular arrays due to ill-conditioned transfer matrices, whereas conventional neural encoders remain strictly tied to the specific acquisition setup seen during training. Furthermore, existing upscaling methods usually assume ideal low-order inputs, ignoring device-specific array artifacts. We present ADEUPS, a unified framework that couples an array-independent Ambisonic diffusion prior with an explicit physical acquisition model during inference, enabling zero-shot encoding and upscaling across diverse array configurations. We train our prior using an order-masking strategy and perform inference via score blending, which we demonstrate to be crucial for solving the underlying inverse problem. Through extensive numerical evaluations across various array geometries, we show that ADEUPS consistently outperforms task- and array-specific baselines. Finally, we conduct subjective listening tests to confirm the perceptual effectiveness of our approach.

Structured Pruning of Feedback Delay Networks

Facundo Franchino, Karolina Prawda

Feedback delay networks (FDNs) are recursive filter structures used to synthesise artificial reverberation. Their feedback matrix governs both energy exchange between delay lines and the computation required at every sample, making its design central to balancing an FDN's acoustic performance and implementation efficiency. In this work, we develop an FDN-specific structured pruning method that connects these acoustic and computational concerns, allowing high-order dense orthogonal designs to be fitted to efficient implementations. The feedback matrix is replaced by stages of fixed permutations and small orthogonal blocks. We characterise connectivity growth with the network depth and derive exact conditional block updates for matrix fitting, followed by refinement against the reference FDN magnitude response. The resulting factors admit direct vectorised evaluation, with arithmetic cost and coefficient storage scaling linearly with network size for fixed block size and depth. On an Apple M2, the selected staged kernels achieve feedback-multiplication speedups over dense BLAS ranging from approximately threefold to over twentyfold across the tested network sizes, while substantially reducing coefficient storage. Objective metrics and listening tests indicate that the proposed staged approximations closely preserve the spectral and temporal character of their dense references. An analysis of processor utilisation and register reuse explains why execution time depends on more than the operation count. Finally, block size and depth provide practical control over response fidelity, storage, and real-time cost, allowing acoustic design to precede the choice of an efficient implementation.

Streaming Speech Recognition on a Microcontroller: a 65.7M-Parameter Transducer in 2 and 4 Bits on the ESP32-P4

Billal Boumedine

We run Kroko, a 65.7M-parameter streaming Zipformer2 transducer for English, on the ESP32-P4, a microcontroller with two 400 MHz RISC-V cores, a 128-bit SIMD extension (PIE) and 32 MB of PSRAM, but no neural accelerator. Without any training, the encoder's 290 matrices are stored with 2 or 4 bits per weight in Hadamard-rotated groups: the width of each matrix comes from its measured sensitivity, and each matrix is first corrected toward the original model's output, then rounded with GPTQ and a search over the group norms. The released 71 MB of int8 weights become a 26.65 MB file. Activations are int16 between the matrix products and int8 into them. A C engine with hand-written PIE kernels processes each 1.28 s chunk of audio in 0.90 s on both cores, beam search included, and its PC build computes the same bits as the chip, so accuracy is measured on whole test sets. LibriSpeech WER is 4.34% on test-clean and 10.26% on test-other, against 3.94% and 9.17% for the released model; on FLEURS and Common Voice the gap grows to 2.3 and 1.1 points. A GPU simulator shows that the integer arithmetic costs nothing and that the whole gap comes from the weights, and an ablation on all of test-clean shows that GPTQ does most of the work. We also report the port's speed history, from 51.3 s to 0.62 s of encoder time per chunk, each design step measured again in one build, an attempt to reach 16 MB that stopped at 19.63 MB, and what did not work. We did not measure energy. The inference engine, the firmware and the 2/4-bit weights are available at https://github.com/BoumedineBillal/esp32-pie-asr.

Personalising Parametric Audio Effect Recommendation for Vocals from Parameter-only Style Priors

Soumya Sai Vanka, Hayato Yamakawa, Kazunobu Kondo 等5人

Audio effect recommendation has mostly modelled style through reference audio and audio-domain losses. Because the parameter-to-audio map of parametric effects is many-to-one, such systems can match a reference without reflecting how a given engineer works. We instead model an engineer's style as a distribution over effect-chain parameters, which matches the data engineers can realistically share: console recalls store parameters without the audio they were applied to. Under a label-shift assumption, a personalised recommendation follows in closed form from a generic content model, a parameter population model and an engineer prior, with no paired engineer audio and no tuned weights. For vocal chains, the content signal is small but real, and concentrated in high-frequency EQ, dynamics and reverb. A forced-choice test with $14$ listeners found that the style-prior and content-only recommendations were distinguishable (89.3%), with results consistent with prior width affecting the balance between style and content, while parameter-space cosine does not predict perceived difference.

Wav2Aug: A Drop-In Speech Augmentation Policy

Gianfranco Dumoulin Bertucci, Mirco Ravanelli

SpecAugment is the dominant speech augmentation policy, but researchers have studied it primarily for automatic speech recognition (ASR), and it operates on spectrograms, limiting its applicability to other speech tasks and waveform models. We propose Wav2Aug, a drop-in waveform augmentation policy that randomly samples two augmentations from a calibrated pool of waveform transformations. We calibrate the augmentation hyperparameters once and reuse them across tasks and architectures, eliminating task-specific tuning. Experiments on ten speech benchmarks show that Wav2Aug achieves ASR performance comparable to SpecAugment while improving over it on average across non-ASR tasks, yielding an average relative error reduction of 12.8% over the strongest compatible SpecAugment baseline. We further show that multi-view training provides additional gains, with two augmented views offering the best tradeoff between performance and computational cost.

SelectSep: Target-Selective Video Queries for Audio Source Separation

Suyeon Lee, Doyeop Kwak, Joon Son Chung

Video-queried sound separation (VQSS) aims to isolate a target sound from an audio mixture using its corresponding video as a query. However, scene-level video queries can become ambiguous when multiple sounding objects are visible, particularly when the target and interferer are semantically similar. We propose SelectSep, a target-selective VQSS framework that jointly uses text and a single click coordinate to specify the target source and instance without requiring segmentation at inference time. We adapt the visual encoder through target-feature distillation with text and click conditioning, and then fine-tune the separator using the adapted visual features. We also introduce an on-screen interference setting, where both the target and interferer are visible, to evaluate target selectivity. Experiments on standard benchmarks and the on-screen interference setting demonstrate consistent improvements in separation quality and target selectivity, particularly for mixtures containing semantically similar sources.

MiDiff: MIDI-Conditioned Diffusion Models for Drum Audio Enhancement

Elad Aspis, Sharon Gannot

Bridging the semantic gap between perfect symbolic musical structure and severely degraded acoustic signals offers a powerful paradigm for high-fidelity audio restoration. We introduce MiDiff, a diffusion-based generative model for drum audio enhancement that conditions the reverse process on temporally aligned Musical Instrument Digital Interface (MIDI) piano rolls. We extend the Score-Based Generative Models for Speech Enhancement (SGMSE) framework in the complex Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT) domain by integrating a pretrained MIDI encoder, multi-scale temporal alignment, and three complementary conditioning strategies: early injection, Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM), and cross-attention. We further show that providing the same piano roll only through classifier guidance, driven by an Automatic Drum Transcription (ADT) model on the audio-only score, yields no measurable gain: the symbolic prior is effective when it conditions the score network. To relax the requirement for ground-truth MIDI at inference, we further propose a hybrid reverse schedule that transcribes the diffusion state online with the same noise-aware ADT network and hands over from the audio-only score to MiDiff at a tunable switch time, cutting test FAD by 47% relative to the audio-only baseline without any symbolic input (switch time selected on the test set). We train our model on the StemGMD dataset using a non-additive degradation pipeline that simulates room reverberation with echo, electret capsule frequency response, and soft-clipping saturation with noise. MiDiff substantially outperforms the SGMSE baseline in terms of Fr\'echet Audio Distance (FAD) on both the validation and held-out test sets. Ablation studies demonstrate that combining all three conditioning strategies yields superior performance and that authentic MIDI velocity dynamics are critical for effective symbolic conditioning.

DRC-TTS: A Dual-Resolution Codec-Based Architecture for Lexicon-Free Malay Speech Synthesis

Tianhui Su, Tien-Ping Tan

Modern discrete neural audio codecs facilitate speech synthesis by replacing continuous acoustic representations with low-frame-rate discrete token sequences. For Malay speech synthesis, character-level input reduces reliance on pronunciation lexicons. However, combining character-level inputs with low-frame-rate discrete codecs introduces a temporal alignment challenge. When predicted character durations are quantized into frame counts at 12.5 Hz, durations shorter than one codec frame may be assigned zero frames. Consequently, the corresponding character representations may be lost during length regulation, potentially leading to pronunciation omissions. This study proposes DRC-TTS, a dual-resolution codec-based architecture for lexicon-free Malay speech synthesis. The model expands character representations at a temporal resolution of 50 Hz and subsequently downsamples them through strided convolution to 12.5 Hz for discrete codec-token prediction. Objective evaluations show that the proposed model reduces the character error rate (CER) from 27.11% for the baseline to 21.86%, an absolute reduction of 5.25 percentage points. The proposed model also achieves a mean opinion score (MOS) of 4.30, compared with 4.12 for the baseline. These results suggest that the dual-resolution design may improve character-level alignment and perceived speech naturalness under the evaluated conditions.

A Tutorial on Generative Models for Music Audio

Mateusz Modrzejewski, Joanna Kurkowska, Dorien Herremans

Music audio generation spans instrumental clips, full songs with vocals, edits of existing recordings, and systems that play alongside a musician in real time. This monograph explains the methods behind these systems for readers familiar with neural-network training who are new to music generation. It introduces neural audio codecs and autoencoders, autoregressive and masked token models, diffusion and flow matching, sampling acceleration, and preference optimization in a shared notation. Worked examples connect training objectives and sampling procedures to published systems. The application sections relate the musical requirements of instrumental generation, songs, editing, and live performance to model design. Comparison tables record representations, methods, inputs, tasks, and release information documented in papers and official sources. Symbolic composition, speech, and sound effects are included where they connect to music audio. We explain how to interpret automatic metrics and learned judges, design and report listening tests, and assess evidence about data provenance, memorization, attribution, and output detection. Selected legal decisions and disputes illustrate the implications for training and release. The reviewed systems combine established generative paradigms with new representations, planning stages, and post-training procedures. Differences in data, scale, and evaluation limit what cross-system comparisons can establish about the sources of reported gains.

Improving the Dependency on Fine-Tuning Data in Transfer Learning for Speech Emotion Recognition

Ricardo Garc\'ia, Rodrigo Mahu, Carlos Busso 等4人

Compensating for train-test mismatches in acoustic environments is important for deployable speech solutions. This paper proposes to reduce the amount of fine-tuning data needed in transfer learning to mitigate the train-test mismatch by using a compensation mechanism based on the feature maximum likelihood linear regression (fMLLR) algorithm. Our scheme incorporates an autoencoder to reduce the number of trainable parameters. Operating on the encoder latent space, our compensation strategy employs an affine linear transform that accounts for rotations and shifts in the feature space. We evaluate the proposed solution in a speech emotion recognition (SER) task within a realistic human-robot interaction scenario. The results show that the proposed architecture outperforms the original scheme without the compensation strategy. We observe a performance gain in the mean concordance correlation coefficient (CCC) of 16.8% using 12.9% of the training set, and 14.4% using 50% of the training set.

ODAQ-Collection: Expanding the Open Dataset of Audio Quality with a Focus on Spatial Distortions

Chih-Wei Wu, Matteo Torcoli, Mhd Modar Halimeh 等8人

The Open Dataset of Audio Quality (ODAQ) is an expanding resource that addresses the scarcity of openly available data in audio quality assessment research. While previous contributions focused mainly on timbral distortions, with spatial processing limited to basic mid-side and left-right stereo conditions, we present a new contribution featuring high-quality music and speech recordings with diverse stereo images, controlled spatial distortions, and subjective listening test results from trained listeners in a laboratory setting. With 148 pairs of stereo audio samples processed by three different method classes, two applying controlled binaural cue distortions and one applying dialogue enhancement, this addition expands the collection to 688 pairs of processed and unprocessed audio signals spanning 17 processing method classes, along with 18,984 raw subjective ratings from listeners. All associated data and code are released as open resources to facilitate future expansions and research on objective audio quality metrics.

Beyond Utterance-Level Speaker Similarity: Perception-Aligned Segmental Evaluation of Accent in Speech Generation

Jinzuomu Zhong, Alice Ross, Catherine Lai 等4人

Utterance-level similarity ratings do not reveal where perceived accent differences occur in generated speech. We introduce a segmental evaluation protocol combining speaker and accent similarity ratings with subword annotations of perceived accent differences provided by listeners from target accent regions. Using newly collected recordings, 47 retained listeners evaluate 200 content-matched reference--candidate pairs spanning four Scottish and Australian voices, two zero-shot TTS systems, and an articulation-based voice conversion system. Subword annotations locate salient pronunciation differences and sometimes yield different system rankings from similarity ratings. Speaker and accent ratings remain strongly associated ($\rho = 0.71$), highlighting the value of examining local perceptual evidence beyond these scores. Evaluation of seven objective distances reveals weak correspondence with subword selections, with correlations reaching at most $\rho = 0.22$. We release code, data, and an interactive demo to support the development of perceptually grounded segmental accent evaluation.
